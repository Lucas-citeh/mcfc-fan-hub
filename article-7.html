Five from Five: Haaland Makes History as City Enter the Break in Dream Shape

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 29 September 2026

Fifteen points from fifteen. Five wins, zero draws, zero losses, a goal difference of plus-eight. Manchester City are heading into the October international break with their best-ever Premier League start under a new manager — and a piece of football history made in stoppage time at the Etihad.

The Milestone That Defines an Era

Erling Haaland's 81st-minute strike against Sunderland on 20 September — his fifth Premier League goal of the season — was more than just a match-winner in a raucous eight-goal thriller. It was historic. The Norwegian became the first player in Premier League history to score against all 25 clubs he has faced in the competition. Not Shearer. Not Henry. Not Aguero, Fowler or Van Persie. Haaland, at 26 years old and just 111 Premier League appearances into his career, has already done something no one else ever has.

The goal itself — set up by Jérémy Doku's direct run and low cross on Doku's first appearance since the Community Shield — was the kind of instinctive finish that makes Haaland so frightening. City trailed 3-2 with 20 minutes left, Sunderland substitute Brian Brobbey having completed his hat-trick moments before. Haaland's intervention — the 81st-minute prod from close range — made it 5-3 and sealed three points. Composure in chaos. The mark of a champion.

A Team of Goal-Scorers

What makes City's start so convincing is the spread of the goals. Haaland leads with five in the Premier League and seven across all competitions — but he is far from alone. Seven different players have contributed goals in the first five league games, with goals arriving from defenders, midfielders and forwards alike.

Rayan Cherki has three Premier League goals and four in all competitions. His footwork brace at Selhurst Park on 28 August — a 54th-minute dazzle and a 59th-minute right-footed curler — announced him as a genuine Premier League force in just his second City season. Cherki's precise strike against Sunderland in the 29th minute showed another dimension: reading the game at pace, arriving in the right place, and executing with the calm of a player far beyond his years.

Antoine Semenyo has two Premier League goals. Marc Guehi and Josko Gvardiol each scored the late headers that stole three points from Bournemouth in the opening home fixture on 23 August. Enzo Fernández announced himself with his first City Premier League goal — Sunderland, ninth minute — the first of what promises to be many. Even the Carabao Cup has produced revelations: 17-year-old Floyd Samba scoring twice on his senior debut against Norwich, and Allan Elias marking his first City start with a composed finish.

This is a team loaded with attacking intent across the pitch. For opposing managers, there is no obvious way in.

The Return from the Break

City have two defining fixtures lined up when the international break ends. First, a trip to Anfield on Sunday 11 October (16:30 BST) to face Liverpool in Premier League Matchday 7. Then, three days later, Paris Saint-Germain arrive at the Etihad in UEFA Champions League Matchday 2 (20:00 BST, 14 October) — a reunion with the defending European champions.

Phil Foden, who has served the third and final match of his domestic suspension (earned for a red card in the Manchester derby on 13 September), returns available for both fixtures. The Liverpool match and the PSG clash are the type of games Foden was built for — high-stakes, on the biggest stages.

Nico O'Reilly, who picked up a back knock in the warm-up before Coventry on 5 September, is targeting a return for Anfield. Maresca confirmed the injury was not expected to be serious. Doku, who came through the Sunderland game as a substitute after his calf problem, will be aiming for a full return to match fitness during the break.

In Numbers

Five Premier League wins. Zero defeats. Fifteen points from fifteen available. Eight PL goals scored, three conceded. Erling Haaland: history made. Rayan Cherki: three goals and counting. The most expensive teenage signing in PL history — Ayyoub Bouaddi — with four appearances at 18 years old. An unbeaten record in Europe. A Carabao Cup fourth round place secured. A new manager, a new era, a new squad — and the best possible start.

Anfield awaits.
