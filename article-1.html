Romano: Tottenham Preparing Official Bid for Marmoush as City Demand £51m — Barcelona, Juventus and Aston Villa Also in the Mix

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 7 July 2026

Omar Marmoush's future at Manchester City is under serious threat this summer after Fabrizio Romano reported that Tottenham Hotspur are preparing an official bid for the Egyptian forward. Manchester City are understood to be demanding a fee in the region of £51m (€60m) for the 27-year-old — but Spurs are not alone, with Barcelona, Juventus, Aston Villa and Galatasaray all also monitoring the situation.

The Romano Report

Fabrizio Romano — whose sourcing of transfer stories carries significant weight across European football — confirmed serious Tottenham interest in Marmoush, with an official bid in preparation. This represents a step up from the preliminary enquiries previously reported by other outlets: Romano's involvement signals the move is a genuine priority for Spurs rather than a speculative link.

For Manchester City, the reported £51m valuation would represent a significant loss on their initial outlay — Marmoush joined from Eintracht Frankfurt in January 2025 for a fee of €75m. His time at the Etihad has been productive: 16 goals and six assists across 61 appearances, forming a compelling partnership with Erling Haaland in the second half of the 2024/25 campaign. However, City's change in management — with Enzo Maresca replacing Pep Guardiola on a three-year deal — has prompted a comprehensive review of the squad's balance, and Marmoush does not appear to be a guaranteed first-team starter in the new era.

Tottenham's Case

Tottenham are in the market for attacking reinforcements this summer and have identified Marmoush as a priority target. GiveMeSport confirmed Spurs are "set to submit an official bid" following Romano's report, while CaughtOffside noted both Barcelona and Tottenham as the clubs showing the strongest concrete interest as of 5 July.

There is added intrigue in that Tottenham are simultaneously pursuing Savinho from Manchester City — meaning one club could potentially sign two City attackers in the same window. Whether Spurs move for both or concentrate their budget on one remains to be seen, but the intent to reshape their forward line with City's cast-offs is notable.

The reported £51m asking price reflects City's willingness to do business, though it is notably below the €75m paid to Frankfurt just 18 months ago. Marmoush currently earns around £165,000 per week at the Etihad, which gives context to what a prospective buyer would need to match or exceed in personal terms.

Competition from Europe's Elite

Tottenham face genuine competition in this pursuit. Barcelona are reported to be keen, with the Spanish giants looking to address their striker situation following Robert Lewandowski's departure on a free transfer. Marmoush's intelligent movement, pressing intensity and ability to link play fits many of the profiles Europe's top clubs demand from a modern centre-forward.

Juventus are also said to be interested as they look to strengthen their attack. Closer to home, Aston Villa — who compete in the Champions League — have registered interest. Turkish giants Galatasaray are pursuing a loan deal with a purchase option, a structure that would require City's co-operation but could suit both parties if a permanent buyer doesn't materialise quickly.

What Comes Next

Manchester City have not made any public statement about Marmoush's future. The club's official position, in the absence of comment, is that he remains under contract and part of the squad preparing for the 2026/27 season. However, the volume and credibility of reporting — led now by Romano — suggests the situation is being taken seriously by all parties.

If Marmoush were to leave, City would have allowed three significant attackers to depart in one summer window: Savinho (in advanced talks with Tottenham), Marmoush, and the ongoing question mark over Jack Grealish's return from a loan at Everton. Enzo Maresca's preference for a specific attacking profile drawn from his Chelsea period may explain why several of Guardiola's forwards appear surplus to requirements in the new setup.

For Marmoush, this represents a pivotal moment. At 27, he enters his prime years with a World Cup to his name and multiple Champions League clubs competing for his signature. Wherever he ends up, his 16-goal contribution to City's 2025/26 campaign — culminating in the FA Cup — has written his name into recent Etihad history.
