Norway vs Ivory Coast — Haaland Returns to Lead Vikings in World Cup Last 32 at Dallas

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 30 June 2026

Erling Haaland returns to lead Norway against Ivory Coast in the FIFA World Cup 2026 Round of 32 at AT&T Stadium, Arlington, Texas today (30 June, 6pm BST / 1pm ET). The Manchester City striker was rested for Norway's final group game against France — a calculated decision by coach Ståle Solbakken — and arrives in the knockout rounds fully fresh with four goals from two appearances in Group I. This is Norway's biggest fixture in a generation, and their most clinical finisher is primed and ready.

Haaland Rested and Ready

Solbakken's decision to leave Haaland on the bench for the France dead rubber — along with nine other regulars — has paid off exactly as intended. While Norway fell 4-1 to a rampant France side in what was a dead rubber for both teams, Haaland emerged from the fixture with his body and his four-goal tally intact. He scored braces against Iraq (4-1) and Senegal (3-2) to drive Norway to six points and a place in the knockout rounds as Group I runners-up.

Norway's predicted starting lineup: Nyland; Ryerson, Ajer, Heggem, Møller Wolfe; Ødegaard, Berge, Aursnes; Sørloth, Haaland, Nusa. Right-back Julian Ryerson sat out the France match with a thigh problem and Solbakken is monitoring his fitness, though he is reported to be improving. Neither Haaland nor Martin Ødegaard featured against France, ensuring both of Norway's most influential players are at full capacity for this knockout tie.

The Ivory Coast Test

Ivory Coast represent a genuine test of Norway's knockout credentials. Coached by Emerse Fae, the Ivorians advance from their group on the back of organised defending, midfield control, and tactical flexibility — a contrast to Norway's more direct, star-power-driven approach. Their key concerns centre on midfield duo Kessié and Sangaré, with Yan Diomandé a dangerous outlet higher up the pitch.

Right-back Wilfried Singo is doubtful for Ivory Coast having missed their final group fixture, which could open space down their right flank — territory Haaland's movement and late runs are well equipped to exploit. Norway's defensive frailties were laid bare by France, conceding three in 32 minutes, so Solbakken will need a more solid structure at the back than was on show in the dead rubber.

The Pressure of the Knockouts

This is the moment Norway's golden generation has been building toward. For Haaland, who has spent his entire career watching Norway fail to qualify for major tournaments, being at a World Cup and in the knockout rounds is already historic. Breaking Norway's all-time World Cup scoring record in the group stage — done in his first two appearances — was a landmark. But the knockout rounds are where reputations are truly forged.

Ivory Coast will not be overwhelmed by Norway's reputation the way smaller group-stage opponents might be. They are a physical, organised side with genuine quality in key areas. The Norwegian defence, however, will be tested, and Haaland's ability to convert the chances that come Norway's way will be the decisive factor.

What This Means for City's Pre-Season

Every Norway win is also a complication for Enzo Maresca's pre-season planning at Manchester City. The further Haaland and Norway go in this tournament, the later he returns to Manchester. City's schedule: Community Shield against Arsenal on 16 August in Cardiff, Premier League opener against Bournemouth on 23 August. Pre-season training under Maresca is set to begin in mid-July — giving limited runway before competitive action starts.

City have navigated Haaland's international absence before and will do so again. Most supporters are more than happy to manage that inconvenience given what the 25-year-old is delivering on the global stage. If Norway progress tonight, the balancing act deepens — but for now, the Etihad will be watching and cheering from afar.
