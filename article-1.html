Doku Commits to City: Winger Agrees New Deal Until 2031 as Romano Confirms Verbal Agreement

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 21 June 2026

Jeremy Doku is staying. Fabrizio Romano confirmed on 19 June 2026 that Manchester City and the Belgian winger have reached a verbal agreement on a new long-term contract until 2031. The 24-year-old, currently on World Cup duty with Belgium, declined advances from numerous interested clubs to make the Etihad his clear priority. An official announcement is expected once administrative and legal formalities are completed.

Romano: Verbal Agreement in Place, Announcement Imminent

The news came directly from Fabrizio Romano on 19 June 2026, with the transfer journalist confirming that Manchester City had secured a verbal agreement with Doku on an extension that runs to June 2031. The deal includes a "drastically increased" salary — the scale of the uplift a reflection of how highly the club value the winger heading into the new Enzo Maresca era, per DH Sports journalist Romain Van der Pluym.

Only administrative and legal details remain to be finalised before the contract is signed and a formal announcement made. Given the verbal agreement is in place and the player's intent is clear, the paperwork is the final hurdle rather than any substantive obstacle.

Doku Said No to Everyone Else

The most telling detail in Romano's report is the context around the agreement: Doku declined offers from "numerous clubs" who had approached him this summer. With City in transition following Pep Guardiola's departure and question marks over several senior players, there was genuine uncertainty about the club's direction — yet the Belgian's answer to every approach was the same. The Etihad was his priority.

That level of loyalty and commitment is not something City can take for granted in the current market, and the club acted swiftly to reward it. At 24 — with his most explosive years likely still ahead of him — Doku is now contractually part of the Maresca project for the medium-term future.

Why Doku Is Worth the Deal

At his best, Jeremy Doku is one of the most unplayable wingers in European football. Signed from Rennes in the summer of 2023 for around £55m, he has developed into a constant attacking threat — direct, quick, and a nightmare for defenders in one-on-one situations. His 2025/26 campaign included periods of exceptional form on the left flank, and under Maresca's pressing system — which demands explosive, ball-carrying wide players — he is arguably a more natural fit than he was in some of Guardiola's possession-heavy iterations.

The World Cup window was always the most logical moment for rivals to test City's resolve. The club's response — moving quickly to a verbal agreement before the tournament group stages had even concluded — signals exactly how central Doku is to future planning.

What It Means for the Squad Picture

Doku's renewal joins a growing list of key contract developments at the Etihad this summer. Phil Foden signed a new four-year deal to 2030 in May 2026. Josko Gvardiol committed until June 2031 on 15 June, ending Real Madrid speculation. With Doku now locked in until 2031, three of City's most potent attacking and defensive assets are secured for the Maresca era.

The picture in wide areas is now clearer: Doku on the left, Antoine Semenyo (whose January 2026 arrival from Bournemouth yielded 17 Premier League goals and the FA Cup Final winner) operating across the front, and Jeremy Doku, Rayan Cherki, and Savinho — if the Brazilian remains — competing for starting positions. How Maresca rotates and deploys that group will be one of the defining tactical questions of 2026/27.

What Comes Next

The official announcement of Doku's extension is expected in the coming days once documentation is signed. Until then, the verbal agreement per Romano carries the authoritative weight that underpins all his transfer reporting. For City fans watching the summer with a mixture of anticipation and anxiety, the Doku news is a clear statement of stability: one of the club's most dynamic players is not going anywhere.
