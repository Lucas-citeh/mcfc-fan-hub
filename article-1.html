Maresca Breaks Silence: 'I'm Planning for Rodri Next Season' as Surgery Date Set and Real Madrid Links Cool

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 25 July 2026

Enzo Maresca addressed the press for the first time as Manchester City head coach on 24 July 2026, confirming Rodri's back surgery is scheduled for Monday 28 July and making clear he is building his plans for 2026/27 with the World Cup winner as part of them. In a significant development, both Real Madrid and Manchester City have denied any formal approach — Madrid reportedly calling City directly to rebuff claims of a pre-transfer agreement.

Surgery Monday, Return Planned for Later in Pre-Season

Rodri's back complaint, which had been managed throughout Spain's World Cup campaign, will finally be addressed under the knife on Monday 28 July. Maresca confirmed the surgery date at his press conference and explained that the procedure had originally been scheduled for before the World Cup — but Rodri chose to delay it to ensure he was available for Spain. Having won the tournament and lifted the Golden Ball, the City midfielder now faces a period of recovery before returning to Carrington.

The timeline for his return to training is uncertain — no specific date has been set — but Maresca spoke with quiet confidence about his midfielder's eventual return. He is expected to miss City's entire Asia Tour (Inter Milan on 1 August in Hong Kong; K League All-Stars on 5 August and Atlético Madrid on 9 August, both in Seoul) and the Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff on 16 August is considered highly unlikely. The Manchester derby on 12 September has been mentioned in previous reports as a more realistic comeback target, though no official timeline has been confirmed by the club.

Maresca: 'I'm Planning for Rodri Next Season'

On the transfer speculation that has swirled since the World Cup Final, Maresca was direct and relaxed. Pressed on the reported Real Madrid interest, the Italian coach said he is "planning for next season" with Rodri and expects the midfielder to join City's training group once he has recovered from surgery and taken a holiday period. He characterised the transfer noise as "normal around big players" — something he said City were not worried about.

The framing matters. Maresca did not hedge, qualify, or deflect in a way that would typically signal that a sale is being considered. He spoke about Rodri as a player in his squad plans — not one whose situation was under review. For a head coach entering his first press conference in a new role, it was a notably firm statement of intent.

Both Clubs Deny Any Formal Approach

Earlier this week, multiple outlets — including CityXtra and Read Man City — had reported that Real Madrid were growing in confidence over a summer deal, with Rodri said to have opened the door to a move to Spain. That reporting generated significant coverage. What followed was equally significant.

Real Madrid contacted Manchester City directly to deny having made any approach for the midfielder, according to Goal.com US and Fox Sports (July 24). The Spanish club were reportedly furious over claims that a pre-transfer agreement had been reached, insisting that no formal contact had taken place and pushing back on suggestions that Rodri's style was a fit for their current setup under José Mourinho. Manchester City, in turn, confirmed that no approach from Real Madrid had been received — a second denial, from the selling club, on the same day.

As of 25 July 2026, the situation is as follows: no official bid has been made, no transfer request has been submitted, and the head coach of Manchester City has publicly stated he expects Rodri to return and be part of his squad. The earlier wave of speculation — which was broadly characterised as "Real Madrid growing in confidence" — has been met with denials from both parties involved.

What This Means for City

The double denial does not guarantee Rodri remains a City player. Transfer stories at this level rarely resolve cleanly before the window closes, and Rodri's contract situation — he enters the final year of his deal in 2026/27 — means the economics will remain a live issue throughout the summer. A player who does not sign a new contract with one year remaining is a player whose value decreases with each passing month.

But the direction of travel, at this precise moment, is clear. The head coach wants to keep him. The club has denied receiving any approach. And Rodri is about to undergo surgery before a recovery period that puts him back at Carrington in due course. City supporters should treat the current situation with appropriate caution — transfer windows can change quickly — but the tone from inside the club could not be more different from the speculation that had built outside it.

Asia Tour and the New Era Begin

With Rodri absent, Maresca faces his first real test of squad depth at the pre-season tour. Elliot Anderson — the club-record £116m signing from Nottingham Forest — is expected to be a central figure in the tour. The England midfielder is fully recovered from his World Cup semi-final campaign and was described by Maresca as ready to integrate into the group at Carrington. Nico Gonzalez and Mateo Kovacic provide experienced cover in central midfield, while summer academy signing Jeremy Monga is also expected to be involved.

Maresca hinted at further transfer activity, saying there is "always something to do" in the window — without specifying names. The pursuit of Lille's Ayyoub Bouaddi is understood to be ongoing, with David Ornstein of The Athletic reporting progress in negotiations at a fee in the region of £85m. No official announcement has been made.
