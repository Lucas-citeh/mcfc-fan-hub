Savinho Nears Tottenham Exit: Romano Confirms Formal Talks as City Eye £55m Sale

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 18 June 2026

Savinho's time at Manchester City may be coming to an end. Fabrizio Romano has confirmed that the club and Tottenham Hotspur re-opened formal transfer negotiations in June 2026, with the Brazilian winger now deemed surplus to requirements under incoming head coach Enzo Maresca. City have placed a €50-60m price tag on the 22-year-old, and Romano has confirmed negotiations are progressing well.

From Blocked Deal to Open Sale

Less than a year ago, Manchester City made their position on Savinho unambiguous. Romano reported in May 2026 that the club "have no intention to let him go, not even for a fee over €70m," with City viewing the Brazilian as a key player for the season ahead under Pep Guardiola. Tottenham's initial approach was dismissed.

What changed everything was Guardiola's departure after his contract expired at the end of 2025/26, and the appointment of Enzo Maresca as head coach on a three-year deal. As Maresca and sporting director Hugo Viana began shaping the squad for a new era, Savinho found himself reclassified. Romano subsequently confirmed that City re-opened formal talks with Tottenham, with the winger described as surplus to requirements and negotiations now "progressing well."

Romano: Savinho 'Attracted by Spurs'

The transfer journalist, reporting in June 2026, confirmed that Savinho is "attracted by Spurs" and that a second significant Tottenham signing announcement could come "soon" — language that places the deal in its most advanced phase yet. The shift from City's hard-line block in May to confirmed formal talks within weeks underlines how quickly the Etihad's transfer calculus has shifted under new leadership.

Romano's role in this story has been central. He confirmed the re-opening of talks, noted City's readiness to sell for the right price, and has provided consistent updates on the deal's momentum. Without a "here we go" as of 18 June, the move is not yet officially complete — but the direction of travel is clear.

The Numbers: A Profitable Sale

Manchester City are understood to want a fee in the region of €50-60m — approximately £43-52m at current exchange rates, though some reports have placed the figure closer to £60m. City paid Girona approximately £30m for the winger in 2024, meaning this represents a genuine return on investment for a player who has made 27 appearances and contributed 3 goals and 6 assists in 2025/26.

The wide-attack picture at City for 2026/27 helps explain the willingness to sell. Antoine Semenyo — 17 Premier League goals in the second half of 2025/26 and scorer of the FA Cup Final winner — has established himself as a starter. Jeremy Doku adds another genuine option on the left. With Maresca's system also capable of deploying midfielders in wider roles, the congestion in wide areas made Savinho the most logical departure candidate among the attackers.

Tottenham's Rebuilding Project

For Tottenham, the acquisition of Savinho would represent a significant statement of intent under Roberto De Zerbi. The Italian head coach, known for his high-energy, positional pressing style from his time at Brighton, views wide players who can carry the ball at pace as central to his system. Savinho — at 22, with Premier League experience and a contract that has already been extended to 2031 — fits the profile precisely.

Spurs have been active early in the summer window and Romano has indicated this deal has been the focus of intensive work between the two clubs. The re-opening of formal talks after City's initial block marks a significant shift in the negotiating position.

What It Means for City's Summer

If the Savinho sale completes, the funds would give Viana additional firepower in the summer window. Manchester City's primary midfield target, Elliot Anderson of Nottingham Forest, remains their biggest ongoing pursuit — with a third bid expected imminently after Forest rejected a £106m + £15m offer in June. A Savinho sale at around £55m would meaningfully contribute to funding the Anderson deal and any other business Maresca identifies.

More broadly, the willingness to sell Savinho signals that Maresca is being given genuine authority over squad composition. This is not a cautious, marginal rebuild — it is a structural reshaping of City's attacking options, clearing space for players who fit the new head coach's vision while realising value on those who do not.
