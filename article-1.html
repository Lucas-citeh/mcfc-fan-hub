Haaland Draws Blank as France Thrash Norway 4-1 — City Striker Still Marches Into World Cup Last 32

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 27 June 2026

Erling Haaland's stunning run of scoring at the 2026 World Cup was halted on Friday evening, as France delivered a decisive 4-1 victory over Norway at Gillette Stadium in Boston. An Ousmane Dembélé hat-trick completed inside 32 minutes sealed the result in remarkable fashion, and though Haaland was kept quiet throughout, it barely matters: Norway had already secured their place in the Round of 32 before a ball was kicked, having won their first two group games. The Manchester City striker ends the group stage with four goals in three appearances and heads into the knockout rounds.

Dembélé's Hat-Trick Puts France in Rampant Mood

The dead-rubber context cut both ways on the night. Norway's first-choice defensive organisation, so solid in the wins over Iraq and Senegal, looked exposed against a French side with something to prove. Ousmane Dembélé was the architect of Norway's downfall, netting at the 7th, 20th, and 32nd minutes to complete what is reported to be the second-fastest hat-trick in World Cup history. France's intensity was such that the first half was effectively over as a contest before the break.

Thelo Aasgaard pulled one back for Norway but the damage had been done. Strand Larsen then missed a penalty in the second half — a moment that summed up Norway's evening — before Désiré Doué added a fourth for France deep in injury time to put the final gloss on a thoroughly one-sided scoreline.

Haaland Kept Quiet — But Norway Are Through

For Manchester City supporters watching from afar, the Haaland-specific concern after this match is minimal. The big Norwegian was kept at arm's length by France's defence on a night when service was limited, and he drew his first blank of the tournament. But four goals in three group games — including braces against Iraq and Senegal — is a return that places him among the leading scorers heading into the knockout stages.

Norway's final group standing — six points from three games — is enough to advance comfortably, and their reward is a Round of 32 fixture. The route that lies ahead is considerably steeper than what came in the group phase: at the World Cup, knockout football is a different animal, and Norway's defensive vulnerabilities, exposed so emphatically by France, will need to be addressed by head coach Stale Solbakken before that next game.

Norway's Group Stage in Review

The broader story of Norway's group stage campaign is one of genuine encouragement. Wins over Iraq (4-1) and Senegal (3-2) — both delivered in large part thanks to Haaland's goals — were enough to ensure qualification before the France fixture arrived. For a nation that spent years watching their talismanic striker lead a team that couldn't reach major tournaments, simply being at a World Cup and advancing from the group stage represents a landmark moment.

Haaland's four goals have already broken Norway's all-time World Cup scoring record, set just six days into the tournament. Whether he can add to that tally in the knockout rounds depends entirely on Norway's ability to improve their collective performances away from simply relying on his individual brilliance.

What This Means for City's Pre-Season Plans

Every Norway win extends Haaland's World Cup stay — and every extra week in the tournament pushes back his return to Manchester for Enzo Maresca's pre-season. With City's 2026/27 season beginning on 16 August (Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff) and the Premier League opener against Bournemouth on 23 August, there is a finite window before Haaland needs to be available.

If Norway progress deep into the knockout rounds, City could face a scenario where their striker returns to pre-season training with limited preparation time. It is a situation the club will manage — and one most supporters are happy to navigate, given what Haaland has been delivering on the international stage. For now, the World Cup rolls on, and so does Norway.
