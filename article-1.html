City Fall to Atlético as Barcelona Agree €50m Rodri Fee

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 9 August 2026

Sunday 9 August brought the end of Manchester City's pre-season Asia Tour — a 2-1 defeat to Atlético Madrid at Seoul World Cup Stadium — and the summer's biggest off-pitch development: Barcelona and Manchester City have reached a club-to-club fee agreement worth €50 million for the transfer of Rodri, with the Ballon d'Or winner expected to travel to Catalonia on 12 August.

Seoul Finale: Atlético Edge City 2-1

On the pitch in Seoul, Enzo Maresca's side wrapped up the Coupang Play Series with their first defeat of pre-season. Memphis Depay's powerful strike from the edge of the penalty area in the 66th minute opened the scoring for Diego Simeone's Atlético Madrid — a finish that underlined exactly why the Dutchman remains one of European football's most dangerous attackers, even at 32.

Yannick Carrasco made it two in the 74th minute with a low finish that beat the goalkeeper at his near post. City offered little in the way of a serious response until the 85th minute, when Ruben Dias arrived from a corner to head home and pull one back — but Atlético saw the game out comfortably.

The defeat — City's first of the tour after the penalty-shootout loss to Inter Milan in Hong Kong and the dominant 3-1 victory over K League All-Stars — will concern Maresca less for the scoreline than for the shape of the performance. Atlético under Simeone have long been one of Europe's most ruthless counter-attacking sides, and their structured defence and quick transitions exposed City on several occasions.

Dias' late header was at least a positive. The Portuguese centre-back — who signed a new four-year deal to June 2030 during the summer — retains his aerial threat at set pieces and will be a critical figure in the Maresca era. City fly home from Seoul ahead of the FA Community Shield against Arsenal on 16 August at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff.

Rodri to Barcelona: Fee Agreed at €50m

The bigger story was playing out off the pitch. Barcelona and Manchester City have now reached a club-to-club agreement worth €50 million for the transfer of Rodri, according to Yahoo Sports and Goal.com (citing Radio Catalunya 1). Transfer paperwork is expected to be completed imminently, with the Spanish international due in Catalonia on 12 August to finalise his four-year deal.

The agreed fee of €50 million represents a compromise from City's initial €80 million valuation — but one that reflects the reality of the situation. Rodri was entering the final year of his contract, and losing him for nothing in summer 2027 was never an option the club would have chosen. Barcelona's economic terms — a reported salary of approximately €30 million per year gross — were agreed in the days after Rodri gave the Catalan club permission to negotiate with City on 7 August.

The deal draws a line under one of the most dramatic transfer sagas of the summer. Rodri had appeared to be heading to Real Madrid for the bulk of July, with personal terms agreed and a first formal offer submitted by Madrid on 6 August. But the fee gap proved unbridgeable — Real Madrid refused to go above €55–60 million while City held firm — and Rodri switched his preferred destination, opening the door for Barcelona to move. The rest followed quickly once the two clubs engaged properly.

What Rodri's Departure Means for Maresca's City

Rodri is the defining figure of City's recent dominance. Over six Premier League title campaigns, his positioning, control and reading of the game turned the area in front of City's defence into an impenetrable zone. No midfielder in the division has matched his pass accuracy, ball recovery rate or defensive press efficiency over the same period. That is not easily replaced.

Elliot Anderson, the £116 million club-record signing from Nottingham Forest, is a different kind of midfielder — a progressive, box-to-box runner whose numbers in carry-and-press metrics are outstanding, but who does not offer Rodri's deep-lying control. Nico Gonzalez provides the holding option but has fewer than 30 Premier League appearances to his name. Mateo Kovacic remains a dependable, if less distinctive, option.

The pursuit of Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille — a potential long-term Rodri successor — continues, with multiple outlets reporting full agreements on personal terms and fee structure. A loan-back to Lille for 2026/27 may delay the Moroccan midfielder's Etihad impact by a year, but his profile makes him the obvious answer to the question Rodri's departure raises.

The Community Shield against Arsenal on 16 August now takes on added significance as the first serious competitive examination of Maresca's City without their talisman. Haaland, Elliot Anderson, Marc Guehi and Nico O'Reilly should all be available after returning from post-World Cup breaks. On this pre-season evidence, the building blocks are there. The challenge is assembling them without the man who made everything tick.
