Romano's 'Here We Go': Rodri Exits Manchester City for Barcelona in €76.5M Deal

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 17 August 2026

It is done. Fabrizio Romano posted his signature confirmation on the evening of 16 August 2026 — Community Shield day — ending a transfer saga that had consumed the entire Manchester City summer: 'RODRI TO BARCELONA, HERE WE GO! Deal agreed for Spanish midfielder to join Barça from Man City, all done for the former Ballon d'Or. Green light from Man City to last Barcelona bid.' The fee is €76.5 million. Rodri signs a four-year deal to June 2030. Seven years, four Premier League titles, the Champions League, the Ballon d'Or, and a World Cup Golden Ball — the greatest midfielder of his era is leaving the Etihad Stadium.

The 'Here We Go' That Ended the Summer

Romano's confirmation arrived on Community Shield evening, hours after City's 3-0 defeat to Arsenal in Cardiff. The timing — a summer of increasingly urgent negotiations condensed into one afternoon — was both apt and brutal. Sources confirmed to ESPN on Sunday that a deal had been brokered between the clubs, with the two sides exchanging paperwork to finalise the agreement. The Associated Press, citing a source, also confirmed the deal via reporting published by the Washington Post.

The fee structure, per ESPN: a guaranteed base of approximately €60 million, with add-ons that take the total package to €76.5 million. That figure represents a significant step up from the €50–60 million reported through most of July and into August, with Barcelona ultimately meeting City's demands after their third offer over the weekend cleared the threshold. Rodri, 30, signs a four-year contract that runs to June 2030 at Camp Nou — a deal reported to be worth approximately €30 million per year gross.

Seven Years, Ten Trophies, One Era

Rodri joined Manchester City from Atlético Madrid in June 2019 for £62.8 million. What followed was the most decorated midfield career in the club's history: four Premier League titles, the 2023 Champions League, two FA Cups, two EFL Cups, and the Club World Cup. In 2024 he became City's first Ballon d'Or winner. In 2026 he captained Spain to World Cup glory — playing the full 120 minutes of the final against Argentina and winning the tournament Golden Ball.

His injury absence in 2024/25 — a knee ligament problem suffered in October 2024 — illustrated precisely how central he had become to the way Manchester City played under Pep Guardiola. The team that finished fifth in the league that season was, in large part, a team without its conductor. His return in the second half of the campaign helped City end the season as FA Cup and EFL Cup winners, though Guardiola's final match — a 1-2 home defeat to Aston Villa on 24 May — arrived at the end of a season that had confirmed Arsenal's dominance.

The Negotiation Timeline

Barcelona's pursuit of Rodri began in earnest after the 2026 World Cup. Real Madrid were also in the picture — Rodri had given Los Blancos a green light before switching preference to Barcelona in early August, per multiple sources — but the defining issue throughout was the fee. City refused to sell below €80 million for weeks. Barcelona's first formal bid was rejected as far too low. Their second, around €60 million, was also turned down. Their third offer, submitted over the weekend of 15–16 August, cleared the bar.

Complicating the timeline was Rodri's back surgery, undertaken on 28 July in Madrid, and his post-procedure rehabilitation at the Etihad Campus. Enzo Maresca — speaking at his pre-Community Shield press conference on 14 August — had confirmed Rodri was present at the CFA, adding "I gave him a big hug. He looks good." That quote briefly raised questions about whether the transfer would happen at all; a separate Goal.com report suggested Rodri would stay if City's fee demands could not be met. Those questions were answered on Community Shield evening.

What It Means for Enzo Maresca

Manchester City's new head coach inherits a midfield that has now lost both of its senior controllers in the same window. Tijjani Reijnders — signed for £46.5 million from AC Milan in June 2025 — departed for Al Qadsiah in the Saudi Pro League for €61 million, per Fabrizio Romano's 13 August confirmation. That exit opened the Bouaddi path; Rodri's confirmation opens the imperative.

Elliot Anderson, City's £116 million summer signing from Nottingham Forest, is the headline new arrival — a box-to-box midfielder whose progressive carries and pressing intensity were among the best in the Premier League in 2025/26. Mateo Kovacic and Nico Gonzalez provide experience. Rayan Cherki, Phil Foden and the versatile Rico Lewis offer additional options from higher positions. But none of them are Rodri, and none of them do what he does.

That is precisely why City's pursuit of Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille has been so persistent. The 18-year-old Moroccan international has had personal terms agreed with City for weeks — per multiple sources, a contract to 2031 — and Lille manager Davide Ancelotti publicly acknowledged on 17 August that Bouaddi could leave for the Etihad. City and Lille are reported to be in the final stages of a club-to-club agreement. No official announcement has been received from either club as of this writing.

What Rodri Said

No departing statement had been issued by Rodri or by Manchester City as of 17 August 2026. The official mancity.com announcement of his departure is expected imminently. Rodri is understood to be travelling to Barcelona in the coming days; some reports suggest he could be unveiled before Wednesday's Joan Gamper Trophy match against Al Ahly at Spotify Camp Nou. City are expected to publish a farewell tribute on their official channels once the formalities are complete.

When the tribute does arrive, it will be among the most significant in the club's history. Rodri — alongside Haaland, de Bruyne, Silva, Agüero and Kompany — belongs in the very highest tier of the Etihad era. His name will not need much explanation to anyone who watched City play football between 2019 and 2026. That, more than any number, is the measure of what they are losing.
