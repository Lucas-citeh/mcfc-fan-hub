Haaland on Fire: City Striker Bags Second Brace as Norway Storm Into World Cup Last 32

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 23 June 2026

Erling Haaland is putting on a one-man show at the 2026 FIFA World Cup — and Manchester City fans are watching on with understandable pride. The Norwegian striker scored twice as Norway defeated Senegal 3-2 on 22 June at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, securing their place in the World Cup last 32 with a game to spare. Haaland now has four goals in two group games, broke Norway's all-time World Cup scoring record, and has emerged as one of the tournament's standout performers.

Four Goals, Two Games, One Record Broken

Haaland had already opened his 2026 World Cup account with two goals in Norway's opening 4-1 win over Iraq. Against Senegal, he added two more in a pulsating second-half display. His first came in the 47th minute — a composed left-footed finish into the upper right corner after a swift counter-attack. His second followed shortly after, a volley struck with his weaker right foot past the Senegalese goalkeeper.

The goals were significant beyond the scoreline. With his efforts against Senegal, Haaland became Norway's outright all-time top scorer in World Cup history — yet another record for a player who seems to collect them with the same regularity he scores goals. His form also places him among the tournament's leading scorers, level with France's Kylian Mbappé in the race for the Golden Boot.

That scoring record — extended across 12 consecutive competitive matches for Norway — underlines just how transformed the Norwegian national team has been since Haaland reached international maturity. For years, Norway failed to qualify for major tournaments despite producing one of football's most prolific strikers. The 2026 World Cup is his stage, and he is making the most of every moment on it.

Norway 3-2 Senegal: How the Group Stands

Norway's 3-2 win in New Jersey was not straightforward. Senegal, a technically accomplished side with genuine quality throughout their squad, pushed them hard across 90 minutes. The five-goal thriller demonstrated both Norway's attacking threat — built almost entirely around Haaland — and the vulnerability of a side that can be exposed defensively when pressed.

The result leaves Norway in a strong position in Group I, through to the last 32 before their final group game. Stale Solbakken's side have now beaten both Iraq and Senegal, collecting six points from six. The final group match against France on 26 June at Gillette Stadium in Boston is now, in effect, a dead rubber for qualification purposes — but a live contest for group supremacy and seeding heading into the knockout rounds.

What Comes Next: France at Gillette Stadium

Norway's final Group I match pits them against France — one of the pre-tournament favourites — in Boston on 26 June. Both sides are already through, so the stakes are nominally lower. But there is nothing nominal about a Haaland-led Norway facing a French side that boasts elite talent across every position. For Haaland, it is another opportunity to perform on the biggest stage.

City fans will be watching with a secondary interest: keeping their striker fit and free from injury for the return to pre-season. Haaland's planned return to Manchester for Enzo Maresca's pre-season programme depends on how deep into the tournament Norway progress. The City faithful will want him to go far — but also to come home in one piece.

What It Means for City's 2026/27 Season

Manchester City's 2026/27 season begins on 23 August with a Premier League home fixture against Bournemouth, following the Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff on 16 August. Haaland's World Cup form — four goals in two games, tournament record-breaking, playing with the confidence of a player at the peak of his powers — is the best possible advertisement for what Enzo Maresca will have available once the squad reconvenes.

With the Elliot Anderson transfer in its final stages and the club working to finalise the squad around Maresca's vision, Haaland's performances at the World Cup serve as a reminder that City's foundation remains the most reliable goal-scorer in world football. As Norway march deeper into the tournament, Manchester City fans have every reason to enjoy the show.
