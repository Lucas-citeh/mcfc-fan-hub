France Thrash Haaland-Less Norway 4-1 — Solbakken Rests City Star for Dead Rubber, Norway Face Ivory Coast in Last 32

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 27 June 2026

Norway head coach Ståle Solbakken made ten changes for Friday evening's dead rubber against France, resting Erling Haaland alongside nine other regular starters as France delivered a dominant 4-1 victory at Gillette Stadium in Boston. An Ousmane Dembélé hat-trick completed inside 32 minutes settled the result comprehensively, though Norway's passage was already secure: they had clinched their place in the Round of 32 before a ball was kicked, having won their first two group games. Haaland, who did not enter the field, ends the group stage with four goals from two appearances and heads into the knockout rounds rested and firing. Norway face Ivory Coast on Tuesday 30 June at AT&T Stadium, Arlington, Texas.

Dembélé's Hat-Trick Puts France in Rampant Mood

The dead-rubber context cut both ways on the night. Norway's first-choice defensive organisation, so solid in the wins over Iraq and Senegal, looked exposed against a French side with something to prove. Ousmane Dembélé was the architect of Norway's downfall, netting at the 7th, 20th, and 32nd minutes to complete what is reported to be the second-fastest hat-trick in World Cup history. France's intensity was such that the first half was effectively over as a contest before the break.

Thelo Aasgaard pulled one back for Norway but the damage had been done. Strand Larsen then missed a penalty in the second half — a moment that summed up Norway's evening — before Désiré Doué added a fourth for France deep in injury time to put the final gloss on a thoroughly one-sided scoreline.

Haaland Rested — Solbakken's Calculated Call

For Manchester City supporters watching from afar, the Haaland news from this game is entirely positive. Solbakken made the pragmatic call to rest his talisman in a match that carried no qualification stakes for Norway — rotating all ten outfield starters and allowing Haaland to remain on the bench for the full 90 minutes. His four-goal tally from two appearances (braces against Iraq and Senegal) remains intact, and he arrives at Tuesday's Round of 32 tie against Ivory Coast fully rested and fresh.

Norway's final group standing — six points from three games — is enough to advance comfortably as Group I runners-up. The route that lies ahead is considerably steeper than what came in the group phase: at the World Cup, knockout football is a different animal, and Norway's defensive vulnerabilities, exposed so emphatically by the French in this game, will need to be addressed by Solbakken before that next match. But going into it with Haaland rested is exactly the position the coach was aiming for.

Norway's Group Stage in Review

The broader story of Norway's group stage campaign is one of genuine encouragement. Wins over Iraq (4-1) and Senegal (3-2) — both delivered in large part thanks to Haaland's goals — were enough to ensure qualification before the France fixture arrived. For a nation that spent years watching their talismanic striker lead a team that couldn't reach major tournaments, simply being at a World Cup and advancing from the group stage represents a landmark moment.

Haaland's four goals have already broken Norway's all-time World Cup scoring record, set just six days into the tournament. Having played two of Norway's three group games and sat out the France dead rubber, he enters the Round of 32 with his body fresh and his record intact. Whether he can add to that tally depends on Norway's ability to improve collectively beyond simply relying on his individual brilliance — Tuesday's tie against Ivory Coast at AT&T Stadium, Arlington will be the first real test of that balance in the knockout stage.

What This Means for City's Pre-Season Plans

Every Norway win extends Haaland's World Cup stay — and every extra week in the tournament pushes back his return to Manchester for Enzo Maresca's pre-season. With City's 2026/27 season beginning on 16 August (Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff) and the Premier League opener against Bournemouth on 23 August, there is a finite window before Haaland needs to be available.

Norway's next test is Ivory Coast at AT&T Stadium, Arlington on Tuesday 30 June. If Haaland and Norway progress deep into the knockout rounds from there, City could face a scenario where their striker returns to pre-season training with limited preparation time — a genuine concern given Maresca's first pre-season begins in earnest in mid-July with the Community Shield on 16 August. It is a situation the club will manage — and one most supporters are happy to navigate, given what Haaland has been delivering on the international stage. For now, the World Cup rolls on, and so does Norway.
