Forest Reject City's £120m Second Bid as Gvardiol Halts Renewal Talks — Double Transfer Blow

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 12 June 2026

Manchester City's summer transfer plans absorbed a significant twin setback this week. Nottingham Forest rejected a second, substantially improved bid for Elliot Anderson — worth £106 million upfront with a further £15 million in performance add-ons — and separately, Josko Gvardiol pressed pause on his contract renewal talks following Jose Mourinho's confirmed appointment as Real Madrid head coach.

Anderson: The Second Rejection

Manchester City's second bid for Nottingham Forest midfielder Elliot Anderson has been turned down, Sky Sports Transfer Centre and CaughtOffside confirmed on 10 June. The improved offer — structured as a £106 million initial fee plus £15 million in performance-related add-ons — would have set a new British transfer record, eclipsing Arsenal's £105 million for Declan Rice in 2023, had Forest accepted it on those terms.

They did not. Forest's objection is not to the headline figure — it is to the structure. The club are demanding a guaranteed flat fee above £120 million: a fixed sum with no dependence on add-ons that may or may not be triggered. From Forest's perspective this is not a pedantic distinction. They have already sold players at clubs' quoted fees only to see add-ons go uncollected; with Anderson's value rising following an outstanding 2025/26 campaign, they have no appetite to repeat the experience.

Why Forest Will Not Blink

Nottingham Forest have every reason to hold firm. Anderson was one of the Premier League's standout midfield talents in 2025/26 — a dynamic, vertically-minded number eight who ranked highly across ball progression, shooting from range, and goal contributions. The club have now qualified for European football and face no financial pressure to sell below their stated terms. Their leverage is genuine, not merely tactical.

City, by contrast, need this deal. The departure of Bernardo Silva on a free transfer has created a gap in midfield — one that Reijnders, Kovacic, Gonzalez, and O'Reilly do not individually fill in the way Enzo Maresca requires. At 23, Anderson is precisely the profile the new head coach has asked for: high-energy, press-resistant, capable of shooting from range and turning defence into attack in two touches.

What City Do Next

City are expected to return to the negotiating table with a third bid, restructured to meet Forest's flat-fee demand. Whether the club are prepared to commit to a guaranteed sum in excess of £120 million on a fixed basis — above the Rice British record — is the live question. Reports indicate that the gap between the two clubs is not unbridgeable, and that Anderson remains City's top transfer priority.

Anderson himself continues to be understood as determined to complete the move. Personal terms have been agreed in principle — a five-year deal, per journalist Nicolo Schira — and the 23-year-old wants the situation resolved. England manager Thomas Tuchel has confirmed that medical examinations can take place during the World Cup, meaning any delay need not extend into August. Manchester United have stepped away from any competing interest.

Gvardiol: The Mourinho Factor Arrives

While the Anderson impasse was developing, a separate piece of significant news landed on Thursday. Sky Sports DE's Florian Plettenberg, corroborated by Diario AS, reported that Josko Gvardiol has halted his contract renewal talks with Manchester City. The Croatian defender — who had been cited as weighing a straight choice between City and Real Madrid — has now paused negotiations on City's side of the equation.

The catalyst is Jose Mourinho. Real Madrid confirmed the Portuguese coach's appointment as first-team head coach for three seasons until June 2029 — a development that Sky Sports directly linked to a stepped-up pursuit of Gvardiol. Mourinho has identified the Croatian as a priority defensive signing, and the arrival of one of football's most decorated managers at the Bernabeu represents a qualitatively different level of appeal than abstract interest from the club.

City's Response: An Upgraded Offer to 2031

Manchester City have not stood still. The club revised their contract proposal upward: the offer now runs to 2031 — an extension on the initial 2030 proposal — with improved salary terms, per CityXtra and Yahoo Sports. Sporting director Hugo Viana had identified Gvardiol's renewal as one of his most urgent priorities on taking the role, and City officials retain internal belief that the defender will ultimately choose to stay at the Etihad.

The footballing case for staying is real. Under Maresca — whose systems at Leicester and Chelsea were built on progressive, ball-dominant defensive structures — a left-sided centre-back who can carry the ball and contribute to build-up is not merely useful but essential. Gvardiol fits that template almost perfectly, and his role in the new era is expected to be more central and consistent than it sometimes was during rotation-heavy periods under Guardiola.

The World Cup Complicates Everything

Gvardiol is in Croatia's squad for the 2026 World Cup, which opened this week. Any formal announcement of his decision — in either direction — is unlikely until Croatia's campaign concludes. Both Real Madrid and Manchester City have navigated summer transfer sagas around major international tournaments before; patience from both clubs is expected, with the practical resolution likely to come in late June or July.

City's contractual position remains strong regardless: Gvardiol's deal runs until 2028, meaning there is no fire-sale scenario, no risk of a free transfer in twelve months. Viana can negotiate from a position of stability. But stability is not the same as leverage — and with Mourinho's arrival at the Bernabeu changing the optics of a move to Real Madrid, the situation demands resolution rather than extended negotiation.

The Bigger Picture

Taken together, this week's news is a reminder that City's rebuild under Maresca is unfolding in a competitive and complicated market. The Anderson pursuit, even now, remains the most likely to succeed — City are the primary suitor, the player is committed to the move, and the gap is one of financial structure rather than fundamental disagreement. A third bid that meets Forest's flat-fee demand resolves it.

The Gvardiol situation is more genuinely uncertain. When a player pauses renewal talks with his club following the appointment of a specific manager at a rival, it signals something more than tactical posturing. City's response — an upgraded offer and publicly expressed confidence in retention — is the right one. Whether it is enough will depend on what Gvardiol decides when he returns from the World Cup.
