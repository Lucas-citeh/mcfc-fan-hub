One of Our Own Until 2030: Foden Signs New Four-Year Deal at the Etihad

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 28 May 2026

Phil Foden has committed his long-term future to Manchester City, agreeing a new four-year contract that will keep the Stockport-born forward at the Etihad until the summer of 2030, with an option to extend for a further year. The deal, confirmed by Sky Sports and ESPN, ended a period of uncertainty around the England international and signals that he will be a central figure in Enzo Maresca's new era.

Foden was entering the final year of his previous contract, and reports had suggested there was a club outside England tracking his situation. Fabrizio Romano later confirmed that a non-English side had been interested in the forward before Foden made clear his intention to stay at his boyhood club. Manchester City's sporting director Hugo Viana is understood to have made securing Foden a non-negotiable early priority for the incoming Maresca regime.

A Difficult Second Half to the Season

The timing of the renewal is notable. Foden's form in 2025/26 was at times inconsistent — he fell out of Guardiola's starting plans in the second half of the campaign, partly because of the explosion of form from new signings like Tijjani Reijnders and the January arrival of Antoine Semenyo. He contributed 7 Premier League goals and 10 across all competitions, with 9 assists, and was used increasingly as an impact substitute after the winter break.

None of that, according to sources close to the deal, dimmed City's desire to keep him. The club's belief in Foden as a generational talent was not shaken by the rotation. Guardiola publicly praised him throughout the season, and Maresca is understood to view Foden as one of the pillars around which his system will be built at the Etihad.

What Maresca Inherits

For the incoming head coach, the Foden renewal represents a significant early win. Walking into a new job with the creative heart of the team tied down until 2030 is a very different proposition from managing an unsettled star in the final year of his deal. Maresca managed a similarly gifted creative profile at Chelsea, and the patterns he used in west London — high-tempo, positionally fluid, with midfielders and forwards overlapping freely — suggest Foden's ability to play between the lines and in a false-nine role will suit the new system.

The 25-year-old brings experience — multiple Premier League titles, a Champions League, an FA Cup and EFL Cup double — alongside the understanding that at City, the expectation is always to compete for everything. He heads into 2026/27 with something to prove after a season where the spotlight fell elsewhere, and that, if anything, tends to bring the best out of him.

One of Their Own

Foden came through City's academy, made his debut at 17, and has now spent the entirety of his professional career at the club. The new deal ensures that if he sees it through, he will have served Manchester City for well over a decade in his prime years. In a transfer market where loyalty is increasingly rare, it is the kind of commitment that resonates — not just with supporters on the terraces, but across the whole fabric of the football club.

Blue Moon on his back. And he's not going anywhere.

Sources: Sky Sports (contract confirmed, May 2026); ESPN (agreement reported, May 2026); Fabrizio Romano (pre-deal interest from abroad confirmed, May 2026).
