Window Open: Romano Confirms City Preparing Third Anderson Bid — 'Confident to Get the Deal Done'

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 15 June 2026

The Premier League's summer transfer window officially opened today — 15 June 2026 — and Manchester City's dominant storyline continues to be Elliot Anderson. Fabrizio Romano confirmed late on 14 June that the club are "preparing a new official bid to be sent soon to Nottingham Forest" and are "confident to get the deal done" after leading the race since March. It is a sharply different tone from the mixed signals of earlier in the week.

Romano: 'New Official Bid Being Prepared'

The Italian transfer journalist confirmed on 14 June: "Manchester City are preparing a new official bid to be sent soon to Nottingham Forest for Elliot Anderson. City confident to get the deal done after leading the race for months, since March. Man United still keen but City ahead and optimistic." The Hard Tackle simultaneously reported that City were "confident of sealing" the deal, citing sources close to the negotiations.

The update marks a decisive shift in tone. Reports from FourFourTwo, Sports Mole, and Yahoo Sports Canada in the days immediately following the second bid rejection (10 June) had raised the possibility that sporting director Hugo Viana was prepared to walk away from the deal if Forest refused to budge on the fee structure. That narrative has now been replaced by something considerably more positive: City are in, staying in, and preparing to table an offer Forest can accept.

Why the Second Bid Failed — and What Comes Next

Manchester City's second offer — structured as £106 million guaranteed upfront plus £15 million in performance-related add-ons — was turned down by Forest on 10 June. The total package of approximately £121 million would have set a new British transfer record, eclipsing Arsenal's £105 million purchase of Declan Rice in 2023. But Forest owner Evangelos Marinakis has been unequivocal: he will not accept a deal structured around add-ons. The club want every penny of the agreed fee guaranteed from day one, with no dependence on appearances, achievements, or conditional clauses that may or may not be triggered.

From Forest's perspective, the position is entirely logical. Anderson was one of the Premier League's most dynamic midfielders in 2025/26, and with European football now secured at the City Ground, the club face no financial pressure to sell on terms that leave money at risk. Their position is not posturing — it reflects the genuine leverage of a club that does not need to sell.

City's third bid, when it arrives, is expected to be structured differently: a higher guaranteed fee — at or above the £120 million threshold Marinakis has named — with minimal or no performance-related contingencies. Whether City are prepared to commit to that level on a fully unconditional basis will determine whether Anderson arrives at the Etihad this summer.

Anderson's Position: Committed

Throughout the twists of the last fortnight, the 23-year-old's own stance has not changed. Journalist Nicolo Schira reported that Anderson has agreed personal terms in principle on a five-year deal at City, and England manager Thomas Tuchel confirmed that World Cup medical examinations can take place during the tournament — meaning the transfer need not wait until Anderson's competitive summer is over. Manchester United have monitored the situation but, per multiple sources, are not prepared to enter a bidding war, and City remain firmly preferred by the player.

Romano's characterisation of City as "leading the race for months, since March" is significant because it pre-dates even the first rejected bid. The groundwork between clubs has been extensive; the bid rejections have been about structure rather than fundamental opposition, and the player himself has been ready to move for some time.

The Wider Window: City's Priorities

The transfer window's opening on 15 June sets the clock running for what promises to be a defining summer of Etihad reconstruction. Enzo Maresca arrives as head coach with Bernardo Silva and John Stones both having departed as free agents — and with Savinho committed to a new deal until 2031 (confirmed via Fabrizio Romano and Manchester City's official channels on 14 June), the outgoing picture is clearer than many feared at the start of June.

Anderson remains the central priority for midfield. At 23, his combination of energy, ball progression, press-resistance, and shooting from range makes him the obvious fit for the dynamic box-to-box role Maresca's system demands. He is precisely the profile the new head coach asked for: an engine capable of turning defence into attack in two touches and producing double-figure goal contributions in a full season.

With a new official bid being prepared and City expressing confidence in closing the deal, the working assumption as the window begins is that Anderson will eventually be a Manchester City player. The question now is price — and whether Viana and Marinakis can find the number that finally makes it happen.
