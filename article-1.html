Anderson Chooses City — But United Are Back: Romano on the Summer's Biggest Midfield Battle

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 31 May 2026

Fabrizio Romano has confirmed that Elliot Anderson has reached agreement in principle on personal terms with Manchester City — a five-year deal, with the Nottingham Forest midfielder making clear his preference is the Etihad Stadium. But the transfer is far from done: Manchester United have re-entered the race, INEOS are "calling and calling", and Forest are standing firm at an asking price of around £125 million.

Romano revealed the state of play in a series of updates stretching from March through to late May 2026. At the time of writing, Anderson is the priority midfield target for incoming Manchester City manager Enzo Maresca, and has been since City's new sporting director Hugo Viana identified the England international as the engine room signing that defines the new era.

Personal Terms Done — But Club Fee a Long Way Apart

The agreement on personal terms is a significant development — it means Anderson himself has committed to the idea of moving to City, and the five-year contract offered is understood to be at the top end of the wage bracket for a non-superstar signing at the Etihad. Romano described City's contract offer as "really important", and the player's response was equally clear: his first choice is Manchester City.

The complication is the fee. Nottingham Forest value Anderson at around £125 million. Manchester City have not yet matched that figure, and a gap of substantial size remains between the clubs. Forest owner Evangelos Marinakis is not known for discounting his prized assets, and Anderson — 22, England international, first-choice midfielder — fits the profile of a player they will not sell cheaply.

United Come Back In

The dynamic shifted further this week when Romano confirmed that Manchester United have re-entered the race. Having initially dropped out of the running — with City appearing to have a clear run at Anderson's signature — INEOS have resumed contact with Forest. Romano's characterisation was blunt: United are "calling and calling".

Whether United's renewed interest is a genuine competitive bid or a negotiating tactic to drive up the price for City is unclear. What it does mean is that Manchester City can no longer simply wait Forest out — the leverage of a rival with resources and motivation has re-entered the equation. City will need to bridge the fee gap to get this deal over the line.

The World Cup Complicates Timing

Anderson is expected to be a key figure in England's squad for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, which begins this summer in the United States, Canada and Mexico. Thomas Tuchel's side face the group stage from mid-June, and with the tournament running into July, the realistic window for Anderson to finalise and complete a move to City could stretch into August.

That timeline matters. Enzo Maresca and his staff want the squad assembled as early as possible in pre-season, and a prolonged World Cup run for England would leave the deal in limbo through much of July. City have options to proceed with pre-season work without Anderson finalised, but the preference — as with all Viana's early business — is to move fast.

Why Anderson Suits Maresca

Anderson is a natural fit for the system Maresca established at Chelsea and is likely to carry over to City. A physically imposing yet technically assured central midfielder, he can operate as a press trigger or as a ball-carrier in tight spaces — exactly the profile Maresca favoured in the role occupied at Chelsea by Moises Caicedo. At 22, he is also a player who can improve further under sustained coaching, which aligns with City's long-term planning horizon.

His time at Forest has seen him develop from a high-energy wide option to a legitimate central midfield force. He reached double figures in goal involvements in the Premier League in 2025/26 and was one of the standout players in the division before England's World Cup preparations began. Maresca's aides have reportedly watched him closely across the season, confirming in the staff's own reporting that his technical profile is exactly what they want.

What Comes Next

The picture, as of 31 May 2026: Anderson wants City, City want Anderson, personal terms are agreed. The obstacle is entirely financial — Forest's price tag versus City's current offer. Manchester United's re-entry adds urgency. The summer transfer window officially opens for Premier League clubs on 15 June, and Romano suggests City will look to accelerate discussions once the window is live.

For City supporters, this is the transfer that defines the Maresca project in its earliest days. Land Anderson, and you have the midfield fulcrum everything else can be built around. Lose him to United, and you go into the new season with a significant gap still to fill.
