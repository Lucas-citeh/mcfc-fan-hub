Norway 2-1 Ivory Coast — Haaland's 86th-Minute Winner Sends Vikings into World Cup Round of 16

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 1 July 2026

Erling Haaland scored his fifth World Cup goal in the 86th minute to seal a dramatic 2-1 victory over Ivory Coast in the World Cup Round of 32 at AT&T Stadium, Arlington, Texas on 30 June. It is Norway's first-ever World Cup knockout win. Haaland and the Vikings now face Brazil at MetLife Stadium on Sunday 5 July (4pm ET / 9pm BST).

Nusa Fires Norway Ahead (39')

Norway made the better start and were rewarded in the 39th minute when winger Antonio Nusa stepped onto his right foot and arrowed a curling finish into the top corner — giving goalkeeper Yahia Fofana absolutely no chance. It was the reward for an organised, press-resistant first-half performance from Solbakken's side, with Sander Berge and Martin Ødegaard controlling the midfield and limiting Ivory Coast's ability to build through the lines.

Haaland was well managed in the first period, dropping deep to link play but rarely finding the space inside the area that he craves. Norway nonetheless carried a deserved 1-0 lead into the break, with the City striker primed and waiting for his moment.

Amad Diallo Levels (74')

Ivory Coast head coach Emerse Fae turned to his bench early in the second half and the decision yielded almost immediate dividends. Amad Diallo — introduced as a substitute — produced a moment of genuine class in the 74th minute, controlling inside the area, beating his marker, and thumping a shot into the turf that bounced up and beyond Ørjan Nyland. The equaliser shook Norway, who suddenly looked uncertain for the first time all evening.

The prospect of extra time loomed over the AT&T Stadium as Ivory Coast grew in confidence and Norway retreated slightly. It was the kind of passage of play that would test the mental fortitude of any team — but Norway have Erling Haaland.

Haaland Writes History (86')

The defining moment came four minutes from time. Oscar Bobb slid the ball into the path of Patrick Berg inside the Ivory Coast penalty area; Berg's cut-back was perfectly weighted, and Haaland arrived at the far post to tap home into an empty net. His fifth goal of the tournament. Norway's most celebrated World Cup moment. A first knockout win in the nation's football history.

The celebration was raw and euphoric — Haaland sprinting to the Norway fans, arms outstretched, roaring with a release of emotion that matched the occasion. For a player who spent his formative years watching Norway miss every major tournament, this was a moment long in the making.

What This Means for Manchester City

From Enzo Maresca's perspective, each additional Norway match extends Haaland's absence from pre-season preparations at the Etihad. The Community Shield against Arsenal is on 16 August in Cardiff; the Premier League opener against Bournemouth follows on 23 August. Pre-season training under Maresca is due to begin in mid-July. If Norway beat Brazil on Sunday and progress further still, the window for Haaland to integrate into the new manager's system before competitive action shrinks considerably.

City will manage — they always do with Haaland. But from a Manchester City standpoint, Tuesday's result means the countdown to his return to Manchester just got pushed back by at least another week. Most supporters would take that trade-off without hesitation.

Brazil Next — Sunday 5 July, MetLife Stadium

The draw has handed Norway the ultimate last-16 challenge: five-time world champions Brazil. The Seleção edged their Round of 32 tie against Japan and head into Sunday's match at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey as firm favourites. Kickoff is at 4pm ET / 9pm BST.

Norway's historic win over Ivory Coast showed they have the resilience and the individual brilliance to trouble anyone. Whether that is enough against Brazil — a team of an entirely different calibre — will be answered on Sunday. The entire Etihad will be watching.
