Anderson Eyes World Cup Final as England Face Argentina in Atlanta — Semi-Final Preview

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 13 July 2026

Manchester City's club-record £116m summer signing Elliot Anderson is two wins away from lifting the FIFA World Cup trophy after England beat Norway 2-1 after extra time in the quarter-final in Miami. England now face Argentina in the semi-final on Wednesday 15 July in Atlanta — a match billed as one of the greatest clashes the tournament has produced in decades. For City fans, the storyline is bittersweet: Haaland is heading home, and Anderson is still standing.

Contrasting Fortunes for City's Stars

The quarter-final weekend delivered a split result for Manchester City supporters. Erling Haaland — who went into the match with seven World Cup goals from five appearances, including a brace against Brazil in the last 16 — was kept goalless as Norway were eliminated. Jude Bellingham's equaliser on the stroke of half-time and a winner three minutes into extra time ended Norway's tournament and Haaland's hopes of becoming the first Norwegian to lift the World Cup trophy.

On the other side of the bracket, Elliot Anderson continues. The 23-year-old central midfielder, who became the most expensive British player in history when City agreed a £116m deal with Nottingham Forest earlier this summer, is an integral part of Thomas Tuchel's England squad. He will now be asked to help England navigate the hardest possible test remaining in the tournament — Argentina, with Lionel Messi potentially playing his final World Cup match.

How England Got Here

England have been tested throughout this World Cup but have found ways to advance. Against Norway in the quarter-final at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami, they fell behind to an early goal before Bellingham restored parity in first-half stoppage time. The second half remained tight, and in extra time it was Bellingham again — pouncing on a fumbled shot by Norway's goalkeeper to bundle home from close range. The 2-1 victory sealed England's passage to only their fourth World Cup semi-final.

Bellingham has been England's standout player of the tournament, scoring decisive goals in the knockout rounds that have drawn comparisons to some of the competition's all-time great performances. But England have been more than a one-man show, with the collective defensive structure and press under Tuchel proving difficult to break down for opponents.

Argentina: The Toughest Test

Argentina secured their semi-final spot by overcoming Switzerland 3-1 after extra time on 12 July, setting up a meeting with England that will be played out in Atlanta on Wednesday evening. Lionel Messi has been central to Argentina's campaign and, at 38, is widely expected to be appearing in his final World Cup. The prospect of his team reaching the final — and potentially winning back-to-back titles following their 2022 triumph in Qatar — adds enormous narrative weight to what is already the standout fixture of the knockout rounds.

Argentina's squad also includes several top-level European club players whose form throughout the tournament has reinforced their status as co-favourites alongside England to lift the trophy. England have never beaten Argentina at a World Cup in normal time, a record that adds further edge to the showdown.

What a Deep Run Means for City

For Enzo Maresca, England's run presents a logistical consideration. Anderson was expected to join City's pre-season Asia tour, which includes friendlies against Inter Milan (1 August, Hong Kong), the K League All-Stars (5 August, Seoul), and Atlético Madrid (9 August, Seoul). If England reach the final on 19 July and win, Anderson will return to Manchester with barely three weeks before the Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff on 16 August and City's Premier League opener against Bournemouth on 23 August.

Maresca has publicly acknowledged the situation. City are planning to accelerate Anderson's physical integration once he returns, given his role as the linchpin of the midfield the new head coach is constructing. The club will manage his minutes carefully in pre-season to ensure he is match-ready for the competitive campaign.

For Haaland, the World Cup exit — though painful — means he returns to City's pre-season earlier than anticipated. The Norwegian will join the Asia tour and have additional time to adapt to Maresca's system before the season opener. In Haaland's mind, that may be one small consolation from a tournament that ended cruelly just one game too soon.

Looking Ahead

The England vs Argentina semi-final kicks off on Wednesday 15 July at 8pm BST in Atlanta. A win would take England into the final on 19 July — and keep Anderson away from Manchester for another week. City fans will be watching with a peculiar mix of emotions: willing Anderson on while also calculating when he might be back at Carrington.

Whatever happens on Wednesday, the 2026 World Cup has already left its mark on Manchester City's summer. Haaland's run of seven goals will fuel his hunger for the new campaign; Anderson's emergence on the international stage has reinforced why City spent a record fee to bring him to the Etihad. The new era under Maresca is about to begin — one way or another, by the end of this week they will know when it truly starts.
