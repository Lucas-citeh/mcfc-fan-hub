Tuchel Green Light: Anderson Free to Complete City Medical at World Cup as Romano Issues Positive Update

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 9 June 2026

England manager Thomas Tuchel has explicitly confirmed he will allow squad members to undergo medical examinations to complete club transfers during the 2026 FIFA World Cup in North America — a ruling that directly clears the path for Elliot Anderson to finalise his move to Manchester City while on international duty. Fabrizio Romano has simultaneously issued a positive update on City's stance, confirming the club remain confident of striking a deal with Nottingham Forest despite the initial bid rejection.

The Tuchel announcement, first reported by CityXtra and corroborated by multiple sources on 9 June, is significant on two levels. First, it removes a logistical obstacle that had threatened to delay an agreement into late summer: England's World Cup campaign begins on 17 June, and with Anderson eager to settle his future beforehand, the prospect of a medical taking place in North America while he is on Three Lions duty now becomes a real possibility. Second, it signals that Tuchel — despite having Anderson available for selection in a summer where City's numbers are already being stretched by Bernardo Silva's departure — is not going to stand in the player's way.

Romano's Update: City Confident Despite Forest's Stance

Fabrizio Romano has issued a fresh update on the Anderson situation, confirming that Manchester City's confidence in completing the deal has not been shaken by Nottingham Forest's rejection of their opening bid. Forest are holding firm at a figure in excess of £100 million for the 23-year-old England international, whose contract at the City Ground runs until 2028. Their position is understandable: Anderson was one of the standout midfielders in the Premier League in 2025/26, they are under no financial pressure to sell, and their leverage increases with every day they can point to a World Cup audition that further cements his value.

City, according to Romano's reporting, remain undeterred. The club's sporting director Hugo Viana has identified Anderson as the number-one midfield priority for Enzo Maresca's rebuild — ahead of the Plan B option of Sandro Tonali (Newcastle United, also per Romano) — and the club are preparing a second, significantly improved bid that is expected to be submitted as soon as the Premier League transfer window fully opens.

The Personal Terms Picture

While club-to-club negotiations remain ongoing, the player's own position could scarcely be clearer. Anderson has already agreed personal terms with Manchester City in principle — a reported five-year deal, per journalist Nicolo Schira — and the 23-year-old wants to have his future sorted before England's opening World Cup match against Croatia. Manchester United, who had been credited with interest, have now largely stepped back: sources indicate INEOS are focused elsewhere in the market and believe Anderson would prefer City in any case.

Tuchel's decision to permit medicals during the tournament is, in practical terms, a nod to the reality on the ground. Anderson is one of several players in the England squad whose transfer situations are unresolved heading into the tournament, and Tuchel — who built a pragmatic reputation for handling the intersection of club and international football during his time at Chelsea and Bayern — has chosen not to create unnecessary friction over something he cannot prevent.

Forest's Position: Firm, But Bridgeable

Nottingham Forest's rejection of City's opening offer was swift and unambiguous, and their £100 million-plus valuation is not a negotiating position so much as a firm floor. The club qualified for European football in 2025/26 and have no reason to accept a discount for a player who remains central to their own ambitions. That said, sources with knowledge of the talks suggest the gap between the two clubs is not unbridgeable. City are willing to approach Forest's valuation, and the structure of the deal — add-ons, performance bonuses, sell-on percentages — may allow both clubs to land at a headline fee that satisfies Forest while keeping the net spend manageable for City.

The key variable is time. City want the deal done before pre-season begins in July. Forest's leverage diminishes slightly the closer the season gets without a resolution, particularly if Anderson makes clear — as he appears already to have done — that his preference is for the Etihad. A transfer saga that bleeds into August serves neither club well.

What Anderson Brings to Maresca's City

Enzo Maresca's midfield blueprint for 2026/27 requires exactly the profile Anderson represents. The departure of Bernardo Silva — City's pre-eminent ball-carrier and press-escaper — left a void that none of the existing midfield options fully fills. Tijjani Reijnders, Mateo Kovacic, Nico Gonzalez and Nico O'Reilly are all capable of contributing, but none has Anderson's combination of vertical dynamism, shooting from range, and ability to turn defence into attack in two touches.

At 23, Anderson also fits the age profile of City's rebuild. He will be at the peak of his powers when the current generation of Haaland, Foden, Cherki and Reijnders are in their late twenties — precisely the window in which City will want to be competing at the top of the Premier League and in Europe under Maresca.

The Broader City Picture

The Anderson pursuit is happening against a backdrop of significant summer activity at the Etihad. Savinho is on the verge of completing a £55–60 million move to Tottenham Hotspur. Jeremy Doku, whose exit had been rumoured given his restricted minutes, is instead understood to be one step away from signing a new contract until 2031 — a statement of Maresca's faith in the Belgian. Josko Gvardiol's contract situation has been met with public confidence from Hugo Viana, who made the renewal of the Croatian defender a priority on taking up the sporting director role. And at the back, the addition of Marc Guehi from Crystal Palace in January 2026 has already given the defence an upgrade that will carry forward into the new era.

The Anderson deal is the centrepiece of all of it. When it is concluded — and the direction of every credible report suggests it will be concluded, at a price — Enzo Maresca's City will have a midfield that can genuinely compete with the best in Europe.
