Deal On: Tottenham Launch Formal Talks With City for Savinho as De Zerbi Works Fast

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 3 June 2026

Tottenham Hotspur have launched formal transfer negotiations with Manchester City for Brazilian winger Savinho, with Fabrizio Romano reporting the deal is "on" and new Spurs head coach Roberto De Zerbi working quickly to get it over the line. The 22-year-old is open to the move, and City are ready to sell at approximately £60 million.

The development marks the most significant step yet in a deal that had been gaining momentum throughout late May. While Tottenham's interest was first signalled when Romano confirmed De Zerbi's full approval for the transfer, the opening of formal negotiations on Tuesday 2 June — confirmed by Romano and Caught Off Side — signals that both clubs are now actively working toward an agreement rather than merely circling one another.

Romano: 'Deal On'

Fabrizio Romano reported on 2–3 June that formal talks are under way and the deal is "on," with De Zerbi "working fast" to finalise it. In Romano's transfer reporting, that language marks a clear escalation: it means both parties have moved beyond initial interest and are now in the process of negotiating a fee. His phrase "deal on" has consistently preceded official announcements by a matter of days or weeks in prior windows.

Manchester City's position is that they are willing to sell at a figure in the region of £60 million — substantially less than Savinho's original acquisition cost but a reflection of market value for a player who made fewer starts than expected in 2025/26 as competition for wide positions intensified. For City, a clean sale at that figure represents good business on an asset who deserves to be a first-choice starter.

De Zerbi's Specific Request

The key factor driving pace on the Tottenham side is Roberto De Zerbi's personal backing. The Italian, appointed as Spurs' head coach earlier this summer, identified Savinho specifically as the wide player who fits his system and gave his full approval to the signing before club-level negotiations had even begun. That alignment between manager and sporting department removes an obstacle that has derailed other Tottenham transfer pursuits in recent windows.

De Zerbi's style of football — attack-oriented, high-press, with wide forwards who carry the ball directly — suits Savinho precisely. At Brighton and Inter Milan, De Zerbi consistently built his attacks around direct, nimble wingers who could beat their man in one-versus-one situations. Savinho, when given licence and confidence, is one of the most dangerous dribblers in the Premier League. A move to Spurs under De Zerbi would give him exactly the platform his City career denied him.

A Record-Breaking Fee for Spurs

At approximately £60 million, the deal would surpass Tottenham's existing transfer record. That is a significant statement of intent from a club that has spent recent years rebuilding its financial model under ENIC. The willingness to break the record for a wide player — rather than a centre-forward or defensive cornerstone — reflects how seriously De Zerbi has communicated his vision to the board.

Savinho is also a player at the right end of the age curve: 22 years old, with his best football still ahead of him and an international future with Brazil. If De Zerbi can unlock the version of him that City supporters glimpsed in flashes, the fee will look entirely justifiable.

City's Summer Rebuild Continues

Savinho's anticipated departure — if confirmed — would be the fourth senior exit of Manchester City's summer. Bernardo Silva and John Stones have already left as free agents following the end of their contracts, and Manuel Akanji's permanent move to Inter Milan was triggered in May after the Italian champions met the contractual conditions. Losing Savinho would further reduce the depth in wide attacking areas, leaving Jeremy Doku and Antoine Semenyo as the primary wide options — or pointing firmly toward incoming reinforcement.

Sporting director Hugo Viana and head coach Enzo Maresca have identified the replacement target: Yan Diomande, the 19-year-old RB Leipzig winger who won the Bundesliga Rookie of the Year award with 13 goals and 10 assists in 2025/26. The pursuit is competitive — Liverpool are pushing hard for a pre-World Cup agreement — but City view him as the long-term successor for the position. At €100–120 million, Leipzig's asking price is steep, but the profile fits Maresca's requirements precisely.

What Comes Next

The Premier League transfer window officially opens on 15 June. With the 2026 World Cup beginning on 11 June, City and Spurs will likely seek to reach at minimum a fee agreement before the window opens, giving both clubs certainty as the tournament gets under way. Romano's "deal on" language, combined with the active formal talks, suggests an official announcement is a matter of when rather than if.

For Savinho, the timing is poignant: he will travel to North America this summer with Brazil — and when he returns to domestic football in August, it will almost certainly be in a Tottenham shirt.
