England Eliminated: Anderson Heads Home as Argentina Stun Three Lions in Atlanta Semi-Final

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 16 July 2026

England's 2026 World Cup campaign ended in heartbreak on Wednesday evening as Argentina came from behind to win 2-1 at Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta, booking their place in the final. Anthony Gordon had put England ahead in the second half — tapping home from Morgan Rogers' cross — but Argentina hit back through Enzo Fernandez (85') and a dramatic Lautaro Martinez header (90+2'), with Lionel Messi assisting both. For Manchester City fans, the defeat means one thing above all else: Elliot Anderson is coming home.

How Argentina Won It

England had carried the superior momentum into the second half, with Anthony Gordon breaking the deadlock by converting at the back post after Morgan Rogers' teasing cross found him unmarked. For a spell that stretched almost to the 85th minute, England looked set to reach a first World Cup final since 1966.

Argentina, however, refused to capitulate. Enzo Fernandez — the Chelsea midfielder carrying enormous weight in this tournament — levelled with a long-range strike that left Jordan Pickford with no chance. And when, seven minutes later in stoppage time, Lionel Messi threaded a pass through a static England defence, Lautaro Martinez met it with a firm header to put the defending champions into the final. Messi, who had assisted both goals, left the field to a roar from the neutral sections — and the certainty that, at 38, he has one final act to write on the world's biggest stage.

England were left deflated. A team that had navigated every round with discipline and menace ultimately paid for a cautious approach in the closing stages that allowed the reigning world champions — who now face Spain in the final on Sunday 19 July at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey — to find a way through.

Anderson's World Cup — and What Comes Next

For City fans, Elliot Anderson was a constant point of interest throughout England's campaign. The 23-year-old central midfielder — who became the most expensive British player in history when City agreed a £116m deal with Nottingham Forest earlier this summer — was part of Thomas Tuchel's England squad for the duration of the tournament. England had gone further than they did in the quarter-final, only to be cut down at the semi-final stage by the world's most decorated player.

Anderson is now expected to return to Carrington for a period of rest and recovery. According to reports from CityXtra, Anderson is unlikely to travel on Enzo Maresca's Asia Tour — which takes City to Hong Kong to face Inter Milan on 1 August and then to Seoul for fixtures against the K League All-Stars (5 August) and Atlético Madrid (9 August). The club had always planned for this possibility, and Maresca's coaching staff have drawn up an accelerated integration programme to bring Anderson up to match fitness on his return.

Anderson's City debut is expected to come in the FA Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff on Sunday 16 August — just ten days before City's Premier League opener against Bournemouth at the Etihad. That timeline is challenging but manageable, and City will have no incentive to rush a player they paid a record fee to acquire.

The Pre-Season Picture

Maresca's squad had already been assembling in stages as players returned from World Cup duty. Erling Haaland reported back to Carrington following Norway's quarter-final exit to England on 11 July — and, with seven tournament goals to his name, will head to Asia as one of the headline names of the pre-season tour. Omar Marmoush has also been available for several weeks, having returned after Egypt's elimination in the last 16.

The departure of Anderson from the World Cup is bittersweet. As a City supporter the instinct is to want him back at Carrington; the England fan would have wanted one more week. What it does guarantee is that, by the time the Community Shield arrives, Anderson will have had time to complete his contractual paperwork, meet his new teammates, and absorb the early workings of a Maresca system that promises to be demanding and intricate in equal measure.

Argentina Await Spain

Argentina's reward for eliminating England is a final meeting with Spain — who had earlier produced a dominant 2-0 victory over France on 14 July. The two European powers will contest a compelling final: Spain have been the tournament's most consistent team, while Argentina have Messi, the greatest player in history, searching for one last piece of immortality. Kick-off is 3pm ET (8pm BST) on Sunday 19 July at MetLife Stadium.

For City fans watching from across the Atlantic, the final is a sideshow. The real countdown now is to 16 August and the first glimpse of a sky-blue shirt on Elliot Anderson.
