Rodri Signing Day: City's Decisive Wednesday as Community Shield Looms

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 12 August 2026

Wednesday 12 August is the day Manchester City supporters have been bracing for all summer. Rodri — Ballon d'Or winner, World Cup Golden Ball recipient, and the midfield engine behind City's six Premier League titles — is expected to arrive in Barcelona today to sign a four-year deal with FC Barcelona, completing the €50 million transfer agreed between the clubs on 9 August. No official announcement from mancity.com or FC Barcelona has been received as of the time of writing; but barring an unexpected final-hour complication, this is the day Rodrigo Hernández Cascante bids farewell to the Etihad.

The Rodri Saga: From Real Madrid to Barcelona

The summer felt for months as though it was heading to one destination. Real Madrid were the frontrunners for most of July, with personal terms reportedly agreed and a first formal approach to City submitted on 6 August. But the fee gap proved unbridgeable — City held firm at €80 million while Madrid refused to go beyond €55–60 million. On 7 August, Rodri gave Barcelona the green light instead, per ESPN, Sky Sports, Yahoo Sports and beIN Sports. By 9 August, Barcelona and Manchester City had reached a club-to-club agreement at €50 million, with Radio Catalunya 1 and Yahoo Sports confirming the terms that evening. Rodri's preference to join alongside Spain's other World Cup players made today the natural arrival date.

The departure had been further complicated by the fact that Rodri underwent back surgery in Madrid on 28 July — a lumbar issue he managed throughout Spain's World Cup campaign. He would not have been available to play until mid-September in any case. That made his extended stay in City's colours this summer less a question of contributions and more one of formalities. The opening months of the 2026/27 season were always going to be played without him; the question became permanent only with the transfer.

What Rodri's Departure Means for Maresca's City

Rodri's absence from the squad has already been a fact of pre-season life. Enzo Maresca's side shaped up without him in all three tour games — a penalty-shootout defeat to Inter Milan in Hong Kong, a 3-1 win over K League All-Stars in Seoul, and a 2-1 loss to Atlético Madrid in the same city. The midfield combination of Mateo Kovacic, Tijjani Reijnders and Elliot Anderson — who has been integrating at Carrington since returning from England's World Cup semi-final campaign on 16 July — will be the foundation for the Community Shield in Cardiff on Sunday.

Rodri is not easily replaced. Over six Premier League title campaigns, his positioning, press-resistance and reading of the game turned the area in front of City's defence into an impenetrable zone. Elliot Anderson is a different animal — a progressive, box-to-box runner whose carry and press numbers are exceptional, but who does not offer the same deep-lying control. Nico Gonzalez provides the holding option but has fewer than 30 Premier League appearances to his name. Kovacic remains dependable, if less distinctive.

The longer-term answer remains in progress. The pursuit of Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille has accelerated significantly — FootballTransfers reported an agreement reached and TribalFootball.com placed the Bouaddi and Rulli deals both close to completion in reports this week. The Moroccan midfielder, 18, is regarded across the club as the natural long-term successor — but whether he joins this summer or after the loan-back obstacle is resolved, his acquisition represents City's clearest statement of intent about the post-Rodri era.

Rulli: Close to Official Confirmation

On the goalkeeping front, Gerónimo Rulli's arrival from Olympique de Marseille is moving steadily toward official announcement. The Argentine completed his medical at the Etihad Campus on Monday 10 August, per CaughtOffside, and multiple outlets including beIN SPORTS ("Rulli Joins Manchester City to Strengthen Goalkeeping Options") and leadership.ng ("Manchester City Sign Marseille Goalkeeper Rulli For £1.7m") have already published the signing as complete. A two-year contract to June 2028, with an option to extend by a further year, has been reported by both beIN SPORTS and Yahoo Sports. The fee is in the region of £1.7 million–€2 million.

Rulli, 34, joins as backup to Gianluigi Donnarumma, who remains the uncontested first choice following his move from PSG in summer 2025. The choice of Rulli — an experienced, decorated professional who started the 2021 Europa League Final for Villarreal — over a younger alternative underlines that City want calm, senior depth in the goalkeeper position, not another development project. His formal announcement on mancity.com is expected imminently.

Bouaddi: Intensive Final Push

The Bouaddi saga continues its slow final approach. Goal.com described the deal as "imminent" on 11 August; Yahoo Sports and Get French Football News reported City and Lille "poised to finalise" the agreement. Fabrizio Romano, via mancitynews.com, stated City are "on the verge" of their fifth summer signing. FootballTransfers went further, reporting an agreement reached at a record fee — in the region of €85–100 million — that would make Bouaddi the most expensive teenager in football history.

The outstanding issue has been the loan-back arrangement. Lille wanted Bouaddi to return for the 2026/27 season; Bouaddi himself reportedly prefers to stay in Manchester and begin his City career immediately. That preference from the player's side gives City leverage. The absence of an official announcement from either mancity.com or Lille means the deal remains in our Rumours panel — but the volume and direction of credible reporting over the past week suggests it is a matter of when, not if.

Community Shield: Four Days in Cardiff

Whatever today's news brings, the first competitive fixture of the Maresca era is now four days away. The 2026 FA Community Shield takes place at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff on Sunday 16 August, kicking off at 15:00 BST. The match is live on TNT Sports and HBO Max in the UK.

Arsenal arrive in Cardiff as Premier League champions, having pipped City by three points on the final day of the 2025/26 season. City arrive as FA Cup holders — Antoine Semenyo's 72nd-minute back-heel at Wembley in May sealed a 1-0 victory over Chelsea. For Maresca, the Community Shield is the first chance to demonstrate on a competitive stage how he wants to play — and for supporters, the first glimpse of what post-Rodri, post-Guardiola City looks like when it matters.

Haaland, Elliot Anderson, Marc Guehi and Nico O'Reilly are all expected to be available having returned from post-World Cup breaks. The pre-season evidence — Reijnders' energy, Semenyo's continued excellence, Mubama's promise — suggests Maresca's squad has more depth and direction than the summer's high-profile departures might imply. The answers begin in Cardiff on Sunday.
