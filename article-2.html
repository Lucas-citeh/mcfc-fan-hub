Rodri Stays: Fabrizio Romano Confirms Real Madrid Will Not Pursue City's Ballon d'Or Winner This Summer

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 25 June 2026

Manchester City fans can breathe easier. Fabrizio Romano has confirmed that Real Madrid will not pursue a transfer for Rodri this summer, ruling out the most credible exit threat facing the Ballon d'Or winner. City's contract offer remains on the table, with renewal talks expected to resume once Spain's World Cup campaign concludes.

Romano Confirms: Real Madrid Step Back

The transfer story that had lingered through much of the spring — would Jose Mourinho's Real Madrid attempt to sign Rodri after landing Bernardo Silva? — has been definitively answered. Fabrizio Romano confirmed that, following the completion of the Silva deal, Real Madrid decided against pursuing the Spanish midfielder. Club president Florentino Perez separately confirmed that Rodri was never a target for the club in the current window.

The decision makes strategic sense for Madrid. With Bernardo Silva arriving on a free transfer and Mourinho's midfield priorities satisfied, there was no room or urgency for a further, enormously expensive addition in the same area. The Portuguese connection that made the Rodri link plausible — Mourinho and his compatriots Silva and, potentially, Ruben Dias — was always more about opportunity than a settled plan.

City's Contract Offer: Ball in Rodri's Court

Manchester City have had a contract proposal on the table for Rodri for some time. The Ballon d'Or winner, whose current deal expires in the summer of 2027, entered the World Cup window with talks ongoing but unresolved. Rodri's own public position has been consistent and measured: "If there hadn't been a World Cup, things might be different now." His focus, for now, is entirely on international duty with Spain.

Reports from May 2026 indicated City were "nearing" a renewal, with club sources consistently emphasising that Rodri was not going anywhere. Rodri himself, speaking to TNT Sports before the tournament, described himself as "very calm" about his future — a phrase that, taken alongside Romano's Madrid confirmation, paints a picture of a player whose mind is already settled on staying.

Why the Real Madrid Threat Was Always the Key Risk

The concern around Rodri's contract was never really about money. Manchester City are one of the wealthiest clubs in world football, and the terms gap that Romano earlier reported — a "significant" difference between City's offer and Rodri's demands — was always understood to be bridgeable with goodwill on both sides. The real risk was always the same one that claimed Bernardo Silva: the pull of the Bernabéu, amplified by Mourinho's Portuguese-speaking rebuild.

With that threat now formally off the table, the path is clear. Rodri's agent and Manchester City can resume talks in July or August, once Spain's tournament run has concluded. There is no queue of clubs lining up to trigger a bidding war; City hold the contractual and financial leverage, and Rodri has given every public signal that he wants to remain at the Etihad.

What Rodri's Renewal Would Mean for Maresca

Enzo Maresca takes over a squad in transition — Guardiola gone, Bernardo Silva gone, John Stones and Manuel Akanji also departed. Retaining Rodri would be the single most important piece of continuity available to him. The Spaniard's ability to dictate tempo, protect the defence, and distribute with precision is unmatched in the Premier League and arguably in European football. He is the kind of player around whom Maresca can build an entire system with confidence.

The Elliot Anderson pursuit, which has consumed most of the transfer window's attention and resources, is designed to add dynamism and box-to-box energy to a City midfield being reshaped. But no acquisition closes the gap left by losing Rodri. With Real Madrid out of the picture, and Rodri himself relaxed about his future, the prospect of building Maresca's era around the Ballon d'Or winner looks more realistic than it did even a month ago.

The World Cup ends for most nations by mid-July. Then the conversation begins in earnest.
