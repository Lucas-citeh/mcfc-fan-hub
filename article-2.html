Reijnders Future in Doubt: Atletico Madrid Open Talks as Maresca Plots New Midfield Order

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 10 June 2026

Tijjani Reijnders could be leaving Manchester City just twelve months after joining, with Sky Sports reporting the club are weighing up the Dutch midfielder's future as Atletico Madrid have opened talks over a potential transfer. The development adds a significant subplot to City's summer rebuild under new head coach Enzo Maresca — who takes charge with questions already forming over the shape of his midfield.

Reijnders arrived at the Etihad from AC Milan in the summer of 2025, one of the most eye-catching pieces of business in City's post-2024 rebuild. He started the 2025/26 campaign brightly, excelling in transition and arriving late into the penalty area with a directness that City's midfield had often lacked. But as the season wore on, he found his starts rationed: he made 27 appearances across the Premier League campaign but was rarely a fixture in the eleven, and his contribution — five goals and four assists in the league — sat below the level expected of a player signed at that price.

Atletico Madrid: Talks Opened, Offer Being Prepared

According to Football España, Atletico Madrid made initial contact with Manchester City as far back as late May. CaughtOffside followed on 4 June, reporting that Atletico are now preparing a formal bid for the 27-year-old. Sky Sports subsequently reported that City are actively considering Reijnders' future — a phrase that, in the context of a managerial transition and a squad restructure, carries real weight.

The logic of the move from Atletico's perspective is clear. Diego Simeone has long sought a dynamic box-to-box midfielder who can carry the ball through lines, press with intensity, and contribute directly at goal. Reijnders fits that profile almost perfectly: a modern number eight with the athletic profile, the pressing capacity, and the range of passing that Simeone's evolving system demands. At 27, he is in the prime years of his career and would not require the developmental patience that younger targets might.

City's Position: Contractual Strength, But Not Actively Pushing

Manchester City are not, according to the reporting available, actively trying to move Reijnders on. He signed a five-year deal when he joined last summer, giving City firm contractual ground. But the nuance matters: City are not ruling out a sale either, particularly if Atletico's offer reflects the player's market value — reports suggest the Spanish club are preparing a bid in the region of €60–70 million, which would represent a meaningful return on the €55 million City paid AC Milan twelve months ago.

The key context is Maresca's arrival. When a new manager takes over at a major club, the squad inevitably gets reassessed from scratch. Some players who thrived under the previous regime find themselves surplus to requirements; others, perhaps underestimated, are identified as pillars of the new project. Reijnders' position in Maresca's thinking is not yet clear — the Italian has not publicly addressed individual squad members — and that uncertainty is precisely the kind of environment in which a well-resourced club like Atletico Madrid moves quickly.

The Midfield Picture for 2026/27

If Reijnders does leave, the shape of City's midfield for 2026/27 becomes even more dependent on resolving the Elliot Anderson situation. City's pursuit of the Nottingham Forest midfielder has been framed as a priority for sporting director Hugo Viana precisely because the club needs a dynamic ball-carrier in the mould of Anderson. Reijnders, in theory, occupies a similar role — but if Maresca has reservations about his fit, and if Atletico are willing to pay what City paid for him, the calculus shifts.

The depth picture behind Reijnders includes Mateo Kovacic, Nico Gonzalez, Nico O'Reilly, and Rayan Cherki — the latter of whom can operate in a more advanced midfield position. Of those, Kovacic (32) is not a long-term solution, and Gonzalez and O'Reilly are still developing. If Anderson arrives and Reijnders leaves, Maresca would be working with a younger, more dynamic midfield unit — which may be precisely the direction the new manager wants to go.

What Happens Next

The next few weeks will be decisive. The Premier League transfer window opens on 15 June, and with Reijnders heading to the Netherlands' 2026 World Cup squad — and both City and Atletico Madrid having players in contention for the tournament — there is a practical argument for deferring formal negotiations until July. But the groundwork is being laid now.

Whether this ultimately becomes a full-blown transfer saga or quietly dissipates will depend on two things: whether Maresca confirms publicly that Reijnders has a role in his plans — which would cool Atletico's interest significantly — and whether City's pursuit of Anderson intensifies to the point where selling Reijnders to fund further recruitment becomes financially attractive. For now, it is an open question. And open questions, in the current transfer market, tend to resolve with movement.
