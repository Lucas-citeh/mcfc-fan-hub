Haaland Heads Home: City's Goal Machine Reports Back as Maresca's Pre-Season Gathers Pace

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 14 July 2026

Erling Haaland is back in Manchester and reporting to City's Carrington training complex after Norway's elimination at the hands of England in the World Cup quarter-final. The Norwegian ended the tournament with seven goals from five appearances — one of the standout individual tallies at the 2026 World Cup — but a Jude Bellingham brace in extra time brought the dream to an end on 11 July. Now, with Enzo Maresca's squad assembling for what will be his first pre-season in charge, Haaland's return is a significant boost ahead of the Asia Tour.

A World Cup to Remember — But It Still Hurts

Haaland's 2026 World Cup was, by almost any individual measure, exceptional. Seven goals from five games — including a brace in a memorable 2-1 win over Brazil in the round of sixteen — kept him in contention for the Golden Boot throughout the knockout rounds. He carried Norway to the quarter-final, the deepest the nation has progressed at a major international tournament in decades.

The quarter-final, however, brought heartbreak. Facing an England side inspired by Jude Bellingham, Haaland was kept goalless as Norway were edged out 2-1 after extra time at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami. Bellingham equalised on the stroke of half-time and grabbed the winner three minutes into additional time. It was a cruel outcome for a player who had given everything on the international stage — and one that will only sharpen his hunger for the 2026/27 club season.

What His Return Means for Maresca

For incoming head coach Enzo Maresca, Haaland's arrival back in Salford is one of the more welcome developments during what has otherwise been a complicated summer. With a large portion of the squad dispersed across the 2026 World Cup — particularly deep into the knockout rounds — Maresca has had to build his pre-season plans in stages, integrating players as they return from international duty.

Haaland gives Maresca his most important attacking asset ahead of the Asia Tour. City are scheduled to face Inter Milan in Hong Kong on 1 August, before travelling to Seoul for fixtures against the K League All-Stars on 5 August and Atlético Madrid on 9 August. All three games will be Maresca's first as Manchester City manager. Having Haaland in the squad for those fixtures — assuming he comes through pre-season training without issue — will be significant as the new head coach begins to impose his system on the squad.

Omar Marmoush, whose Egypt were eliminated by Argentina in the round of sixteen, has also been available to Maresca for several weeks. With the main attacking options now assembling at Carrington, City's preparation is entering its most important phase.

Anderson: Still Going

The one significant absentee from Maresca's preparations remains Elliot Anderson. City's club-record £116m signing from Nottingham Forest continues at the World Cup with England, who face Argentina in the semi-final on Wednesday 15 July in Atlanta. A victory for England would keep Anderson in the United States for the final on 19 July — meaning he would not return to Carrington until late July at the earliest, leaving him with a compressed window before the Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff on 16 August and City's Premier League opener against Bournemouth on 23 August.

Maresca has acknowledged the situation, with City planning an accelerated integration programme for Anderson on his return. His role in the new head coach's midfield is expected to be central, and the club will manage his early minutes carefully to ensure he is ready for the competitive calendar.

The Season on the Horizon

Maresca inherits a squad in deliberate transition. The departures of Pep Guardiola, Bernardo Silva, John Stones, Nathan Aké, and Manuel Akanji represent a genuine change in the group's experience and character. But the summer arrivals — Elliot Anderson (central midfield, club-record £116m from Nottingham Forest), Jeremy Monga (winger, £10m from Leicester City), Mathys Detourbet (winger, on loan to Monaco), and Pierce Charles (goalkeeper, on loan to QPR) — point to a deliberate generational reset aligned to Maresca's vision.

With Haaland at the spearhead of the attack, Maresca has an immediately lethal focal point to build around. The Norwegian's return is the moment City fans have been waiting for since Norway's exit: confirmation that the goal machine is back in sky blue, recharged and hungry. The new era begins in Hong Kong in eighteen days' time.
