Norway vs Brazil — World Cup Round of 16 Preview: Haaland Eyes Sixth Goal as City Pre-Season Ticks Down

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 1 July 2026

Twenty-four hours after Erling Haaland's 86th-minute winner wrote Norwegian football history, the prize is clear: five-time world champions Brazil, at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, on Sunday 5 July (kickoff 4pm ET / 9pm BST). Haaland has five World Cup goals in three games. Norway have just claimed their first-ever knockout win. The biggest test yet awaits.

The Fixture

MetLife Stadium, the 82,500-seat home of the New York Giants, stages this last-16 tie on Sunday. Brazil advanced through their Round of 32 match against Japan to set up a meeting with the now battle-hardened Norwegians. It is the game of the Round of 16 on paper — Norway's historic momentum against the tournament's most storied nation.

Norway head into Sunday on the back of back-to-back positive results and a group that has rallied around the prospect of going further than any Norwegian team in history. Brazil arrive as firm favourites, but having been pushed closer than expected by Japan, they know the knockout rounds carry no guarantees.

Haaland's Tournament in Numbers

Five goals in three games. Haaland has been the decisive force at every turn: a brace against Iraq (4-1), two more against Senegal (3-2), and now the dramatic 86th-minute winner that ended Ivory Coast's World Cup. He has shown the full range of a complete striker — link-up play, late runs, hold-up work — and has arrived at every critical moment with the composure that defines elite-level finishers.

Against Brazil, he will face a defensive structure of an entirely different calibre. The Seleção's centre-backs are experienced, physical, and well-drilled in managing direct strikers. How much space they afford Haaland — and how Norway set about creating it — will determine whether Tuesday's heroics can be repeated.

The Brazil Threat

Brazil are not merely five-time world champions by reputation. Their squad for this tournament contains genuine quality at every position, from a technically excellent midfield to a front line capable of breaking any defence in the world on their day. They edged their group-stage campaign and defeated Japan to reach this stage — not dominant, but efficient. The expectation is on them, and that pressure can cut both ways.

Norway will look to compact their defensive structure, press high when the opportunity presents, and hit Brazil on the transition. Solbakken's side have shown they can be resilient in adversity — the Ivory Coast comeback, conceding in the 74th minute and responding with Haaland's 86th-minute winner, demonstrated exactly that quality.

What a Norway Win Means for City

For Enzo Maresca and Manchester City's pre-season planning, this fixture is being followed as closely in Manchester as in Oslo. City's Community Shield against Arsenal falls on 16 August in Cardiff; the Premier League opener against Bournemouth is 23 August. Pre-season training is expected to begin in mid-July.

If Norway lose on Sunday 5 July, Haaland could realistically be back in Manchester by 8–10 July, giving him a meaningful pre-season window. If Norway progress through the last 16 and into a quarter-final (from approximately 11 July), that window shrinks significantly. A semi-final run would bring Haaland back with barely a fortnight before the Community Shield.

City have managed this before, and Maresca will have plans in place for all scenarios. But in a season where a new manager is installing a new system with new personnel, having their most important player available from day one of preparation would be valuable. Sunday's result will shape the timeline.

Norwegian Belief Has Never Been Higher

For Norway's supporters, all of the above is noise. This generation of players — led by Haaland and Ødegaard — has spent their careers watching the national team fall short of qualification for major tournaments. Being at a World Cup and in the last 16, with a historic first knockout win already banked, represents everything they were promised was possible.

The odds are long against Brazil. The precedent from Tuesday — Haaland arriving in the 86th minute to win the game — shows that Norway do not need to be the better side across 90 minutes to go through. They need moments. And right now, they have the man most likely to create them.
