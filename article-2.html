Maresca Confirmed: Romano Reports Three-Year Deal to Succeed Guardiola

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 23 May 2026

Enzo Maresca will be Manchester City's next manager. Fabrizio Romano has confirmed the Italian has reached total verbal agreement with the club and is set to sign an initial three-year deal at the Etihad Stadium, succeeding Pep Guardiola when the Spaniard departs after Sunday's final-day fixture against Aston Villa.

The appointment, first reported in principle by David Ornstein of The Athletic on 18 May, is now understood to be as good as done. Romano's report, published on 20 May, provides the additional detail of a three-year contract term — taking Maresca through to at least the summer of 2029. The announcement is expected to come in the days following Sunday's match.

A Familiar Face Returns

Maresca, 46, is no stranger to Manchester City. He served as one of Guardiola's assistant coaches during the 2022–23 season — the campaign that delivered the historic treble. His training-ground fingerprints are on the methods, structure, and culture that Guardiola built at the Etihad, giving the appointment a sense of continuity at a time when the club might otherwise have lurched toward a complete reset.

After leaving City's coaching staff, Maresca took his first senior management role at Leicester City, winning the Championship title and guiding the Foxes back to the Premier League in 2023–24. He was subsequently appointed Chelsea manager, where he lasted until New Year's Day 2026 before leaving following conversations with the Manchester City hierarchy about succeeding Guardiola.

What He Inherits

Maresca will take charge of a squad in the middle of a rebuild. Bernardo Silva and John Stones both depart as free agents this summer, ending long and decorated chapters at the club. The challenge is to maintain the standards that have brought six Premier League titles in eight seasons without the manager who made it possible.

There are reasons for optimism. Erling Haaland — the fastest player in Premier League history to score 100 goals — remains the focal point of the attack heading into a new era. Phil Foden signed a new four-year contract this week, running until 2030, signalling his own commitment to the project through the transition. A squad of genuine quality, with Tijjani Reijnders, Josko Gvardiol, and Rayan Cherki all in the prime or early stages of their careers, gives Maresca a strong foundation to build from.

Guardiola's Shadow

No incoming manager faces a harder act to follow. Guardiola's ten years in Manchester have produced six Premier League titles, the 2022–23 Champions League, seven League Cups, and two FA Cups — including the trophy won at Wembley just one week ago when Antoine Semenyo's back-heel finish settled a 1–0 victory over Chelsea. In any other era, that would be a career highlight. For City, it is a punctuation mark.

Maresca knows the club, the playing style, and many of the players. That familiarity is precisely why City moved for him. The task is to keep the machine running — and perhaps, in time, to make it his own.

Sources: Fabrizio Romano (20 May 2026); David Ornstein, The Athletic (18 May 2026).
