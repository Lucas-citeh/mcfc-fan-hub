Savinho Signs New Deal: Tottenham Saga Over as Brazilian Commits to City Until 2031

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 29 July 2026

The Savinho transfer saga is over — and Manchester City have won. The club officially confirmed on 29 July 2026 that the Brazilian winger has signed a new six-year contract at the Etihad Stadium, keeping him until summer 2031 with an option to extend by a further year. In his first interview following the announcement, Savinho declared: "City is the best club in the world."

The Deal That Ends the Saga

Less than 48 hours after Fabrizio Romano had declared that Savinho's move to Tottenham Hotspur was "absolutely on", Manchester City officially confirmed the opposite: Savinho is staying, and has committed to a new long-term deal. The contract, confirmed by mancity.com, is for six years and supersedes the existing arrangement that had been due to run to 2031 — extending his stay into the 2030s with an additional option year.

Goal.com captured the mood at Spurs succinctly: "Spurs dealt massive blow as winger decides to extend Man City contract after transfer collapse." The Tottenham deal, which Romano had described as progressing and close to completion, collapsed before reaching the announcement stage. The Brazilian chose to remain in Manchester despite concrete interest from the north London club.

What Went Wrong for Tottenham

Tottenham's interest in Savinho had been the dominant subplot of City's summer window. Reports from mid-July suggested City and Spurs had reached an agreement in principle of approximately £60–65m, with Savinho understood to have agreed personal terms. The only obstacle, per Romano and supporting sources, was City's insistence on securing a winger replacement before sanctioning the sale.

It appears that obstacle proved decisive. City were unable to find a suitable replacement winger at a price and availability that worked within their timeframe — and without that replacement, the sale never received final sign-off. The transfer ultimately collapsed, with Savinho opting to commit his future to the Etihad rather than press for a move.

What Savinho Said

In a written interview published alongside the announcement on mancity.com, Savinho spoke candidly about his decision to stay. The Brazilian declared that City is "the best club in the world" — words that will carry particular weight given the context of the saga that preceded them. The interview represents his clearest public statement on the matter and draws a line under months of speculation.

What This Means for Enzo Maresca's Squad

Savinho's confirmation as a City player for 2026/27 significantly strengthens Enzo Maresca's options in wide areas. The squad's attacking width now reads: Jeremy Doku (left), Savinho (right), Antoine Semenyo (left/right), Omar Marmoush (centre/right), Rayan Cherki (left/central) and new signing Jeremy Monga (right). It is a substantial bank of attacking options, and one that gives Maresca genuine flexibility as he builds his own style at the Etihad.

The decision also has implications for the club's broader transfer strategy. With Savinho staying, City's immediate need for a winger replacement disappears — freeing their attention for other priorities: completing the Ayyoub Bouaddi signing from Lille (medical reported as imminent earlier this week) and managing the ongoing Rodri situation, which now dominates the window's remaining narrative.

A Summer Window Taking Shape

For a window that opened with significant uncertainty — Guardiola's departure, the appointment of Maresca, an ageing squad stripped of key figures — City's summer rebuild is beginning to acquire clarity. Elliot Anderson (£116m from Nottingham Forest) brings elite box-to-box energy. Bouaddi, when confirmed, will reshape the midfield axis. Jeremy Monga adds precocious youth to the attack. And now Savinho, whose future had looked uncertain for weeks, has signed on for the long haul.

Tottenham's loss is a significant one. They had positioned themselves for a statement signing and came close. Now they must return to the market without their primary winger target. For City — and for Maresca — Savinho's commitment represents the kind of unambiguous good news that has been in short supply during a complicated few weeks.
