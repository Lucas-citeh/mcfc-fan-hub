Haaland Fires Norway Past Brazil; 10-Man England Survive the Azteca — City's Stars Set for a Quarter-Final Clash

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 6 July 2026

A remarkable 24 hours in the 2026 FIFA World Cup has left two Manchester City players in the quarter-finals — and on a collision course with each other. Erling Haaland scored twice in the final 11 minutes to fire Norway to a stunning 2-1 win over Brazil on Sunday 5 July; Elliot Anderson and his England teammates beat Mexico 3-2 at the Estadio Azteca while playing with 10 men for the final 36 minutes. Norway meet England in the quarter-final on Saturday 11 July in Miami (10pm BST).

Norway 2-1 Brazil — MetLife Stadium, Sunday 5 July

For 78 minutes of this match, Brazil looked in control. A strong defensive display had kept the five-time world champions at arm's length, but Norway were being pressed back and chances were scarce. Bruno Guimarães saw an early penalty saved, keeping the Scandinavians alive, but as the clock ticked towards the 80th minute it looked as though Brazil's tournament would continue.

Then came Haaland. On 79 minutes, Andreas Schjelderup's cross from the right found the Manchester City striker arriving at the near post, and his powerful header gave Alisson no chance — 1-0 Norway. The MetLife Stadium erupted. Three minutes into injury time, with Brazil pushing desperately for an equaliser, Haaland collected possession on the edge of the box, drilled a low shot through the legs of Danilo Santos and past Alisson for 2-0. It was over.

Neymar converted a late consolation penalty to make it 2-1, but there was no late drama to save the Seleção. Brazil, five-time winners and pre-tournament favourites, were eliminated in the round of 16 — their earliest World Cup exit since 1990. For Norway, it was a historic night: their first-ever World Cup quarter-final appearance.

Haaland's brace took his tournament tally to seven goals — the joint-highest at the 2026 World Cup and a serious contender for the Golden Boot. For context: he had scored five goals in all of Norway's World Cup appearances before this tournament began. He has already rewritten Norwegian football history.

Mexico 2-3 England — Estadio Azteca, Monday 6 July

If Norway's win was dramatic, England's was extraordinary. At the most storied venue in World Cup history — the Azteca in Mexico City, at an altitude of roughly 2,240 metres — Thomas Tuchel's side produced one of the tournament's most memorable performances to advance 3-2 in a match that had everything.

Jude Bellingham scored twice in the opening stages to put England in command. Mexico fought back — Julian Quiñones pulled one back before half-time — and the game was finely poised at 2-1 when English right-back Jarrell Quansah received a red card in the 54th minute following a VAR review. England, with 10 men for the remainder of the match, were suddenly vulnerable.

But Harry Kane stepped forward: a penalty in the 60th minute restored England's two-goal cushion to make it 3-1. When Raúl Jiménez converted a penalty of his own in the 69th minute to make it 3-2, the Azteca shook. England held on, absorbing pressure, defending deeper and deeper, surviving on set-piece clearances and Jordan Pickford's brilliance in goal to reach the last eight.

Elliot Anderson was part of England's squad throughout. The City record signing is on course to return for pre-season later than initially hoped — but at least he is still playing tournament football.

The Quarter-Final Draw: Norway vs England

The draw delivered a remarkable subplot: Norway face England in the quarter-final on Saturday 11 July at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami (10pm BST / 5pm ET). Haaland and Anderson — City's two biggest stars of the summer — will face each other in the last eight of the World Cup, just weeks before they are scheduled to be teammates at pre-season training.

One of them will have their World Cup ended on 11 July. If the other's nation survives, a semi-final falls around 14–15 July, and the final on 19 July. In that scenario, a player could miss the entire Asia pre-season tour and return with barely a week before the Community Shield against Arsenal on 16 August in Cardiff.

What This Means for Maresca's Pre-Season

Enzo Maresca is monitoring the situation carefully. Both Haaland and Anderson are guaranteed to be absent until at least 12–13 July after the quarter-final. Standard return-to-club protocols following a World Cup knockout — typically 2–3 weeks of rest and recovery depending on how deep the run goes — mean their integration into City's pre-season will be limited.

If both nations are eliminated in the quarter-final on 11 July, Haaland and Anderson could conceivably be available for the final pre-season fixture against Atlético Madrid in Seoul on 9 August. A deeper run for either pushes that prospect back further. City have managed similar situations before — Haaland only joined pre-season training late in summer 2022 — but the new-manager dynamic makes early bonding and tactical installation more valuable than in a settled regime.

Whatever happens, the City dressing room will be watching the quarter-final very closely. And at least two of Maresca's players will be very conflicted about who to cheer for.
