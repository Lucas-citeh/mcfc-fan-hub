Transfer Window Opens Sunday: City Set to Fire Third Anderson Bid as Savinho Exit Looms

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 13 June 2026

Manchester City begin what promises to be one of the most consequential transfer summers in recent Etihad history this Sunday, when the Premier League window opens on 15 June. Two dominant storylines are expected to define the opening days: a third bid for Nottingham Forest midfielder Elliot Anderson — BBC Sport's Saturday gossip column confirming the 23-year-old as the club's No.1 priority over Crystal Palace's Adam Wharton — and the imminent formalisation of Brazilian winger Savinho's departure to Tottenham Hotspur, a deal multiple sources described as "on the verge of completion" as recently as 9 June.

Anderson First: BBC Confirms City's Priority

BBC Sport's Saturday transfer gossip column, published 13 June, states that Manchester City prefer Elliot Anderson to Adam Wharton as their primary midfield acquisition for the 2026/27 season. The confirmation matters because it resolves one genuine live question: whether City might pivot to Wharton — valued at up to €115 million by Crystal Palace and attracting attention from Real Madrid — as a more achievable alternative should Forest's flat-fee demands prove immovable.

City will not pivot. The BBC's report aligns with a consistent stream of reporting from Sky Sports, Fabrizio Romano, and David Ornstein (The Athletic) throughout the summer, all naming Anderson as the club's No.1 priority since at least March. A third, restructured bid is expected to arrive at the City Ground shortly after the window opens on Sunday.

The sticking point remains Forest's demand for a guaranteed flat fee above £120 million — a fixed sum with no reliance on performance-related add-ons. City's second bid, rejected on 10 June, was structured as £106 million upfront plus £15 million in add-ons, which would have eclipsed Arsenal's £105 million British record for Declan Rice had Forest accepted it on those terms. They did not. The gap between the clubs is not one of overall value but of structure, and City are understood to be prepared to close that gap in the third offer.

Anderson himself remains committed to the move. Personal terms have been agreed in principle — a five-year deal, per journalist Nicolo Schira — and England manager Thomas Tuchel has confirmed that medical procedures can take place during the World Cup, meaning no delay need extend into August.

Savinho to Spurs: Formalisation Expected This Week

The other major piece of business City must conclude in the window's opening days is the departure of Savinho. CaughtOffside's Paul O'Keefe, who has an established record of breaking City-related deals, reported the Brazilian's move to Tottenham Hotspur as "on the verge of completion" on 9 June — with a basic fee of approximately £60 million agreed and only add-on details to finalise. Savinho has agreed personal terms with Spurs on a deal until 2031, worth £120–140k per week per O'Keefe. Fabrizio Romano confirmed Tottenham are "set to announce" the deal once the window opens.

Savinho departed for Brazil's 2026 World Cup squad on 11 June. With the formal transfer procedures — contract exchange, medical, announcement — able to proceed from Sunday, confirmation within the first week of the window is widely anticipated. His departure would represent a fee of approximately double the £30.8 million City paid Troyes for the winger in July 2024.

The Wider Picture: Gvardiol Decision Awaited, Rodri Edging Closer

City's summer agenda extends beyond Anderson and Savinho. The most significant subplot of the coming weeks is Josko Gvardiol's decision on his future. The Croatian defender halted renewal talks on 11 June — a development driven by the confirmed appointment of Jose Mourinho as Real Madrid head coach, who has made Gvardiol a personal priority. City responded by upgrading their contract proposal to 2031, and the club retain strong internal belief that their offer will ultimately be accepted.

The latest reporting from CityXtra (13 June) indicates that both Real Madrid and Barcelona are monitoring the situation but are applying no immediate deadline pressure — both clubs are content to wait for Gvardiol's own decision. A resolution is expected once Croatia's World Cup campaign concludes, likely in late June or early July. Gvardiol's current deal runs until 2028, meaning City have no urgency to sell.

Rodri, meanwhile, is edging closer to signing a contract extension. Multiple reports in May and June indicated City were nearing an agreement, with the Ballon d'Or winner telling TNT Sports he is "very calm" about his future. Real Madrid have not taken any official steps to sign the midfielder, per CityXtra. Locking Rodri in would remove the summer's most significant uncertainty.

The Broader Rebuild

Enzo Maresca inherits a squad that is undeniably in transition. Bernardo Silva and John Stones have both departed as free agents. Savinho is on his way out. Gvardiol's future is unresolved. Against that backdrop, Anderson's arrival — should City get the deal over the line — would represent the most important single act of the summer: a dynamic, press-resistant ball-carrier who can fill the void left by Silva's departure from midfield and give Maresca the engine his system demands.

Sporting director Hugo Viana identified Anderson as the priority. The BBC's Saturday confirmation that Anderson is the choice over Wharton is the clearest signal yet that Viana and Maresca are aligned and that the pursuit will be seen through. Whether Forest ultimately take the guaranteed flat fee above £120 million that City are now prepared to table will define the early weeks of this new era.
