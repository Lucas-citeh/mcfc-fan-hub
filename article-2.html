Romano: City in Initial Talks for World Cup Sensation Bouaddi — Loan-Back Deal Could Unlock €80m Move

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 9 July 2026

Fabrizio Romano has confirmed that Manchester City have held initial talks with Lille over a move for Ayyoub Bouaddi, the 18-year-old Morocco midfielder whose performances at the 2026 FIFA World Cup turned him into one of the most coveted teenagers in European football. Romano says City are "keen on Bouaddi" and are exploring a loan-back structure that would see the player sign for the club this summer but remain at Lille until the start of the 2027/28 season.

Romano Confirms Initial Talks

The transfer expert confirmed the situation on X (formerly Twitter): "Manchester City held initial talks for Ayyoub Bouaddi as potential target for the future. There are 4 clubs involved, PSG not advancing yet having different top priorities. #MCFC keen on Bouaddi with possible plan to let him stay at Lille until start of season 2027/28."

Romano's involvement signals this is a genuine priority rather than speculative interest. City's sporting director Hugo Viana has identified Bouaddi as a cornerstone of the club's long-term midfield rebuild under Enzo Maresca — with Elliot Anderson (confirmed at £116m in July 2026) representing the short-term statement and Bouaddi potentially the next generation alongside him.

Bouaddi's World Cup Breakout

The 18-year-old had already drawn attention in Ligue 1 with Lille, but his performances for Morocco at the 2026 World Cup elevated him into a different conversation entirely. Technically assured on the ball, composed under pressure, and capable of carrying play from deep, Bouaddi drew immediate comparisons to the elite tier of European midfielders a decade his senior.

He joined Lille's academy after emerging through youth football in Belgium, where he was born. His senior Lille debut came in the 2024/25 season and he became a regular starter in 2025/26. Lille's coaching staff have been open about their desire to keep him, but the economics of modern football — and the profile of clubs competing for his signature — make a sale within the next 12 months highly likely.

The Loan-Back Structure

The proposed structure is increasingly common for elite young players from continental clubs. City would agree a fee and contract with Bouaddi now, securing his signature against other suitors, but the player would remain at Lille on loan for the 2026/27 season. This suits all parties: Lille receive a guaranteed sale at a strong valuation; Bouaddi continues to develop in a familiar environment with first-team football; City lock in a generational talent without disrupting their own squad depth in the short term.

Lille's asking price has been reported across various outlets in the €80–100 million range, with the club holding significant leverage given the number of clubs competing. Manchester City, Arsenal, Liverpool and Manchester United are all understood to have registered concrete interest, with PSG having stepped back for now to focus on other priorities per Romano.

From City's perspective, the loan-back model also avoids a potentially awkward situation: Bouaddi, at 18, would need to be eased into Maresca's system carefully regardless. The 2026/27 season at Lille gives him another year of uninterrupted development — by the time he joins City in summer 2027, Maresca's system will be embedded and Anderson will have spent a full season as the heartbeat of the midfield. Bouaddi would slot in as a complementary piece rather than a central figure from day one.

The Competition Problem

Romano's note about "4 clubs involved" illustrates the challenge City face. Arsenal are particularly aggressive: reports suggest sporting director Edu Gaspar has already aligned on Lille's loan-back request and is prepared to meet the €80m+ asking price. Liverpool's technical staff have watched Bouaddi repeatedly this season. Manchester United have reportedly entered the race as well.

City's advantage, beyond their financial muscle, is Enzo Maresca himself. The Italian coach operated in Ligue 1 as a player late in his career and has maintained strong French football contacts through his time managing in the English Football League and Premier League. Hugo Viana's network from his Porto sporting director years also extends into French football, where Lille have strong institutional relationships with City's former director of football.

What Comes Next

Romano's "initial talks" language indicates this is in the early stages — there is no fee agreement, no personal terms agreed, and no formal bid has been lodged. The World Cup is still ongoing (the quarter-finals are on Saturday 11 July), and Bouaddi is likely being monitored further by all competing clubs before major bids follow. The market for a player of this profile only accelerates once the tournament ends and clubs start making definitive decisions.

For City fans, the prospect of a midfield axis of Elliot Anderson and Ayyoub Bouaddi — at £116m and potentially £80m+ respectively — represents exactly the kind of long-term ambition the Viana/Maresca era needs to signal to reassure supporters after Pep Guardiola's departure. Whether City can outmanoeuvre the competition this summer remains to be seen.
