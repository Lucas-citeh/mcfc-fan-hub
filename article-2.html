Blues Around the World: City Send 15-Player Contingent to the 2026 FIFA World Cup

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 1 June 2026

With the 2026 FIFA World Cup kicking off on 11 June across the United States, Canada and Mexico, Manchester City will have their largest-ever presence at a World Cup: 15 first-team players confirmed across 10 nations, representing every corner of the globe. It is a remarkable illustration of how comprehensively City have rebuilt since Pep Guardiola's arrival — and it hands new head coach Enzo Maresca a pre-season starting gun that is later than he might like.

The first 48-team World Cup will run through to the final in New York on 19 July. City's players could be absent for all of pre-season depending on how deep their nations advance. For Maresca, assembling a starting XI for the Community Shield against Arsenal on 16 August will require some creative planning — though he will be careful not to say so publicly while his players are representing their countries.

England: Guehi, O'Reilly and Trafford with the Three Lions

Three City players have been confirmed in Thomas Tuchel's England squad: centre-back Marc Guehi, midfielder Nico O'Reilly and goalkeeper James Trafford. Guehi joined in January 2026 from Crystal Palace and has become a cornerstone of the back four; O'Reilly, 20, earned his place off the back of a breakout campaign in which he scored six goals across all competitions and starred in the FA Cup run. Trafford, City's second-choice goalkeeper, will compete with Jordan Pickford and Dean Henderson for the gloves.

Norway: The Haaland Show

Erling Haaland heads to his first senior World Cup after single-handedly dragging Norway to their first tournament appearance since 1998. The 25-year-old scored 16 goals in Norway's eight qualifying matches and arrives at the tournament with 55 international goals in 49 appearances — already his country's greatest-ever scorer by a distance. Norway are drawn in a competitive group but Haaland alone makes them dangerous against any opponent.

Spain: Rodri's Turn to Shine Internationally

Ballon d'Or winner Rodri is a central part of Spain's squad as they enter the tournament among the favourites. The City midfielder is joined in Spain's engine room by Pedri, Fabián Ruiz, Martín Zubimendi, Gavi and Álex Baena. Spain, as Euro 2024 champions, bring significant pedigree — and Rodri's role as the fulcrum of Luis de la Fuente's side mirrors his importance at club level.

Netherlands: Reijnders Inherits the Creative Burden

Tijjani Reijnders has been confirmed in Ronald Koeman's Netherlands squad. Having joined City from AC Milan in the summer of 2025 and gone on to score five Premier League goals with four assists in his debut season, Reijnders heads to North America as one of the Dutch squad's most important midfield presences. With his City form attracting widespread attention, he will be expected to be a key figure for the Oranje.

Croatia: Gvardiol and Kovacic

Josko Gvardiol and Mateo Kovacic both represent Croatia. Gvardiol, who chipped in with three goals from defence this season, is one of the most complete centre-backs in world football at 24. Kovacic, an experienced international, brings composure and creativity to Zlatko Dalić's midfield.

Belgium: Doku

Jeremy Doku has been named in Rudi Garcia's Belgium 26-man squad. The 24-year-old winger contributed five Premier League goals and eight across all competitions this season. One of the most dangerous dribblers in the Premier League when given licence to run at defenders, Doku will provide Belgium with a different dimension in attack.

Algeria: Ait-Nouri

Left-back Rayan Ait-Nouri was named in Algeria's final World Cup squad — the only Premier League player in their selection. The 24-year-old has been City's first-choice left-back since joining from Wolves last summer, making 30 appearances this season with two goals and five assists.

Egypt: Marmoush

Omar Marmoush joins Egypt's squad having returned to his best in City's colours after some early settling-in difficulties. The January 2025 signing from Eintracht Frankfurt brought seven goals across all competitions for City this term and will be Egypt's focal point in attack.

France: Cherki's First Major Tournament

Rayan Cherki has been included in France's 26-man squad — his first major international tournament as a City player. The Lyon-born playmaker joined City in summer 2025 and despite a mixed debut season at club level (19 appearances, four goals, six assists), has emerged as a creative option for France off the bench. At 21, the World Cup represents a significant step in his international development.

Ghana: Semenyo

FA Cup Final hero Antoine Semenyo represents Ghana after a breakout half-season at City that yielded 22 goals across all competitions. Semenyo's January arrival transformed City's attack and his 72nd-minute winner in the FA Cup Final against Chelsea became one of the images of the 2025/26 campaign. Ghana will count on his directness and finishing at the tournament.

Portugal: Dias and Nunes

Ruben Dias and Matheus Nunes both featured in Portugal's provisional squad for the tournament. Dias, City's captain and defensive rock, brings leadership and experience; Nunes, who has reinvented himself as a reliable right-back and wing-back option under City's system, adds versatility. Bernardo Silva — who departed City as a free agent this summer — also joins them in the Portugal squad.

The Maresca Pre-Season Puzzle

With the World Cup final scheduled for 19 July, City will not have anything close to a full squad available during the early weeks of pre-season. Maresca will likely begin work with younger and fringe players before the bulk of his international stars return through late July and early August — leaving a tight four-to-six-day turnaround before the Community Shield on 16 August in Cardiff.

It is not an ideal preparation window, but it is a shared reality for all top clubs at major tournaments — and for City, having 15 players at a World Cup is a measure of just how far the club's global standing has risen.
