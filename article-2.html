Surgery, Recovery and the Waiting Game: What Happens Next for Rodri at Manchester City

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 20 July 2026

The World Cup celebrations will not last long. The morning after Spain's 1-0 extra-time win over Argentina — a night Rodrigo Hernández will savour for the rest of his life — the attention of Manchester City supporters turns to a more immediate concern: when does their most important player return to the Etihad, and what happens in the meantime?

The Procedure

Reports from multiple outlets in the days leading up to the final confirmed that Rodri is set to undergo surgery on a back injury once his World Cup commitments with Spain end. The procedure has been described as minor in nature, with one report citing a duration of approximately 15 minutes for the surgery itself. That context is important: this is not a reconstruction or a complex intervention of the kind that kept him sidelined for nine months following his ACL injury in September 2024.

Nevertheless, surgery carries inherent uncertainty. The procedure and recovery period are unlikely to be straightforward once the body's response to the operation becomes clearer, and Manchester City's medical staff — among the best in European football — will make no decisions about Rodri's return timeline until they have had the chance to assess him properly at Carrington.

Return Timeline: Two Schools of Thought

On the question of when Rodri will be available to play, there is a notable split in reporting. At one end of the spectrum, CityXtra reported earlier this week that Rodri may miss the start of City's competitive season, with the Manchester derby in mid-September cited as a potential target for his return to the starting lineup. That timeline — effectively ruling him out of the Community Shield (16 August) and the first two or three Premier League fixtures — has given supporters cause for anxiety.

Other outlets have been more optimistic, suggesting the procedure carries a fast recovery and that Rodri could be available much sooner than mid-September — perhaps even for the Community Shield against Arsenal in Cardiff. City have not commented publicly and will not do so until the surgery has been completed and assessed.

The most likely scenario sits somewhere between those two poles. A player who has played 90 minutes in a World Cup Final on 19 July will need time to recover from the tournament even before the back procedure is factored in. Flying back to Manchester, undergoing surgery, and then entering the rehabilitation process means that the pre-season Asia Tour — which opens on 1 August in Hong Kong against Inter Milan — is effectively out of the question.

What City Do Without Him

The answer, in the short term, is: Elliot Anderson. City's club-record £116m signing from Nottingham Forest returned from England's World Cup semi-final campaign last week and, according to CityXtra, is expected to integrate at Carrington ahead of the Community Shield rather than joining the Asia Tour. Anderson was signed precisely because of the depth he provides in central midfield — a box-to-box presence whose progressive carrying, pressing intensity and late runs into the box make him a genuine starter rather than a backup option.

Nico Gonzalez, the composed Spanish deep-lying playmaker signed from Porto in January 2025, also stepped up during Rodri's ACL absence last season and demonstrated that he can perform in the holding role at Premier League level. Mateo Kovacic and the returning Tijjani Reijnders provide further cover, while Maresca — who ran a 4-2-3-1 at Chelsea — may use double pivots that reduce the reliance on any single midfielder to fill Rodri's specific function.

But there is no replacing Rodri when he is at full fitness. The Ballon d'Or winner and newly crowned World Cup champion is, by common consensus, the best defensive midfielder on the planet. Maresca knows this; his squad-building decisions this summer have been designed in part to allow City to carry Rodri's absence during the early weeks of the season without losing significant ground at the top of the table.

The Bigger Picture

City's Premier League opener is Sunday 23 August at home to Bournemouth. If Rodri is to be available for that game, surgery and recovery would need to proceed on the more optimistic timeline — and City's medical team would need to clear him for contact training within approximately three weeks of the operation. It is possible. It is not guaranteed.

What is guaranteed is that Rodri returns to Carrington as a World Cup winner — the last piece of major international silverware that had eluded him in a decorated career. He will undergo surgery, recover, and walk back through the doors at the City Football Academy in due course. The question of exactly when that happens will be answered one step at a time. Blue Moonies, for now, can simply enjoy the fact that their man has conquered the world.
