City Confirm Monga Signing — 17-year-old Leicester Winger Joins on Five-Year Deal

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 11 July 2026

Manchester City have officially confirmed the signing of Jeremy Monga from Leicester City, with the 17-year-old winger penning a five-year contract running to summer 2031. The fee is reported at £10 million, rising to £12.5m with add-ons. The announcement came one day after Monga turned 17, with City seeing off competition from Arsenal — who had submitted an opening bid that was rejected — to land one of the most coveted teenagers in English football.

The Signing Confirmed

Manchester City confirmed the deal on mancity.com, describing Monga as an "exciting" addition to Enzo Maresca's squad. Sky Sports simultaneously confirmed the £10m deal as complete, with the winger signing until 2031. Fabrizio Romano had earlier issued his "here we go" confirmation on X, indicating personal terms between the player and the club were already agreed and the deal was entering its final administrative stages.

It is City's fourth confirmed arrival of the summer, following French forward Mathys Detourbet (€25m from Troyes, on loan to Monaco), goalkeeper Pierce Charles (£10m from Sheffield Wednesday, on loan to QPR), and England midfielder Elliot Anderson (club-record £116m from Nottingham Forest). The timing — just after Monga's birthday — is significant: under Football Association rules, professional contracts cannot be signed by players under 17, meaning City had been waiting for the date to finalise the deal.

Who is Jeremy Monga?

Jeremy Monga is a direct, pacey winger who joined Leicester City's academy at the age of eight and broke into the first team during their 2025/26 Championship campaign. He made 27 appearances for the Foxes in the second tier — a remarkable number for a teenager — and contributed one goal and two assists. His ability to beat defenders in tight areas and carry the ball at pace drew comparisons to some of the Premier League's most dynamic wide players.

His youth development was conducted entirely in England, and he impressed for Leicester's U18 and U21 sides before graduating to senior minutes. At just 17, he already has the physical profile and mental composure to handle high-level football — exactly the attributes that caught City's attention ahead of the 2026/27 rebuild under Enzo Maresca.

How City Saw Off Arsenal

Arsenal were first to move publicly, submitting an opening bid that Leicester rejected. City then entered negotiations and moved quickly. According to ESPN, Hugo Viana — City's sporting director — personally prioritised the deal, seeing Monga as a long-term piece of the attacking unit that Maresca is constructing. City's track record of developing young wingers through their first-team environment proved a persuasive element of the pitch to Monga and his representatives.

Monga's willingness to sign for City over a Champions League-calibre club like Arsenal is significant. It suggests a belief that the Maresca era — combined with the strength of City's squad infrastructure, training facilities, and the presence of established wide players to learn from — represents the right environment for his development.

Where Does He Fit?

At 17, Monga joins a forward line that includes Erling Haaland, Jeremy Doku, Antoine Semenyo, Omar Marmoush, and the departing Savinho (whose move to Tottenham is unconfirmed but widely anticipated). He is unlikely to be a regular starter in 2026/27 but will be integrated into the first-team squad and expected to contribute off the bench and in cup competitions.

Unlike the club's other summer arrivals — Detourbet and Charles who are both immediately out on loan — Monga stays at the Etihad. This is a deliberate decision: Maresca wants the teenager in and around the first team from day one, absorbing the style of play and developing in the highest-possible training environment. The expectation is that Monga will emerge as a starter within two to three seasons.

City's Summer is Taking Shape

With Monga in, City's summer business represents a significant generational shift. The departures of Bernardo Silva, John Stones, Nathan Aké, Pep Guardiola, and Manuel Akanji have created real space — financially and in terms of squad dynamics — for Viana and Maresca to rebuild on their own terms. The Elliot Anderson deal signalled ambition; Monga signals eye for the future.

Further business is expected. Savinho's future remains unresolved, and City's attacking depth — while growing — may require at least one more senior arrival before the season opener against Bournemouth on 23 August. But for now, Monga's signing is a statement of intent: that the club is willing to invest heavily and quickly to secure England's best young talent before their rivals can.
