Growing Confidence: City Ready Second Mega-Money Bid for Anderson as Move Edges Closer

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 7 June 2026

Manchester City are preparing a second, substantially improved bid for Nottingham Forest's Elliot Anderson, with multiple outlets now reporting "growing confidence" that a deal will be concluded before England's 2026 World Cup opener against Croatia on 17 June. Sky Sports have confirmed City remain in pole position for Anderson's signature despite Forest's firm stance on their valuation.

City's opening formal bid — submitted before the Premier League transfer window opened on 15 June — was swiftly rejected by Forest, who are understood to be holding out for a figure in the region of £100–105 million for the 23-year-old England international. The rejection was confirmed by Sky Sports and The Athletic's David Ornstein. Rather than cooling their interest, City's response has been to prepare a second, significantly larger offer as the clubs move toward bridging the gap on the fee.

The Second Bid: What's Being Reported

Reports citing sources close to the deal describe a "massive" improved bid being readied by City's football operations team. Forest's £100m+ asking price, while steep, reflects the midfielder's status as one of the Premier League's standout players in 2025/26 and the fact that his contract runs until 2028, removing any financial urgency on their part to sell cheaply.

Sky Sports have been consistent throughout the process: Anderson is Manchester City's number-one midfield transfer priority, ahead of Plan B option Sandro Tonali of Newcastle United. That hierarchy has not changed, and City's willingness to return with an improved offer underlines how central Anderson is to Enzo Maresca's rebuild plans.

Anderson himself has already signalled his position clearly: the 23-year-old has agreed personal terms with City in principle — a reported five-year deal — and is keen on the Etihad project under Maresca, who shaped Chelsea's tactical identity before taking the City role. The player's preference for City is understood to have been a factor in Manchester United largely stepping back from the race, with INEOS focused elsewhere in the market.

The World Cup Deadline

Both the player and club have a shared incentive to resolve the situation quickly. Anderson is part of Thomas Tuchel's England squad for the 2026 World Cup, with the Three Lions opening their campaign against Croatia on 17 June. Anderson has expressed a desire to have his club future settled before the tournament begins in earnest, allowing him to focus entirely on the competition without a transfer saga running in the background.

City are pushing hard to exploit that window. The transfer window officially opened on 15 June, giving the clubs a narrow slot of a few days in which a deal could be announced before England's opening match — though it remains possible, and perhaps likely, that the formal agreement comes while Anderson is at the World Cup itself, with paperwork completed once the tournament concludes.

Forest's Position: Firm, But Not Intractable

Nottingham Forest are not under compulsion to sell. They finished inside the top half of the Premier League in 2025/26 with European football secured, and Anderson's contract situation gives them leverage. Their rejection of the opening bid was a firm statement of intent: they will not accept a discount because a buyer is eager.

However, sources suggest City and Forest are closer in principle than the "rejected bid" framing implies. The two clubs know where each other stands. City are showing willingness to approach Forest's valuation, and the growing confidence reported around the deal suggests both parties believe a number exists at which this can be done. It may require City to structure the deal creatively — add-ons, performance bonuses, sell-on clauses — to bridge the gap between their offer and Forest's asking price without triggering the full headline fee upfront.

What Anderson Brings to Maresca's City

The strategic rationale for City is clear. Bernardo Silva's departure as a free agent this summer left a significant void: a midfielder capable of carrying the ball forward dynamically, progressing through lines with the ball at pace, and contributing in transition. Tijjani Reijnders, Mateo Kovacic, Nico Gonzalez and Nico O'Reilly are all capable midfielders, but none offers quite the vertical dynamism Anderson's game provides.

For Enzo Maresca — who built Chelsea's attack around progressive ball-carriers and runners from deep — Anderson is close to an ideal type. His pressing ability, his shooting from range, and his capacity to turn defence into attack in two or three touches align precisely with what a Maresca team looks like at full power. At 23, he also fits the age profile of City's rebuild: a player who will still be at the peak of his powers when the current generation of Haaland, Foden, Cherki and Reijnders are between 26 and 30.

What Comes Next

The coming days are critical. If City submit an improved bid that Forest accept — or one that kickstarts serious counter-negotiations — the deal could be agreed rapidly given that personal terms are already substantially in place. Failing that, the negotiation will extend into the World Cup period, with both clubs aware that City's ability to pivot to Plan B (Tonali, per Romano) creates a soft deadline for Forest to consider.

For City fans, the direction of travel on this story has shifted meaningfully in 48 hours: from an initial rejection and uncertainty to "growing confidence" and an improved bid incoming. That is the right arc for a deal to follow. The next update is likely to be the one that matters.
