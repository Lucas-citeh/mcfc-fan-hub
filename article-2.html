Trafford Departs for Leeds as Rodri Switches Target to Barcelona

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 7 August 2026

Thursday, 7 August brought two seismic transfer developments for Manchester City — one closed chapter and one stunning reversal. Mancity.com officially confirmed James Trafford's permanent move to Leeds United for a fee that could reach £45 million, making him the most expensive British goalkeeper in history. In a separate story that is still unfolding, Rodri has turned away from Real Madrid and given FC Barcelona the green light to negotiate with City, per multiple tier-1 sources.

Trafford Confirmed — A Proper Send-Off for a Homegrown Keeper

Manchester City and Leeds United jointly announced the transfer of James Trafford on Thursday morning, ending weeks of speculation that had stretched back to late July when Fabrizio Romano issued his "here we go" on the deal. Trafford has signed a five-year contract at Elland Road, and the guaranteed fee stands at £40 million, rising to £45 million with performance-related add-ons — a new record for a British goalkeeper, surpassing the £30 million Everton paid for Jordan Pickford in 2017.

City negotiated a 20% sell-on clause and matching rights as part of the agreement — standard protections for a homegrown player with potentially significant future value. Trafford's FIFA-mandated post-World Cup break, which was the final regulatory barrier to completing the paperwork, ended on 8 August; the deal was processed the day before.

Trafford made 17 appearances for City across all competitions in 2025/26, winning the FA Cup and EFL Cup during a season in which he was the established number two behind Gianluigi Donnarumma. He returned to the Etihad from a loan spell at Burnley in the summer of 2025 fully aware that Donnarumma's arrival made regular first-team minutes difficult to come by at City. A move for consistent playing time was the right outcome for all parties.

"James is a fantastic goalkeeper and we hope he enjoys a long and successful career at Leeds," the club's official statement noted. He leaves with two domestic cup winners' medals and the respect of everyone at the Etihad.

Rulli — The Replacement is Almost There

City's moves to fill the vacancy have been progressing in parallel. Manchester City and Olympique de Marseille have now reached an agreement in principle for the transfer of Gerónimo Rulli, per Get French Football News, Yahoo Sports and The Hard Tackle. Only minor details relating to agent fees are said to remain outstanding before the deal can be made official.

The Argentine goalkeeper — who has a curious prior connection to City, having been signed from Deportivo Maldonado in 2016 before spending his entire tenure at the club on loan and never making a senior appearance — agreed personal terms on a two-year deal at the Etihad earlier this month. He brings 34-year-old composure, Champions League experience and the hunger of a player motivated to re-enter the Premier League. At a fee of approximately €2–3.5 million, he represents exceptional value as Donnarumma's understudy. No official mancity.com announcement has been made as of 7 August 2026, but the deal is expected to be confirmed in the coming days.

Rodri's Stunning Reversal — Barcelona the New Destination

The second major story of the day was the most unexpected. For weeks, the Rodri transfer saga had appeared to be heading in one direction: towards Real Madrid. The Ballon d'Or holder had agreed personal terms with Madrid; Real Madrid had submitted their first formal club-to-club offer on 6 August; and all roads seemed to lead to the Bernabéu.

That changed on Thursday. Per ESPN, Sky Sports, Yahoo Sports, beIN Sports and Forbes (citing Spanish outlet Jijantes), Rodri has grown tired of the fee gap between Real Madrid and Manchester City — with City asking around €80 million and Madrid unwilling to go beyond €55–60 million — and has now given FC Barcelona permission to negotiate directly with City. Economic terms between Rodri and Barcelona are reportedly agreed, with the Spanish international set to sign a four-year deal. A salary figure of approximately €30 million per year gross has been reported by Yahoo Sports.

The shift marks a significant reversal from just weeks ago, when Tribuna.com reported Rodri had rejected approaches from both Barcelona and PSG to hold out for Real Madrid. The protracted nature of the fee negotiations appears to have changed his thinking. Forbes, citing Jijantes — the Spanish outlet with close sources to Rodri's camp — went further, stating that Rodri has agreed to join FC Barcelona.

Manchester City are expected to receive a fee in the €60–70 million range from Barcelona — a slight compromise on their stated €80 million valuation, but a meaningful return on a player entering the final year of his contract who they cannot afford to lose for nothing in summer 2027. No official announcement from Manchester City, FC Barcelona or Real Madrid had been made as of 7 August 2026.

What It Means for Enzo Maresca's City

Should Rodri complete a move to Barcelona before the window closes on 1 September, Enzo Maresca will begin his Premier League debut season without the player many regard as the world's best midfielder. It is an enormous absence — Rodri has won six Premier League titles and one Champions League at the Etihad, and his disciplined, metronomic presence at the base of midfield has been the spine of the most successful period in City's history.

City's response to his potential departure has been proactive. Elliot Anderson, signed from Nottingham Forest for a club-record £116 million, is a natural box-to-box presence who can fill part of the void. Nico Gonzalez offers a disciplined holding option. And the pursuit of Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille — a deal that remains in progress — is clearly framed as the long-term Rodri successor, even if a loan-back to Lille may delay his Etihad arrival by a year.

The Community Shield against Arsenal on 16 August now takes on added significance as the first real examination of how Maresca's City will look without their talisman. Rodri is in any case sidelined by back surgery until at least mid-September, so the opening weeks of the season would always have been contested without him. The question is whether those opening weeks stretch into a season-long adjustment.
