Opening Bid Rejected: City Make Their Move for Elliot Anderson as Nottingham Forest Stand Firm

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 4 June 2026

Manchester City have formally entered the bidding for Elliot Anderson by submitting an opening offer to Nottingham Forest — which the club swiftly rejected. The development, reported by The Athletic's David Ornstein and Laurie Whitwell on 3 June, represents the most concrete step yet in City's summer pursuit of the England midfielder and confirms Anderson as Enzo Maresca's number-one midfield target.

Sky Sports News separately confirmed on 3–4 June that Anderson remains Manchester City's priority signing in the central midfield position, despite the rejection. With the Premier League transfer window opening on 15 June and the 2026/27 fixture list due to drop on 19 June, City are expected to return with an improved offer once the window is formally open.

The Bid and the Rejection

The precise fee in City's opening offer has not been disclosed. However, the landscape for central midfielders at Anderson's level is well established: Moises Caicedo (£115m), Enzo Fernandez (£106m) and Declan Rice (£105m) have all set benchmarks north of £100 million in recent windows. Nottingham Forest are believed to be holding out for a figure in that range, or close to it, and their rejection signals they will not be moved cheaply — even by a club of City's standing.

Forest are under no financial compulsion to sell. The club have secured their top-flight status, have Anderson contracted until 2028, and are operating from a position of strength. Their message to City, in refusing the opening bid, is that they will drive the hardest possible deal if Anderson is to leave the City Ground this summer.

Anderson: City's Primary Target

Elliot Anderson, 23, has been the name most consistently linked with City's midfield rebuild since Bernardo Silva's departure as a free agent at the end of his contract. The Newcastle-born midfielder — still just 23 — established himself as one of the most dynamic central midfielders in the Premier League during his time at Forest, combining high energy, pressing ability, and an eye for goal that City's engine room has been missing.

For Enzo Maresca, whose system at Chelsea and in his managerial career has relied on midfielders who can carry the ball forward and operate at pace in transitions, Anderson represents a near-ideal fit. His ability to pick the ball up deep and drive through the thirds aligns precisely with what the new City head coach is expected to demand. Reports from Nicolo Schira indicated in late May that Anderson had reached a verbal agreement with City on personal terms in principle — a five-year deal — suggesting the player himself is fully committed to the Etihad project if the clubs can agree a fee.

The World Cup Complication

Both parties face a ticking clock. Anderson is part of Thomas Tuchel's England squad for the 2026 World Cup, with the tournament kicking off on 11 June across the United States, Canada and Mexico. England's opening group game falls in the second week of the competition, meaning Anderson is expected to depart for the pre-tournament camp imminently. That narrows any pre-window negotiating window to just a matter of days.

City and Forest are unlikely to reach a formal agreement before the window opens on 15 June. However, the opening of formal negotiations — bid made, bid rejected — means both clubs know where they stand. The groundwork is laid; the second act begins on 15 June.

Competition From United and Arsenal

Manchester City are not alone in pursuing Anderson. Manchester United and Arsenal have both been credited with serious interest, with Sky Sports reporting City in pole position despite those clubs' presence. The fact that City moved to submit a bid before the window opened — absorbing the expected rejection in the process — suggests they are determined to secure the deal quickly and not allow a bidding war to develop in July, when pre-season preparations are under way and World Cup returnees are trickling back.

Arsenal's own interest in Anderson is reportedly tied to whether they can move other midfield targets first; United's pursuit has been characterised as less advanced. City's edge comes partly from the player himself: having agreed personal terms in principle, Anderson understands what Maresca's City project entails and appears aligned with it.

The Bigger Midfield Picture

Landing Anderson would go a long way toward addressing the most pressing area of City's squad after a summer that has already seen Bernardo Silva and John Stones depart, with Savinho potentially to follow. Rodri, Phil Foden, Tijjani Reijnders, Mateo Kovacic, Nico Gonzalez, Nico O'Reilly and Rayan Cherki provide a core — but the dynamic, progressive carrying-midfielder slot that Silva occupied for years has been left conspicuously unfilled. Anderson, City believe, is the man to fill it.

The rejected opening bid is not the end of the story. It is, by every measure, the beginning of it.
