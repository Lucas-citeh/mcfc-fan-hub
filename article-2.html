City's World Cup Watch: Haaland Faces Brazil, Anderson Faces Mexico at the Azteca

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 3 July 2026

Two Manchester City players remain alive in the 2026 FIFA World Cup heading into a decisive weekend. Erling Haaland and Norway face five-time world champions Brazil at MetLife Stadium on Sunday 5 July; Elliot Anderson and England face Mexico at the iconic Estadio Azteca on Monday 6 July (1am BST, 7 July). For Enzo Maresca and his pre-season planning, the clock is ticking.

Haaland's Norway vs Brazil — Sunday 5 July, MetLife Stadium (9pm BST)

Norway arrived at this Round of 16 tie in extraordinary fashion. Haaland's 86th-minute tap-in against Ivory Coast on 30 June — his fifth goal of the tournament — sealed a historic 2-1 win: Norway's first-ever World Cup knockout victory. The celebration that followed, Haaland sprinting to the corner flag, arms outstretched, told its own story about how much this moment meant to a generation of Norwegian football fans who spent years watching their country miss every major tournament.

Now comes the hardest possible test. Brazil reached the Round of 16 by defeating Japan and arrive at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey as heavy favourites. The Seleção have a front line capable of cutting through any defence on their day. Their centre-backs — experienced, physical, well-drilled in dealing with direct strikers — will provide the toughest examination of Haaland's tournament so far.

Norway's approach will almost certainly involve compacting defensively, pressing high when opportunity presents, and looking to counter through Haaland's hold-up play and Antonio Nusa's pace on the break. It worked against Ivory Coast. Whether it works against Brazil is another question entirely. Kickoff is 4pm ET / 9pm BST on Sunday 5 July.

Anderson's England vs Mexico — Monday 6 July, Estadio Azteca (1am BST, 7 July)

England's route to the Round of 16 was not straightforward, but they got there. Harry Kane scored twice in a 2-1 comeback win over DR Congo on 1 July, and Thomas Tuchel's side now face the hosts, Mexico, at the most storied stadium in World Cup history.

The Estadio Azteca sits in Mexico City at an altitude of approximately 2,240 metres above sea level. Tuchel was candid in his pre-match briefing: the altitude is "impossible" to fully adapt to in the time available, and England's preparation has focused instead on minimising its impact through hydration protocols and pacing. Mexico, playing at home and acclimatised to the conditions, hold a significant environmental advantage.

Anderson is expected to be part of Tuchel's plans. The 23-year-old — now officially a Manchester City player following the completion of his club-record £116m transfer — has been involved throughout England's World Cup campaign and enters Monday's match in form. Should England eliminate Mexico and progress into a quarter-final around 10–11 July, his return to Manchester for pre-season will be further delayed.

What This Means for Maresca's Pre-Season

Enzo Maresca's first pre-season as Manchester City head coach begins in mid-July. The Community Shield against Arsenal is on 16 August in Cardiff; the Premier League opener against Bournemouth follows on 23 August. Every World Cup game Haaland or Anderson plays is time they are not at the Etihad absorbing the new manager's methods, building chemistry with new teammates, or integrating into Maresca's system.

If both Norway and England are eliminated this weekend, Maresca could have Haaland and Anderson available from approximately 8–10 July — a meaningful pre-season window. If either or both progress deeper into the tournament, quarter-finals fall around 10–11 July and semi-finals around 14–15 July. A late run from Norway in particular would bring Haaland back barely a fortnight before the Community Shield.

City have managed similar situations before and will have contingency plans in place. But in a year where a new manager is installing a new system with an almost entirely rebuilt squad, the benefit of having their two most prominent attacking players from the start of pre-season cannot be overstated. Sunday and Monday will shape the next chapter of Manchester City's summer.
