PSG and Barcelona Enter Rodri Race — But Ballon d'Or Winner Reportedly Eyes Only Real Madrid

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 1 August 2026

The Rodri transfer saga has entered a new, more complicated phase. As Manchester City took to the pitch at Kai Tak Stadium in Hong Kong for Enzo Maresca's first game in charge, the club simultaneously fielded interest in their most decorated midfielder from two of Europe's biggest clubs. Paris Saint-Germain have made contact with City to explore the conditions under which a Rodri deal could be structured, while Barcelona have also entered the picture — per multiple reports. But the story's most significant detail may be what Rodri himself reportedly wants: Real Madrid, and only Real Madrid.

PSG Enter the Picture

PSG's approach, reported by CityXtra and cited by Yahoo Sports, represents a significant new development in a saga that had previously focused exclusively on a Madrid destination. The French champions contacted Manchester City to understand the framework of any potential deal — not yet a formal bid, but a clear expression of interest from a club with the financial muscle to close any gap that Real Madrid cannot or will not bridge.

PSG's motivation is straightforward. They spent much of 2025/26 investing heavily in their squad following further Champions League underperformance, and Rodri — the Ballon d'Or winner, Spain's World Cup captain, and one of the finest deep midfielders of his generation — represents the kind of transformative signing that would shift their ambitions. City's reported willingness to listen to approaches at a fee above €80 million makes the timing politically convenient for PSG, who can broadly match that figure.

Barcelona's Interest Linked to De Jong Injury

Barcelona's emergence in the Rodri race has a specific trigger: Frenkie de Jong suffered a medial collateral ligament injury in his right knee during the World Cup, leaving the Dutch midfielder facing a significant period on the sidelines. With their midfield options diminished, Barcelona's sporting directors reportedly reached out to Rodri's representatives to gauge interest, per Tribuna.com.

The Catalan club's financial situation remains complex, which makes a deal at the figures City are demanding — €80 million-plus — difficult to structure without creative accounting or player exchange proposals. They are a monitoring presence rather than a frontrunner, but their entry into the picture adds pressure on all parties to resolve matters sooner rather than later.

Rodri's Reported Position: Only Real Madrid

The most striking element of Friday's reports is Rodri's own stance. Per Tribuna.com, the World Cup Golden Ball winner has rejected approaches from PSG and Barcelona and has no interest in moving to either club. He is understood to want to return to Spain — and specifically to Real Madrid, the club he has long been linked to as a potential long-term home. There is a clear personal logic: Rodri is from Villarreal, spent his career in the Spanish league before joining City, and has publicly spoken warmly of Ligue 1 and La Liga in his interviews. Madrid is the football institution that fits the narrative of his career's next chapter.

That clarity of preference gives Real Madrid significant leverage in their ongoing fee negotiations with City. If Rodri will only go to the Bernabéu, Madrid know they face no meaningful competition — and City know that holding out for €80 million without a willing buyer at that price may mean Rodri simply runs down his contract (which expires in summer 2027) and leaves for free next year.

The Fee Gap: The Pivotal Question

As of 1 August 2026, the numbers remain as follows: Manchester City are asking for approximately €80 million or above for a player who is in the final year of his contract. Real Madrid, per Managing Madrid, are willing to pay in the region of €50–60 million — a gap of €20–30 million depending on the source. An "agreement" on personal terms between Rodri and Real Madrid has been reported by Football365, meaning the player's wages and contract length are effectively settled in principle. The final obstacle is the club-to-club fee.

The pressure on City to accept a lower figure is real. Holding a player who has given his green light to leave for over a year and allowing him to walk for free in 2027 — as happened with Bernardo Silva — would represent a significant failure of transfer strategy. But City's counter-argument is that Rodri, even at 30 and post-surgery, is a generational talent whose replacement value far exceeds €60 million. Completing the Ayyoub Bouaddi deal from Lille — understood to be in its final stages at around £86 million — would remove the most obvious obstacle to accepting Madrid's approach.

The Back Surgery Factor

Context that complicates the timeline: Rodri underwent back surgery in Madrid on Monday 28 July, with his recovery estimated at four to six weeks. That timeline means he would be unavailable until mid-to-late September at the earliest, adding a degree of urgency for whichever club signs him — they will not have their new midfielder available immediately. For Real Madrid, that is manageable; for City, it changes nothing about their negotiating position.

What Happens Next

The signals from City's side remain consistent: they want €80 million and will not be rushed. Real Madrid hold the player's preference. PSG and Barcelona are spectators unless Rodri changes his mind — which all current reporting suggests he will not. The most likely path to resolution is a fee compromise somewhere between €65 million and €75 million, brokered over the next two to three weeks as the transfer deadline approaches. Whether that compromise can be reached in time for Rodri to miss only the opening Premier League fixtures — or whether this saga drags into September — is the question that defines City's entire summer window.
