City Close in on Bouaddi: Agreement Reached with Lille as Medical Imminent

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 26 July 2026

Manchester City are on the verge of signing Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille. A full agreement has been reached with both the player on personal terms — a contract running to June 2031 — and with the French club on a fee in the region of £85m (€100m). David Ornstein of The Athletic reported on 25 July 2026 that the medical is now imminent. No official announcement has been made by Manchester City, but multiple sources describe the deal as days away from completion.

Who Is Ayyoub Bouaddi?

Bouaddi is an 18-year-old central midfielder from Morocco who came through the academy system at Lille, where he has been since the age of twelve. He established himself in Lille's first team during the 2025/26 season and became one of the standout young players of the summer, featuring for Morocco at the 2026 FIFA World Cup — a tournament held across the United States, Canada and Mexico — where his performances against much older and more experienced opponents drew widespread attention from clubs across Europe.

He combines technical precision with an advanced physical profile for his age. Tactically, he reads transitions quickly, can receive and redistribute under pressure in tight spaces, and contributes to both defensive and offensive phases. Those who watched him throughout the World Cup noted his ability to dictate tempo — a hallmark of the type of midfielder Enzo Maresca has always valued most highly.

The Fee and Personal Terms

Lille's valuation of Bouaddi was understood to be in the region of €100m (£85m) — a significant figure for a player who turns 19 later in 2026, but one that reflects both his status as one of the most coveted midfielders in European football at his age and the competitive market for talent following the World Cup.

City and Lille reached an agreement on that figure, according to reports on 25 July 2026. Personal terms — a contract to June 2031, covering five years — were agreed with the player in parallel. The athletic reported a medical is now imminent, with the deal described by multiple outlets as close to being formally completed. No clause for a buy-back or mandatory purchase was reported; this appears to be a straightforward permanent transfer.

City's Plan: First Team, Not a Loan

A key detail that distinguished City's bid from some earlier reports was the club's intention regarding Bouaddi's immediate future. Manchester City informed Bouaddi and his representatives that they want him to go directly into Enzo Maresca's first-team squad for the 2026/27 season — not to go out on loan. This was a meaningful signal. Some rival clubs had discussed offering him senior football immediately via a loan arrangement; City's position was that the Etihad itself could provide that environment.

That framing will carry weight with Bouaddi and his advisers. The argument being made is that training alongside Erling Haaland, Phil Foden, Elliot Anderson and Rodri — when the latter returns from surgery — provides a level of development that few loan clubs could match. Maresca's tactical approach is also well-suited to technically gifted midfielders: at Leicester and Chelsea, he consistently found ways to use players in his mould in central positions.

The Competition: Arsenal's Pursuit Fell Short

Arsenal were the most serious rival for Bouaddi's signature. The Gunners' interest was genuine and sustained — they valued him highly and made a competitive proposal. But as the summer progressed, City emerged as the clear frontrunner. Reports in recent weeks indicated that Bouaddi's preference tilted towards the Etihad once City's offer took shape — the combination of fee that satisfied Lille, personal terms that satisfied the player, and the prospect of direct integration into a title-challenging squad all combined to make the decision relatively straightforward by the end.

What It Means for City's Midfield

If confirmed, Bouaddi would be City's fifth senior summer signing — after Enzo Maresca (head coach), Mathys Detourbet, Elliot Anderson and Jeremy Monga — and their second record-level outlay this window. He arrives into a midfield that has been reshaped significantly: Bernardo Silva's departure to Real Madrid removed the key creative link from the previous era; John Stones, who often played as a midfielder under Guardiola, has also left. Anderson provides progressive carries and energy from box-to-box. Bouaddi, in a more structured role, would add a different dimension — one that sits closer to the axis Maresca prefers his teams to play through.

Rodri's absence through the early months of the season — back surgery on 28 July means he will miss the Asia Tour, the Community Shield, and likely the opening Premier League fixtures — makes this signing more urgent than it might otherwise seem. Nico Gonzalez and Mateo Kovacic provide experienced options, but neither is the kind of statement-level signing that Maresca needs to build the new midfield identity around. If Bouaddi's deal is formally confirmed in the coming days, City's rebuild in the centre of the park will have taken a decisive step forward.
