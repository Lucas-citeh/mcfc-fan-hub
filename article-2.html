Reijnders Officially Departs for Saudi Arabia as City's Midfield Overhaul Accelerates

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 19 August 2026

In the space of 24 hours, Manchester City have officially lost both of their senior central midfielders. Rodri's move to FC Barcelona was confirmed on 18 August; Tijjani Reijnders' departure to Al Qadsiah in the Saudi Pro League was made official on 19 August, with the Dutch midfielder agreeing a five-year deal under Brendan Rodgers. The fee is €61 million — roughly £52 million — for a player who had been at the Etihad for barely a year. The combined outgoings of Rodri and Reijnders in a single 24-hour window represent one of the most dramatic midfield clearances any Premier League club has undertaken in the modern era. And Manchester City's Premier League opener is on Sunday.

One Year, One Season, One Exit

Tijjani Reijnders joined Manchester City in the summer of 2025 from AC Milan in a deal worth approximately £46.5 million. It was a statement signing — a box-to-box midfielder who had flourished under Stefano Pioli, a player with physicality, technical quality and an eye for goal that made him one of Serie A's most complete midfielders. He delivered on that promise in his debut season: 47 appearances across all competitions, seven goals, two assists, and two domestic cup-winners' medals as City claimed the FA Cup and EFL Cup double under Pep Guardiola's final season in charge.

But Reijnders was not Rodri. Nobody in the squad was Rodri. And when Fabrizio Romano confirmed the "here we go" on 13 August — Reijnders set to leave City for Al Qadsiah, "clubs exchanging documents," the Dutch international not reporting for training as he waited to fly to Saudi Arabia — the football world did a double take. A player who had just won two trophies and made 47 appearances in year one, departing before year two had even begun. The money, at €61 million, represents a near-30 percent profit on the original purchase price. For City's financial planning, the equation makes sense. For Enzo Maresca's midfield, it arrives like a thunderbolt.

The Saudi Pull

Al Qadsiah, based in Al Khobar on Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province coast, finished fourth in the Saudi Pro League last season. They are coached by Brendan Rodgers — the former Liverpool, Celtic and Leicester manager who has taken over a project that has the financial muscle of the Public Investment Fund behind it. Reijnders' five-year deal reportedly makes him one of the Saudi league's highest earners. At 27, an age when most elite midfielders are entering their peak years, the decision is eyebrow-raising by European standards but has become increasingly unremarkable in the context of the kingdom's sustained recruitment of Premier League talent.

Multiple sources confirmed the deal on 19 August, a day after Yahoo Sports and the Washington Post had both reported City selling Reijnders to a Saudi club following Rodri's exit. Dailysports declared it official. The full transfer documentation was completed without fanfare — almost as if both club and player had simply decided the time was right, signed the paperwork, and moved on.

Forty-Eight Hours That Rewrote the Midfield

To contextualise the scale of the change: on the morning of 17 August, Rodri and Reijnders were still notionally Manchester City players. By the evening of 19 August, neither was. The Rodri saga had dragged on throughout the summer — back surgery, fee negotiations with Real Madrid and then Barcelona, Romano's "here we go" on Community Shield evening, and finally the confirmation of his arrival in Catalonia on 18 August, where he hailed Barcelona's football values and described leaving City as "a tough decision." Reijnders, by contrast, went quietly: no drawn-out speculation, no public farewell announcement, just a "here we go" from Romano on 13 August and an official confirmation six days later.

What remains of Enzo Maresca's available central midfield as of 20 August: Mateo Kovacic (Croatia, 32), Nico Gonzalez (Spain, 24), Elliot Anderson (England, 22), Rayan Cherki (France, 20 — deployed primarily as an attacking midfielder), Phil Foden (England, 26 — more forward-facing), and Rico Lewis (England, 20 — primarily a defensive utility player). That is not nothing. But it is emphatically not what City had six weeks ago.

The Bouaddi Imperative

Manchester City's pursuit of Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille has been running since late July and now carries an urgency that can no longer be described as a preference. The 18-year-old Moroccan international — one of Ligue 1's most exciting prospects, and a player who represented Morocco at the 2026 World Cup — has had personal terms agreed with City for weeks, per multiple sources. The fee negotiations between City and Lille have centred on a gap between Lille's asking price (around €100 million) and City's highest offer (approximately €90 million plus bonuses).

As of 20 August, that gap has not officially closed — and no announcement has been received from mancity.com or Lille. But Yahoo Sports reported on 20 August that Manchester City are "confident" of landing Bouaddi in a deal worth approximately £85 million ($115 million). Lille manager Davide Ancelotti publicly acknowledged on 17 August that Bouaddi could leave for the Etihad — a significant shift from the Lille camp's previously guarded position. With Rodri and Reijnders both gone, the slot Bouaddi is intended to fill is not merely empty: it is conspicuously, urgently vacant.

Newcastle Eye Nico Gonzalez

Against this backdrop, a further complication emerged on 18–19 August. Newcastle United have opened talks with Manchester City regarding Nico Gonzalez, the Spanish midfielder who joined from Porto in January 2025. Per Yahoo Sports, OneFootball, Football Today and Read Man City, Newcastle are seeking midfield reinforcements following the departures of both Sandro Tonali and Bruno Guimaraes from St James' Park this summer, and Gonzalez — described in some quarters as a "mini Rodri" for his positional play and pressing — has been identified as a potential answer. City are reportedly open to selling, though no fee or personal terms have been disclosed, and no official announcement has been made.

Whether Gonzalez departs or not will depend in part on how quickly the Bouaddi deal is concluded. If the Moroccan signs this week, City have cover. If Bouaddi is delayed — or if the deal collapses — losing a third central midfielder before Sunday's Premier League opener would be extraordinary. At the time of writing, Nico Gonzalez remains a Manchester City player and Newcastle's interest remains at the enquiry stage.

Sunday at the Etihad

Bournemouth arrive at the Etihad Stadium on Sunday 23 August for the opening fixture of the 2026/27 Premier League season. Enzo Maresca's first competitive home game as City's head coach. For most managers taking charge of a new job, the opening weekend brings nerves and anticipation in roughly equal measure. For Maresca, it brings those things and a midfield that has been comprehensively restructured in the final days of pre-season through a combination of departures he did not initiate and signings that have not yet all arrived. The Etihad will fill; the blue shirts will take the pitch; Haaland, Foden, Doku and Semenyo will be there. Mateo Kovacic will start in a position of considerable responsibility. And somewhere in Maresca's technical area, there will be the quiet, persistent awareness that two of the players who would have been most central to his plans are now in Barcelona and Al Khobar.
