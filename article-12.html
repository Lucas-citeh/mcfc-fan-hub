City vs Fulham: Maresca's Men Must End Three-Match Losing Run as Etihad Clash Looms

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 19 November 2026

Manchester City return from the November international break with a point to prove. Three successive Premier League defeats — at Aston Villa, at home to Brighton and at Nottingham Forest — have made Saturday's hosting of Fulham at the Etihad something of a must-win moment for Enzo Maresca's side, who sit on 21 points from ten games.

The Run That Must End

The October-November slide has been difficult to watch for City supporters. A 2-1 defeat at Villa Park on 24 October ended City's perfect start to the Premier League campaign. It was followed by Brighton's historic 1-0 win at the Etihad on 31 October — the Seagulls' first ever league victory at the ground — and then a 3-1 loss at Nottingham Forest on 7 November, where City were outrun and outfought for long spells by Nuno Espírito Santo's side.

City have conceded six goals and scored just two in those three defeats. The problems have been structural as much as individual — transitions, defensive shape and an inability to retain leads have all been visible. Maresca identified those issues in his post-Forest press conference, and the two-week international break gave the coaching staff time to work on them in training at the City Football Academy.

International Break: Who Is Available?

The break brought both rest and international duty for large portions of Maresca's squad. Nico O'Reilly, who was resting a back problem after playing in the international fixtures earlier in the month, is expected to be available for Saturday's match — a boost to City's midfield options. The academy product has been one of City's most consistent performers when fit this season.

Marc Guehi is also a major doubt for Saturday after suffering a hip problem at Nottingham Forest on 7 November — he was substituted at half-time and reports from 19 November indicate his chances of facing Fulham are slim. Sources: Sports Mole (19 Nov 2026); premierleaguenewsnow.com (19 Nov 2026).

Antoine Semenyo, however, remains unavailable. The Ghanaian winger suffered a knee injury — collateral damage around the knee with no major ligament rupture — in the 3-2 win over Ipswich Town on 17 October. Manager Enzo Maresca said at the time that Semenyo faces up to eight weeks out, which points to an estimated return in December. He will not be available on Saturday.

Erling Haaland, who is expected to lead the attack as normal, has nine Premier League goals this season and 14 in all competitions — the striker's output has remained consistent even during the difficult run. He will be central to City's hopes of returning to winning ways.

The Opponents: Fulham

Fulham arrive at the Etihad sitting 15th in the Premier League table on 10 points from 10 matches, making them opponents City will be expected to beat regardless of form. The Cottagers drew 1-1 with Newcastle United on the same weekend as City's Forest defeat. Alvaro Arbeloa's side can be a difficult opponent at the Etihad — they won here in the 2024/25 season — and will not be overawed by the occasion.

City have traditionally been strong at home to Fulham, however, and the Etihad crowd will be expecting a response after three straight defeats. With Napoli visiting in the Champions League just three days later (24 November) and a top-of-the-table trip to Arsenal's Emirates on 28 November to follow, City enter a crucial 11-day stretch that could define their title ambitions.

What to Expect

Maresca is likely to look for more defensive stability after leaking six goals in three games. City's UCL form has been considerably better — they won 3-2 at RB Leipzig on 4 November — and finding that European cohesion in domestic football is the challenge ahead. Rayan Cherki has been City's standout creator this season and will again be expected to unlock a compact Fulham defensive block.

The return of international fixtures has also given several players, including Josko Gvardiol and Ruben Dias, a chance to build match sharpness before Fulham. City need a home win — and given the fixtures that follow, this Saturday feels like the moment to draw a line under the worst run of the season so far.
