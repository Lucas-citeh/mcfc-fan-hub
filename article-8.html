City's November Test: Leipzig, Forest, Arsenal — Can Maresca's Unbeaten Blues Survive Their Toughest Month?

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 17 October 2026

Manchester City sit six points clear at the top of the Premier League, unbeaten in six games. But November brings the toughest sequence of the season: five fixtures in 25 days, including a Champions League bounce-back assignment at RB Leipzig, Elliot Anderson's emotional return to Nottingham Forest, and a title-defining trip to Arsenal. This month will shape the 2026/27 campaign.

The Fixture List

Enzo Maresca's side will play five times between 4 and 28 November across two competitions:

  • Wed 4 Nov: RB Leipzig (A) — UEFA Champions League MD3, 20:00 GMT
  • Sat 7 Nov: Nottingham Forest (A) — Premier League MD8, 15:00 GMT
  • Sat 21 Nov: Fulham (H) — Premier League MD9, 15:00 GMT
  • Tue 24 Nov: Napoli (H) — UEFA Champions League MD4, 20:00 GMT
  • Sat 28 Nov: Arsenal (A) — Premier League MD10, 15:00 GMT

UCL Recovery: Leipzig Away

The month begins in Germany. City travel to Red Bull Arena on Wednesday 4 November for their third Champions League league-phase fixture, needing a response after Pep Guardiola's PSG handed them a chastening 4-2 defeat at the Etihad on 14 October. The margin of that loss — three goals down at half-time before Haaland's brace — reflected the gap in tempo and pressing intensity that Maresca must address.

Leipzig, now in their seventh consecutive Champions League campaign, represent a genuine test. Their high-press, counter-pressing style is well-suited to exploiting transition moments — exactly the weakness PSG exploited at the Etihad. City will need Elliot Anderson and Enzo Fernández to be decisive in central areas, and Haaland to be clinical when chances arrive.

A win at the Red Bull Arena would move City to P3 W2 L1 in the league phase — a healthy position heading into the back half of the group stage. A second defeat in three UCL games would raise questions about whether the 4-2 reverse was a one-off or a genuine vulnerability.

Anderson's Return to Forest

Three days after Leipzig, City face their arguably toughest Premier League assignment of November: a trip to the City Ground to face Nottingham Forest. On paper, Forest may be the least-fancied of City's November opponents — but the emotional context makes it unique.

Elliot Anderson, City's £116m club-record signing from Forest this summer, will walk back into his old stadium for the first time since his departure. The Newcastle-born midfielder was Forest's standout performer in 2025/26, earning his transfer with a series of dominant Premier League displays. His absence was felt at the City Ground this season — Forest are not the same without him — and his return will carry an intensity that goes beyond the usual away-day atmosphere.

Anderson has been magnificent since arriving at the Etihad, earning Premier League Player of the Match in the Manchester derby and setting up Jérémy Doku for City's second goal at Anfield. He'll be desperate to put in a performance against the club that gave him his top-flight platform.

Fulham and Napoli: Consolidation Matches

The second half of November begins with Fulham at the Etihad on 21 November — a game City should win, but one that requires full focus after the Leipzig-Forest double header. Three days later, Napoli visit Manchester for UCL Matchday 4, giving City the chance to continue consolidating in the league phase.

Arsenal Away: The Title Indicator

November closes with the fixture City fans have been waiting for since the fixture list was published: Arsenal away on 28 November. The Gunners beat City 3-0 in the Community Shield in August and remain one of City's most credible title rivals.

By the time the two sides meet at the Emirates, City will know exactly where they stand in the title race. An unbeaten City side arriving six-plus points clear and in Champions League contention would be a very different proposition from the team Calafiori, Havertz and Ødegaard dismantled in Cardiff. Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta will be plotting a season-defining win; Maresca will be targeting a statement that their 6-0 start was no false dawn.

The Foden Factor

Hovering over all five fixtures is the question of Phil Foden. The England international has been absent since his straight red card in the 199th Manchester derby on 13 September — his three-match domestic ban was served against Norwich (EFL Cup), Sunderland and Liverpool, but he also underwent appendix surgery during the international break. The NHS recommends a minimum two-week recovery from an appendectomy; City have not yet given a firm return date.

If Foden returns for Leipzig or Forest, his creativity and ability to unlock deep defences would be a significant boost. City's attack has functioned well without him — Cherki has stepped forward superbly — but Foden at full fitness is the kind of difference-maker who wins games in November when conditions are difficult and legs are heavy.

Verdict

City have been exceptional so far. Six wins from six in the Premier League, maximum points from their opening UCL fixture at Porto. But the PSG result was a warning: this squad is not yet the finished article, and there are opponents with the quality to expose its vulnerabilities.

November is when we find out. It is also when, against the backdrop of the ongoing Premier League financial charges appeal, this group of players has the chance to make the loudest statement of all — on the pitch, where it counts.
