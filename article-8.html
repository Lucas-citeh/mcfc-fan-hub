Bounce-Back Time: City Travel to Nottingham Forest After Back-to-Back Losses

1 November 2026 · Blue Moonies Fan Hub

Manchester City head to the City Ground on Saturday 7 November (15:00 GMT) looking to arrest a two-game Premier League losing run. Defeats to Aston Villa (2-1, away, Matchday 8) and Brighton & Hove Albion (0-1, home, Matchday 9) have trimmed City's nine-game points tally to 21 — still enough to keep Enzo Maresca's side at the top of the Premier League, but the early-season aura of invincibility has faded.

What Went Wrong

The Brighton defeat on 31 October was arguably the more concerning of the two losses. At Villa Park, City were beaten by a physically superior home side on a day when Erling Haaland's second-half header was not enough. Against Brighton at the Etihad, City were largely passive: Bart Verbruggen made seven saves but City rarely tested him in the way a top side should at home. Yasin Ayari's 11th-minute goal — a tap-in after a low cross missed Matheus Nunes — was the only moment of quality the game produced. Back-to-back league defeats, City's first in 11 months, raise questions about their defensive organisation and creativity without Antoine Semenyo, who is not expected back until December.

Anderson Returns to the City Ground

The narrative of Saturday's trip is impossible to avoid: Elliot Anderson, signed for a club-record £116m from Nottingham Forest in July 2026, makes his first return to the City Ground. Anderson left Forest as their best player and the deal that took him to the Etihad remains a subject of lingering resentment among Forest supporters. Maresca will need him performing at his best if City are to get a result on what promises to be a combustible occasion.

Anderson has been City's most consistent midfielder this season. In nine appearances he has contributed directly to four goals and was arguably the Player of the Season performance in the 0-1 Manchester derby win at Old Trafford — where, reduced to ten men after Phil Foden's red card, he put in 76 touches and ran every defensive sprint. The City Ground reception will be hostile, but that may suit a player who feeds on big occasions.

Forest's Form

Nottingham Forest, managed this season under their second-year boss, have made a solid start to 2026/27 and sit comfortably in mid-table. They are compact and hard to break down at home, and the City Ground remains one of the louder atmospheres in the division. Forest beat Liverpool 1-0 at the City Ground last season and their home record against top-six sides has been respectable in recent years. Maresca will be well aware of the danger.

Team News

Antoine Semenyo (knee) remains out and is not expected until December. Phil Foden is fit and available after serving his three-match suspension earlier in October. Ayyoub Bouaddi came off early in the Brighton loss after looking fatigued and may be rotated. Rayan Cherki, who replaced Bouaddi on Saturday, could start from the off. Jeremy Doku — who played against Ipswich and Villa but was an unused substitute against Brighton — could push for a start on the right flank. There are no new reported injuries.

What City Need

Three things need to improve. First, defensive structure: Donnarumma's high line has been exploited twice in the last week by teams who pressed City's full-backs and won second balls. Second, set-piece threat: City have failed to convert on corners and free-kicks in their last four league games. Third, intensity in the first fifteen minutes — both recent losses saw City concede inside the opening quarter of an hour and spend the rest of the match chasing the game. A good start at the City Ground, in a hostile environment, would be the ideal response.

City face a run of four November fixtures that could define the shape of the title race: Forest (a), Fulham (h, Nov 21), Napoli (h, UCL, Nov 24), Arsenal (a, Nov 28). The month ends with the most anticipated fixture of the domestic season so far. Three points at the City Ground would set the tone nicely.
