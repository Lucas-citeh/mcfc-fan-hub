Double Header Preview: Liverpool & PSG — City's Biggest Week of the Season

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 5 October 2026

Manchester City return from the international break having swept through the first five Premier League matchdays with maximum points. Now comes the hardest week of the season so far: a trip to Anfield on Sunday 11 October and then PSG at the Etihad three days later on Wednesday 14 October.

Liverpool at Anfield — Sunday 11 October, 16:30 BST (Sky Sports)

Anfield has never been an easy venue for City, and it will be no different under new Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola, who joined from Bournemouth in the summer. Liverpool sit 7th on 9 points from 5 games — six points behind City — but their form at home has been solid enough to make this a genuine examination.

The absentee news is significant. Phil Foden is definitely out: he is serving the third and final match of his three-game domestic suspension following his red card against Manchester United on 13 September, and is also recovering from the appendicitis surgery that was confirmed during the international break. City confirmed he will not be available for the Anfield trip regardless of the suspension — with no firm return date given for full fitness.

Nico O'Reilly (back injury) is targeting a return for this match, with Maresca describing the injury as "not expected to be serious" when it first occurred ahead of the Coventry fixture on 5 September. The academy graduate missed five games in a row but should be in contention if he has come through the international break unscathed.

Jeremy Doku is expected to be available. The Belgian winger returned to action as a second-half substitute in the 5-3 Sunderland win on 20 September — his first appearance since picking up a calf injury in the Community Shield — and the extra weeks of the break should have him closer to full fitness.

With Foden absent, the expected attacking shape will be built around Rayan Cherki (three PL goals, four in all competitions) in a free role, supported by Erling Haaland as the focal point. Elliot Anderson and Enzo Fernández have established themselves as the central midfield engine, combining to cover ground, press, and carry the ball into dangerous areas.

Paris Saint-Germain at the Etihad — Wednesday 14 October, 20:00 BST (UCL MD2)

Three days after Anfield, attention switches to European competition. PSG arrive at the Etihad as the reigning UEFA Champions League holders. City already have a 2-0 away win at FC Porto on their UCL ledger from Matchday 1 — meaning a victory over PSG would put them in a commanding position in the league phase standings.

Foden's availability for the PSG match is classified as uncertain. The domestic suspension does not apply to European competition, but the appendicitis surgery recovery timeline — a minimum of two weeks from the operation confirmed during the break — means his return depends entirely on medical clearance. No firm return date has been set by Manchester City.

City's Form — Five Wins, Perfect Record

Enzo Maresca's side sit on 15 Premier League points from five games, with a goal difference of +8 — the best record in the top flight. Erling Haaland leads the club scoring charts with 5 Premier League goals and 7 in all competitions, while Cherki has 3 in the league and 4 across all competitions. The settled 4-2-3-1 system and the cohesion between the summer signings — Fernández, Anderson, Bouaddi, Ndiaye, Elias — are already among the most encouraging signs of the new era.

The key question heading into this double header is whether City can maintain their perfect record against two of European football's biggest clubs without one of their most creative players. On current form, there is every reason to believe they can.
