Arsenal vs City: Six Points and 90 Minutes to Close the Gap

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 24 November 2026

Manchester City travel to the Emirates on Saturday 28 November (15:00 GMT) for one of the defining fixtures of the Premier League season so far. Arsenal lead the table; City have not won in four league games. The gap is five points. This match could stretch it to eight — or bring it back to two.

The Context

When these two clubs met in August — the FA Community Shield at the Principality Stadium, Cardiff — Arsenal dismantled City 3-0. Riccardo Calafiori scored after just 24 seconds, Kai Havertz headed a second and Martin Ødegaard rolled in a third before the hour mark. Arsenal have not looked back since, winning the Community Shield and opening their Premier League campaign at a pace that has left the rest of the division scrambling.

City, meanwhile, have suffered a sharp reversal in form. After winning their opening seven Premier League games under Enzo Maresca — the best start to a season any City manager has achieved — they have collected just one point from their last four: a 2-1 defeat at Villa Park, a 0-1 home loss to Brighton, a 3-1 defeat at Nottingham Forest and a 1-1 draw against Fulham at the Etihad, where the crowd booed at full time. Arsenal's lead at the top now stands at five points with 11 games played.

What Arsenal Bring

Arsenal's form has been exceptional. They are the Premier League's highest scorers and defensively one of the meanest sides in Europe. Mikel Arteta's system is now fully embedded: aggressive pressing, rapid transitions and a front line — anchored by Alexandre Lacazette and fed by Martinelli, Ødegaard and an increasingly influential Leandro Trossard — that is capable of punishing any lapse in concentration. The Emirates will be in full voice, and Arsenal know that a win here, combined with City's poor run, could be pivotal.

Can City Rediscover Their Best?

There have been flashes of the team City can be even during the difficult run. Rayan Cherki's pull-back against Forest was first-class; Erling Haaland's threat never disappeared. What has gone wrong is largely structural: too open in transition, conceding from set pieces, and lacking the defensive cover that Rodri provided for so many years. Elliot Anderson has shown quality but cannot replicate Rodri's positional intelligence alone.

Maresca is likely to set up conservatively at the Emirates — not to park the bus, but to establish a defensive foundation first. If the January window brings a defensive midfielder (and there is growing speculation around City's targets), it may be too late for Saturday. The onus falls on Anderson, Bouaddi and Kovacic to hold the shape while Cherki, Haaland and either Ndiaye or Doku carry the attacking intent.

Key Absences

Antoine Semenyo remains absent (knee, estimated return December 2026). Marc Guehi has a hip concern from the Fulham draw that will be assessed; if he misses out, Khusanov is the likely deputy alongside Ruben Dias. Phil Foden is fit and his creativity on the left will be vital if City are to create enough to test Raya or the Arsenal goalkeeper.

The Big Picture

A City win on Saturday would cut the gap to two points and shift the psychological pressure back onto Arsenal. A defeat would open an eight-point chasm before December has even begun. For a City side still learning to function without Pep Guardiola's decade of accumulated tactical refinement, this is the clearest measure of progress under Maresca — and perhaps the clearest statement of intent for the rest of the season.
