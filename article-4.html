City Give Gonzalez the Green Light as Newcastle and Real Madrid Circle

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 23 August 2026

A week ago, Nico Gonzalez was telling anyone who would listen that he wanted to stay at Manchester City. As of 22 August 2026, the club has changed the conversation. Per The Hard Tackle, Manchester City have now informed the 24-year-old Spanish midfielder that he is free to leave the Etihad before the transfer window closes on 1 September. The door — which was officially closed from the player's side just days ago — is now open, and Newcastle United and Real Madrid are standing right outside it.

From "Wants to Stay" to "Free to Leave"

As recently as 20 August, Read Man City had reported that Gonzalez had "made his stance clear" — he was committed to his City future, convinced he could play a significant role under Enzo Maresca now that two senior central midfielders had departed in Rodri and Tijjani Reijnders. Teammates were said to be encouraging him to stay. The player himself believed this was the moment to prove himself at the Etihad.

Manchester City saw it differently. With Elliot Anderson already signed for £116m and Ayyoub Bouaddi's deal from Lille edging towards completion in final-stage negotiations, the arithmetic of City's squad does not necessarily require Gonzalez. The club's message, delivered to the player on or around 22 August according to The Hard Tackle, was simple: he is available.

Newcastle and Real Madrid Step Up

Two clubs immediately emerge as frontrunners. Newcastle United have been in talks with Manchester City since 18–19 August, driven by the urgency of replacing Bruno Guimaraes and Sandro Tonali — both of whom departed St James' Park this summer — before the window shuts. Fabrizio Romano confirmed on 22 August that City are "quite open" to doing business with Newcastle at the right price, while adding that the door to Gonzalez's exit was far from closed.

Real Madrid have now entered the picture alongside the Magpies. CaughtOffside reported on 22 August that Madrid have stepped up their interest in Gonzalez, potentially adding a Champions League-winning destination to the Spaniard's options. Whether the player's preference — should his stance on staying at City have softened following the club's message — leans towards English football or a return to Spain remains to be seen.

OneFootball reported on 22–23 August that Newcastle are "close to signing" Gonzalez in a deal worth approximately £52.5m — a figure that would represent a modest profit on the £52m (€60m) City paid Porto to sign him in January 2025.

What City Get From the Deal

At around £52.5m for a midfielder who has not yet fully nailed down a starting role at the Etihad, the return is fair rather than spectacular. But for Manchester City's financial planning — which has involved significant outgoings this summer in Rodri (€76.5m to Barcelona) and Reijnders (€61m to Al Qadsiah), alongside club-record expenditure on Elliot Anderson — the logic of recycling a player who is surplus to requirements is straightforward.

The caveat is the timeline. With nine days of the transfer window remaining, City need any interested club to move quickly. Newcastle's existing engagement gives them an advantage; the question is whether they are prepared to meet City's valuation before Real Madrid mount a more concrete approach.

Bouaddi Situation: Final Stages

The Gonzalez developments cannot be fully separated from the Bouaddi saga. Fabrizio Romano reported on 22–23 August that negotiations between City and Lille for Ayyoub Bouaddi have moved into the "final stages," describing the deal as "very close between clubs" and stating that City are "on the verge of reaching an agreement." Morocco World News reported that the two clubs have scheduled new dedicated talks to finalise the transfer.

Bouaddi, 18, has been the subject of months of negotiation: City agreed personal terms with him on a contract to June 2031 weeks ago, but the fee gap between Lille's asking price (~€100m) and City's offer (~€90m plus bonuses) has been the persistent sticking point. Romano's language on 22–23 August suggests that gap is now narrowing to a close. If confirmed, his arrival would effectively make Gonzalez's departure clean and logical rather than a forced sale.

Window Closing In

The summer transfer window closes at 23:00 BST on 1 September 2026. City's most active period of business — the departures of Rodri and Reijnders, the arrivals of Anderson and Rulli, the ongoing Bouaddi and Gusto negotiations — has been compressed into a fortnight of extraordinary activity. Gonzalez's situation now forms part of that final sprint.

As of the morning of 23 August, there has been no official announcement from mancity.com or Newcastle United regarding Gonzalez, and no Fabrizio Romano "here we go" on the deal. But the shift in City's position — from reluctant seller to willing one — changes everything. Watch this space.
