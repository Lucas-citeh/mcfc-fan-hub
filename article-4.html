City U-Turn Complete: Savinho Sold to Tottenham for £85m

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 25 August 2026

The football world does not do irony like Manchester City's transfer department. Just 27 days after Savinho stood in front of the cameras and declared "City is the best club in the world" following his new six-year contract signing, Manchester City confirmed on 25 August 2026 that the Brazilian winger has been sold to Tottenham Hotspur for £85 million — a club-record sale. The transfer, confirmed by both clubs and reported by the Associated Press and Al Jazeera, takes City's summer business into extraordinary territory.

The Sequence No One Saw Coming

The story of Savinho's summer at City is one of football's great reversals. Tottenham had chased the Brazilian relentlessly throughout July 2026, making multiple approaches as Spurs' new project under their chairman picked up pace. City rebuffed every approach and, on 29 July, delivered what looked like a definitive answer: Savinho had signed a new six-year contract keeping him at the Etihad until 2031, with an option to extend by a further year. The player's own post-signing statement — emphatic, affectionate — suggested the chapter was closed.

Tottenham, evidently, read it differently. A revised offer landed within days. The fee on the table this time — an initial £75 million with up to £10 million in achievable add-ons, of which approximately £5 million are reported to be effectively guaranteed — appears to have changed the calculation for Manchester City's board, even amid an already seismic summer of transfer activity. The Washington Post, reporting from an Associated Press source, confirmed City agreed to the club-record sale. Al Jazeera confirmed the signing of the winger by Tottenham on 25 August 2026.

What City Are Getting

The headline figure is £85 million, with at least £80 million virtually certain to land at the Etihad. For context: City purchased Savinho from Girona — then still on a season-long loan from CFG sister club Troyes — for a reported £34 million in summer 2024. The uplift in value over two seasons at the Etihad represents a significant profit, and one that funds further activity in the remaining days of the window.

Whether that activity will include a replacement for Savinho, or whether Enzo Maresca intends to use the sale proceeds on different targets, remains unclear. Maresca has made no public comment on the departure. The window closes at 23:00 BST on 1 September.

What Tottenham Are Getting

Tottenham's summer spending now exceeds £400 million after this deal — a statement of extraordinary intent. Savinho provides Ange Postecoglou's, or whoever is managing Spurs, with a direct, high-pressing, one-versus-one winger who ranked among the Premier League's elite dribblers in 2024/25. His acceleration, close control, and ability to create from nothing on the right side represent exactly the type of profile Tottenham have been seeking.

At 22, he also fits the age profile Spurs are building towards — young enough to improve further, established enough to contribute immediately. He will not be short of motivation to prove his worth, given the eyebrows that will inevitably be raised at the manner of his departure from City.

A Reshaped Front Line

Savinho's exit leaves Maresca with a reshuffled attack. Erling Haaland, Phil Foden, Jeremy Doku (currently recovering from injury), Antoine Semenyo, Omar Marmoush, and Jack Grealish remain as options out wide and through the middle. The Brazilian had been a reliable starter throughout pre-season — appearing in all three Asia tour matches — before seemingly falling in the pecking order as the season began.

Sky Sports noted in reporting the Savinho deal that Tottenham were simultaneously "closing in on Omar Marmoush" — City's Egyptian forward — which would represent a further thinning of Maresca's attacking options. No official announcement has been made on Marmoush as of 25 August, and the situation is developing.

A Window to Remember — For All the Wrong Reasons

Manchester City fans will spend the weeks after this transfer window trying to make sense of what happened to their club over the summer of 2026. Pep Guardiola departed after 10 years. Rodri left for Barcelona. Bernardo Silva finally completed his long-anticipated Real Madrid move. Reijnders went to Saudi Arabia. And now Savinho — who was meant to be the one who stayed — is a Tottenham player.

The counter-argument from the boardroom is compelling in its own terms: the arrivals of Donnarumma, Elliot Anderson, Bouaddi, Rulli, Monga, and Charles represent a significant rebuild, and the net spend has likely come out ahead. But the emotional weight of these departures — and the particular sting of Savinho's July contract being torn up within a month — is the defining feature of this particular summer.
