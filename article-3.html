Elliot Anderson to City: Romano Confirms Deal in 'Final Stages' as Medical Nears in USA

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 20 June 2026

Manchester City's summer transfer campaign has reached a critical juncture. Fabrizio Romano confirmed on 19 June 2026 that the club's long-running pursuit of Nottingham Forest and England midfielder Elliot Anderson has entered the 'final stages', with the 23-year-old expected to undergo a medical in the United States during the FIFA World Cup. City are growing increasingly confident of completing a deal that would smash the British transfer record.

Romano: 'Final Stages' After Intensive Work Behind the Scenes

After weeks of bids and rejections, Romano's update is the clearest signal yet that the Anderson deal will be done. City believe they can secure the agreement, with the transfer entering its final stages following intensive work from sporting director Hugo Viana. The transfer journalist reported that Anderson is ready to undergo medical testing in the USA while on World Cup duty with England — a significant logistical milestone that reflects how close the two clubs are to a resolution.

England manager Thomas Tuchel had already given clearance for the medical to take place during the tournament. Personal terms between the player and the club are understood to have been agreed in principle — a five-year deal, per transfer insider Nicolo Schira — meaning the sole remaining obstacle is a club-to-club fee agreement.

The Fee Dispute: Narrowing But Not Resolved

City's pursuit has been defined by a protracted fee dispute with Forest. The Athletic's David Ornstein first confirmed an opening bid had been rejected on 3 June. Sky Sports then reported that a second offer — £106m guaranteed plus £15m in add-ons, totalling approximately £121m — was also turned down on 10 June. Forest's position, set by owner Evangelos Marinakis, has been consistent: a guaranteed flat fee at or above £130m, with no add-on structures.

The gap between City's last offer and Forest's demand stands at approximately £9m on the base fee. Given Romano's 'final stages' framing following this impasse, the expectation is that City are preparing to match or move very close to Forest's valuation. Should a deal be concluded, it would set a new British transfer record.

Why City Want Anderson So Badly

The 23-year-old Newcastle-born midfielder is a complete box-to-box operator — engine, intensity, and technical quality in tight spaces. His 2025/26 season at Nottingham Forest was outstanding, establishing him as one of the Premier League's best midfielders and drawing interest from clubs across Europe before City emerged as the clear frontrunner. Manchester United, once reported as rivals, officially withdrew from the race on 16 June, per ESPN, leaving City with clear air.

For Enzo Maresca — whose system demands midfielders who press aggressively, recover possession, and contribute in both phases — Anderson is an ideal fit. His ability to operate in a double pivot or as a progressive ball-carrier gives Maresca the flexibility the current squad does not fully provide. With Rodri's contract situation unresolved beyond summer 2027, adding Anderson alongside him would represent a generational midfield transition.

Plan B: Sandro Tonali

Should the Anderson deal ultimately collapse — an outcome that appears increasingly unlikely — Fabrizio Romano has confirmed that Sandro Tonali of Newcastle United is City's named alternative. The Italian international, now clear of his betting ban, is a top-tier midfield option. Romano's identification of Tonali as Plan B underlines the depth of City's midfield planning and that Viana has contingencies in place.

What Comes Next

A Romano "here we go" had not yet been issued as of 20 June 2026. The medical expected in the USA is the next milestone to watch. Once complete and a fee agreed, formal announcement and unveiling would follow — most likely before pre-season begins in July. For Enzo Maresca's rebuild, Anderson would be the headline signing of the new era: a statement of intent from a club determined to return to the summit of English football.
