Farewell Pep: Watkins Double Ends Guardiola's Reign in 1-2 Defeat to Villa

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 27 May 2026

Pep Guardiola's ten-year reign as Manchester City manager ended not with a victory lap but with a 2-1 defeat to Aston Villa at the Etihad on Sunday, as Ollie Watkins scored twice to complete a bittersweet final day for City's departing supporters.

Antoine Semenyo's 23rd-minute volley had given the home side the lead — his 11th goal in City colours since his January arrival from Bournemouth — but Watkins equalised two minutes into the second half after pouncing on a poor John Stones header from a corner. The England striker completed his brace in the 61st minute, stroking past James Trafford after jinking through the City defence, and that was enough for Villa to claim the points.

Guardiola's Nine Changes

With the domestic double already secure and the season title race long decided in Arsenal's favour, Guardiola made nine changes from the FA Cup Final side that had beaten Chelsea at Wembley eight days earlier. Bernardo Silva captained the team in what would be his final City appearance, lining up alongside Nico Gonzalez in central midfield. John Stones started at centre-back alongside Rúben Dias — a deliberate decision to give both departing players a send-off in front of their home crowd. Erling Haaland did not feature.

The starting lineup — Trafford; Lewis, Stones, Dias, Aké; Bernardo, Nico Gonzalez; Semenyo, Reijnders, Savinho; Foden — reflected the emotional weight of the occasion as much as any tactical calculation.

Guard of Honour, Tears on the Touchline

The defining images of the afternoon were not from open play but from the substitutions. The emotional apex came in the 59th minute when Bernardo Silva was withdrawn, walking through a guard of honour from both sets of players to a standing ovation from all four sides of a packed Etihad. He left in tears, embracing Guardiola on the touchline. John Stones received the same treatment at the 77th minute, when a triple substitution also brought on Gvardiol, Rayan Ait-Nouri and Jeremy Doku.

A crowd of 60,332 had come not expecting an easy victory but to say goodbye, and the occasion delivered on every human level even as it fell short on the scoreboard.

The Final Ledger

The defeat meant City ended the 2025/26 Premier League season with 23 wins, 9 draws and 6 losses — 78 points, finishing second behind Arsenal's title-winning 85-point campaign. The domestic double — FA Cup and EFL Cup — provides real substance to the campaign. Erling Haaland claimed his third Premier League Golden Boot with 27 goals; Antoine Semenyo finished with 17 Premier League goals and 22 in all competitions after his January arrival.

For Guardiola personally, Sunday was match 593 in charge of Manchester City. He departs having won 305 of them. Six Premier League titles, two FA Cups, seven League Cups, one Champions League — a managerial tenure without precedent in the English game.

A New Chapter Begins

The day after the final whistle, Manchester City confirmed the appointment of Enzo Maresca as their new head coach on a three-year contract running until June 2029. Maresca, 46, was Guardiola's own assistant during the treble-winning 2022/23 campaign. He inherits a squad of genuine quality — Haaland, Foden (contracted until 2030), Reijnders, Cherki, Gvardiol — with the challenge to keep the machine running and, in time, to make it his own.

The era is over. A new one has already begun.
