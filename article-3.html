Title Race Goes to the Wire: City Must Win at Bournemouth

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 18 May 2026

Manchester City's Premier League title dream is still alive — but only just. Arsenal lead the table by two points with two games remaining, and Pep Guardiola's side must win both of their final fixtures while hoping their rivals stumble.

City head to the Vitality Stadium on Tuesday night for a 20:00 kick-off against Bournemouth, a ground where they have historically found things difficult. Three days later, they host Aston Villa at the Etihad on the final day of the season. The arithmetic is simple: win both and apply maximum pressure. Drop points anywhere and the title goes to Arsenal.

The Run-In

City arrive at this juncture in fine form. A 3-0 demolition of Crystal Palace on 13 May — with Semenyo, Marmoush and Savinho on the scoresheet — was followed by a 1-0 FA Cup Final win over Chelsea at Wembley three days later. The domestic double is already secured. But the league remains the prize that matters most to supporters.

Arsenal currently sit on 79 points, two clear of City's 77. Guardiola's men have played 36 games, winning 23, drawing 8 and losing 5, with a goal difference of +43. The final two games represent the last chance to overhaul the gap.

Bournemouth Away

The Vitality Stadium trip is arguably the harder of the two remaining fixtures. Bournemouth have had a strong second half of the season and will be motivated as they push for a high finish of their own. The Opta supercomputer gives City a 45.3% chance of victory, with the match finely balanced.

Guardiola is expected to rotate following the exertions of Wembley, with key players likely to be freshened up ahead of what could prove to be a decisive final-day encounter. Erling Haaland, who was rested against Palace, is expected to lead the attack.

Final Day vs Aston Villa

If City are still in contention after Bournemouth, they face Aston Villa at home on Sunday 24 May in what could be a winner-takes-all occasion. The Opta supercomputer — now with official final-day figures available — rates City as favourites at 50.1%, with a draw at 23.1% and a Villa win at 26.8%.

Villa are chasing Champions League qualification themselves, which makes the prospect of an open, attacking game at the Etihad more likely. City will need to be at their best.

The Bigger Picture

Whatever the outcome over the next week, this has already been a remarkable season. The FA Cup and EFL Cup double is in the cabinet. Phil Foden has committed his future to the club until 2030. New signings Antoine Semenyo and Marc Guehi have exceeded expectations since arriving in January.

The departure of Bernardo Silva and John Stones this summer means significant reconstruction lies ahead — but for now, every last ounce of attention is on the Premier League title. Tuesday night at Bournemouth is where it begins.
