End of an Era: City Finish Second, Win the Double, and Say Goodbye to Pep

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 24 May 2026

Pep Guardiola's final game as Manchester City manager ended in a 1-2 home defeat to Aston Villa on Sunday afternoon, but to judge the 2025/26 season by that single result would be to miss the point entirely. City end the campaign as FA Cup and EFL Cup holders, Premier League runners-up on 78 points, and the departing venue for one of the greatest managers football has ever seen.

Final Day: 1-2 to Aston Villa

Guardiola shuffled his pack for the occasion, making nine changes and handing farewell starts to Bernardo Silva — captaining the side for the 460th and final time — and John Stones, who ended a decade at the club. James Trafford started in goal; Nathan Ake, Rico Lewis, Ruben Dias and Stones formed the back four; Nico Gonzalez and Tijjani Reijnders sat in midfield; Savinho, Bernardo and Phil Foden operated behind Antoine Semenyo up front.

City led through Semenyo's 23rd-minute close-range finish from a corner — fitting that their January signing should mark the occasion. But Ollie Watkins turned the game in the second half, stabbing home from a corner in the 47th minute to equalise and then curling a right-footed finish across goal in the 61st minute, VAR confirming the winner. Guardiola's era ended, as it began, at the Etihad — only this time there was no trophy waiting at the end.

The emotional apex came in the 59th minute when Bernardo Silva was substituted, walking through a guard of honour from both sets of players to a standing ovation from all four sides of a packed Etihad. Stones received the same treatment at the 77th minute, when a triple substitution also brought on Gvardiol, Rayan Ait-Nouri and Jeremy Doku.

The Season in Full

Strip away the final-day sentiment and the 2025/26 record stands up well. City won the EFL Cup in February, then completed the domestic double with a 1-0 FA Cup Final victory over Chelsea at Wembley on 16 May — Antoine Semenyo's 72nd-minute back-heel finish giving the club a 16th major trophy under Guardiola's management.

In the Premier League, City accumulated 78 points from 38 games (23 wins, 9 draws, 6 losses) and finished second behind Arsenal, who were confirmed champions after City's 1-1 draw at Bournemouth on 19 May — Arsenal's first title in 22 years. The gap between the teams — four points — is modest by historical standards, but it was Arsenal's season throughout.

Individual Honours

Erling Haaland claimed his third Premier League Golden Boot in four seasons with 27 goals — he did not feature in the Villa finale — extending his record as the fastest player in Premier League history to reach 100 goals. Antoine Semenyo, the January signing from Bournemouth, finished with 17 Premier League goals and 22 in all competitions, earning widespread recognition as one of the signings of the season. Phil Foden signed a new four-year contract through to 2030, an emphatic statement of intent ahead of the Maresca era.

Departures and What Comes Next

Bernardo Silva and John Stones both leave as free agents this summer, ending chapters of extraordinary distinction. Between them they played more than 750 games for City and collected dozens of major honours. Their exits, coming at the same time as Guardiola's, represent the largest single generational shift the club has faced since the Sergio Agüero era ended.

Incoming manager Enzo Maresca is expected to be formally appointed in the coming days, having reached verbal agreement on a three-year deal. The Italian — who served as Guardiola's assistant during the 2022-23 treble campaign — inherits a squad of genuine quality: Haaland, Reijnders, Gvardiol, Cherki, Semenyo, Foden and a back line reinforced by the January arrivals of Marc Guehi and Nathan Ake. The challenge is to keep the machine running — and, in time, to make it his own.

Guardiola's 429 matches, 305 wins and a permanent stand bearing his name at the Etihad are the numbers. What they represent is harder to quantify. Blue Moonies — thank your manager.
