Fee Agreed: City and Spurs Strike ~£60m Deal for Savinho as Romano Signals Announcement Close

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 8 June 2026

Manchester City and Tottenham Hotspur have reached a basic fee agreement for Savinho worth approximately £60 million, with Fabrizio Romano signalling that Spurs are "set to announce" the Brazilian winger's arrival once the Premier League transfer window opens on 15 June. The deal — which began as a "want" from Savinho himself just days ago — has moved at remarkable speed to the cusp of an official announcement.

Multiple outlets reported on 7–8 June that a core financial agreement has been struck between the two clubs. CaughtOffside reported on 7 June that Tottenham had reached a "basic agreement" with Manchester City at approximately £60 million, with only the add-on structure now requiring finalisation. GoonerDaily followed on 8 June confirming the fee agreement had been reached. The figures represent an increase from the £50 million being discussed earlier in the week, suggesting City extracted a higher fee than the initial reports indicated they would accept.

Romano: Spurs 'Set to Announce'

Fabrizio Romano — whose reporting on this deal has been the most consistent and reliable thread from start to finish — has now indicated that Tottenham are "set to announce" Savinho, with the formal confirmation expected to follow once the window officially opens on 15 June. In Romano's transfer lexicon, "set to announce" sits one step below "here we go" and indicates that all substantive elements of the deal are resolved. Personal terms, which were never expected to be a sticking point given Savinho's explicit desire to make the move, are understood to be agreed.

The speed of movement from the Romano confirmation of Savinho "wanting Tottenham" (5–6 June) to a fee agreement in principle (7–8 June) reflects the fact that both clubs had been laying groundwork for weeks. Roberto De Zerbi had given his personal approval for the deal before club-level negotiations even opened, and Manchester City were willing sellers once it became clear that Savinho's preference had crystallised.

The Fee: ~£60m

The agreed fee of approximately £60 million represents a notable recalibration from the £50 million figure that had been widely reported as the likely landing zone. City had originally valued Savinho at closer to £60 million, and that figure — rather than the discounted £50 million that Tottenham were initially pushing toward — appears to be where the deal has settled. That City held their asking price despite Savinho's desire to leave is a small but meaningful negotiating win for a club in full summer rebuild mode.

For context: Savinho was acquired by Manchester City from Girona — a City Football Group sister club — in the summer of 2024 for approximately €70 million. A £60 million sale in sterling represents a small loss in euro terms, but one City had accepted as the cost of freeing a player who no longer wanted to be at the club and funding recruitment elsewhere. The fee unlocks meaningful spending power for Enzo Maresca's team as they pursue Elliot Anderson, a replacement winger, and ongoing right-back recruitment.

What Savinho's Exit Opens Up

Savinho's departure clarifies several things for City's summer planning. Jeremy Doku — whose progress has at times been hampered by competition for wide positions — moves into a more central role in the wide attacking hierarchy alongside Antoine Semenyo. Reports from Vital Football on 8 June suggest Savinho's exit also opens up the possibility of Doku signing a new contract, which City would view as a priority given his age and potential.

City's primary replacement target in the wide forward position remains Yan Diomande, the 19-year-old RB Leipzig winger who won the Bundesliga Rookie of the Year award in 2025/26 with 13 goals and 10 assists. Leipzig are asking €100–120 million, and Liverpool are competing aggressively for the player's signature. Whether City commit to Diomande at that price — especially with the Elliot Anderson deal simultaneously consuming significant capital — remains the open question of the summer.

Savinho's World Cup Timing

Brazil are scheduled to begin their 2026 World Cup campaign on 11 June, with Savinho expected to be part of the squad. This creates a minor logistical delay: Tottenham and City are expected to formalise the announcement as soon as the window opens on 15 June, potentially while Savinho is already in tournament action. This mirrors how several previous high-profile World Cup-summer deals have operated — the player signs during the tournament, with the unveiling and medical scheduled once their national team's run concludes.

Savinho will join Tottenham having made 27 appearances for Manchester City in 2025/26, contributing 3 Premier League goals and 6 assists in what was a stop-start campaign hampered by squad rotation rather than any loss of form. Under De Zerbi — whose system at Genoa and then Brighton was built on exactly the kind of direct, technical wide play that defines Savinho's game — the expectation is that the 22-year-old will find the consistency that occasionally eluded him at the Etihad.

City's Era Ends With Savinho

For Manchester City supporters, Savinho's departure marks another chapter turning. Signed as a statement of intent, he delivered moments of unquestionable quality — a thunderous dribble here, a goal against the run of play there — without ever fully delivering on the promise that a €70 million fee implied. In the context of a summer that has already seen Bernardo Silva, John Stones, and Pep Guardiola depart, and with Enzo Maresca's imprint beginning to shape the new squad, Savinho leaving completes a significant generational shift.

The formal announcement, Romano suggests, is very close. The only thing standing between Savinho and a Tottenham debut is fifteen days of calendar.
