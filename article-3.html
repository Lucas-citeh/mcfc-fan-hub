Maresca Era Begins: City Draw 1-1 With Inter in Hong Kong, Lose on Penalties

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 2 August 2026

Enzo Maresca's reign as Manchester City head coach began on Saturday afternoon at Kai Tak Sports Park in Hong Kong — and it ended with a penalty shootout defeat to Inter Milan, though not before City showed enough in the first 90 minutes to give supporters genuine encouragement heading into the new season. The final score was 1-1 after normal time, with Inter claiming the Asahi Super Dry Trophy via the spot-kicks in front of 42,286 fans.

Mubama Opens the Scoring

City took the lead in the 14th minute through a well-worked move that immediately showcased one of Maresca's key tactical ideas. Antoine Semenyo — arguably City's most dynamic performer across the 90 minutes — surged down the left flank and drove into the Inter half before cutting the ball back precisely into the path of Divin Mubama, who converted from close range. The goal set the tone for a bright opening period from City, with Semenyo's energy and directness causing Inter's backline persistent problems.

Mubama, a powerful young centre-forward, led City's line with confidence throughout and gave Inter's defence a different kind of problem to the ones they had faced in pre-season so far. His performance in the first half was among the positive takeaways from Maresca's opening game.

Pavard Levels for Inter

Inter responded quickly. Benjamin Pavard, the French international and former Bayern Munich centre-back, pulled the score level in the 20th minute, following up well after Federico Dimarco's drive had tested Gianluigi Donnarumma. The Italian goalkeeper — making his first appearance for City in a competitive fixture under Maresca — was beaten by the follow-up and the game was level.

From that point, the match became a more even, physical battle through midfield. Tijjani Reijnders and Mateo Kovacic were composed in the engine room, while Phil Foden and Savinho added craft on either side. But further goals proved elusive in what was, after all, a first pre-season outing — the players still finding their rhythm under the new manager's system.

Penalty Shootout — Inter Win

After 90 minutes of 1-1, the match was decided by a penalty shootout. Inter goalkeeper Ivan Provedel was the standout performer in the spot-kicks, making a key save to swing the trophy towards the Nerazzurri. Inter won the shootout and claimed the Asahi Super Dry Trophy — their reward for a strong penalty performance rather than any failing on City's part in the 90 minutes.

Maresca took the defeat in stride, as would be expected from a manager who has always placed greater emphasis on what happens from the Community Shield onwards. Pre-season shootout losses do not define eras.

Maresca's System: First Impressions

The most notable element of City's performance was the shape Maresca deployed: a compact 4-2-3-1, with Kovacic and Reijnders shielding the defence and a fluid front four rotating around Mubama at the tip. It is a familiar system for students of Maresca's Chelsea work — the same double pivot, similar half-space runs from the wider attackers — and it already looked cohesive in patches.

Semenyo was the pick of the attacking players. His 14th-minute assist summed up what he brings: pace, decision-making on the ball, and the ability to create from wide areas. He won three separate individual duels in the first half and repeatedly drew fouls when Inter pressed high. The Ghana international looks well-suited to Maresca's demands for intensity and directness on the counter.

Donnarumma was steady, commanding his area well and making one important stop before the Pavard equaliser. Rico Lewis — who started at right-back — showed his usual press-resistance and tidy passing, while Josko Gvardiol and Abdukodir Khusanov gave City a physical presence at the back. Khusanov, in particular, won aerial duels convincingly when Inter looked to go long.

Context: What Comes Next

Saturday's game was the first of three Asia Tour fixtures for City. After returning from Hong Kong, the squad flies to Seoul for two fixtures in the Coupang Play Series: K League All-Stars on Wednesday 5 August at Seoul World Cup Stadium, followed by Atlético Madrid on Sunday 9 August — both at 13:00 BST. City then return to the UK ahead of the FA Community Shield against Arsenal on Sunday 16 August at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff.

The squad with World Cup commitments — including Erling Haaland (who sat out the pre-season opener), Elliot Anderson, Marc Guehi and Nico O'Reilly — are expected to join on a staggered basis ahead of Cardiff. The Community Shield against Premier League champions Arsenal, in the opening competitive fixture of the Maresca era, is now less than two weeks away.

On the evidence of 90 minutes in Hong Kong, City are a work in progress — as every team is at this point in pre-season — but the foundations of something structured and direct are already visible. The Maresca era has begun.
