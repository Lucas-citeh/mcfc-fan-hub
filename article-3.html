City Land Bouaddi in £86m Deal — Morocco Star Puts Pen to Paper

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 25 August 2026

The wait is over. Manchester City have confirmed the signing of Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille in a deal worth up to €100 million (approximately £86 million), making the 18-year-old Moroccan midfielder the most expensive teenage signing in Premier League history. Bouaddi put pen to paper on a six-year contract running until June 2031, and will wear a squad number to be confirmed.

Record-Breaking Deal

The transfer, confirmed by Fabrizio Romano with his trademark "Here We Go" on 23 August 2026, ends a protracted summer saga that began in late July. The fee structure is reported as approximately €95 million guaranteed with up to €5 million in performance-related add-ons — a package that surpasses the £59 million Manchester United paid Lille for Leny Yoro in 2024 as the highest fee ever committed to a teenage player in Premier League history.

Manchester City confirmed the deal on mancity.com, with the club's Inside City series ('Episode 524: New signing and new deals') marking the announcement. Bouaddi travelled to Manchester to complete his medical and sign his contract, with the formalities finalised ahead of the Premier League deadline of 1 September 2026.

The Player

Born in France to Moroccan parents, Ayyoub Bouaddi chose to represent Morocco internationally, and it was on the world stage at the 2026 FIFA World Cup — hosted across the United States, Canada and Mexico — where he announced himself to European football's elite. At just 18, Bouaddi played a key role in Morocco's World Cup campaign, his composure and creativity in midfield drawing attention from virtually every top club on the continent.

At Lille, the midfielder made 46 Ligue 1 appearances over two seasons and was widely regarded as the standout player in French football's next generation. His ability to receive under pressure, progress the ball through lines, and arrive late into goalscoring positions drew inevitable comparisons to the player he has effectively been signed to succeed at the Etihad: Rodri, who departed for FC Barcelona earlier this month in a €76.5 million deal.

Filling the Rodri-Shaped Hole

The departure of the Ballon d'Or winner and 2026 World Cup Golden Ball recipient created the most pressing vacancy in Manchester City's squad this summer. Elliot Anderson, the club-record £116 million signing from Nottingham Forest, provides box-to-box energy, but Bouaddi represents a different profile — a deep-lying creator capable of dictating tempo, the role Rodri owned so completely during his seven years at the Etihad.

Enzo Maresca, whose first Premier League game as City manager produced a dramatic 2-1 comeback win over Bournemouth on Sunday, now has the midfield depth he has been working without for the opening weeks of the season. Mateo Kovacic, Elliot Anderson and Nico O'Reilly have covered the ground so far; Bouaddi's arrival gives Maresca a genuine succession plan for the most important position on the pitch.

What Comes Next

Bouaddi is ineligible for Friday's trip to Crystal Palace at Selhurst Park — registration deadlines mean he will not be available until Matchday 3 at the earliest. City's next home league game, against newly promoted Coventry City on 5 September, may be the occasion when the Etihad crowd sees its new signing in a competitive shirt for the first time.

The transfer window closes on 1 September. Malo Gusto remains a target at right-back — City have made fresh contact with Chelsea, who are asking approximately €87 million — and the Nico Gonzalez situation has accelerated rapidly, with Fabrizio Romano issuing his "Here We Go" for the Spanish midfielder to join Newcastle United in a deal worth approximately £50 million. Maresca's squad is taking shape, and the piece the fans most wanted — a midfield architect — is now in place.
