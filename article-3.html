Real Madrid Place Concrete Offer on Table for Gvardiol as City Fight Back with 2030 Contract Proposal

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 11 June 2026

Sky Sports transfer expert Florian Plettenberg has reported that Real Madrid have placed a concrete offer on the table for Manchester City and Croatia centre-back Josko Gvardiol. Manchester City have responded with a contract extension proposal running to 2030 — setting the stage for one of this summer's most consequential defensive transfer decisions.

Real Madrid Make Their Move

Plettenberg's report, published on 10 June, represents the clearest indication yet that Real Madrid are mounting a serious bid for the Croatian. Earlier speculation in May had placed Barcelona and Bayern Munich alongside Real Madrid as interested parties; the picture has now sharpened considerably. A formal, concrete offer from the Bernabeu signals that this is no longer exploratory — Real Madrid have committed to a pursuit and are prepared to meet the financial demands that would be required to prize Gvardiol away from a club that does not want to sell.

Real Madrid have long been admirers of Gvardiol's profile: a left-footed centre-back comfortable at left-back or as a ball-playing centre-half, composed under pressure, aggressive in duels, and progressive in possession. Those attributes are rare in the European market and command significant fees. CaughtOffside had previously placed a valuation of approximately €75 million on the defender — a fee that, if accurate, would represent one of the most expensive defensive acquisitions of the summer window.

Bayern Munich: Out of the Race

An important subplot in the saga has been resolved. Bayern Munich, who had been among the clubs with declared interest in Gvardiol, are understood to be out of the running. Reports from CityXtra and Yahoo Sports indicate the situation has crystallised into a straight choice: sign a new contract with Manchester City or move to Real Madrid. Bayern's exit simplifies the landscape considerably, removing one source of leverage — and potentially making City's task of persuading Gvardiol to stay somewhat easier, given that the alternative is now specifically Real Madrid rather than a broad auction.

Barcelona retain some background interest but are not understood to be at an advanced stage of negotiation. The race, in its current form, is two-horse.

City's Counter: A Deal to 2030

Manchester City are not passive observers. Hugo Viana, who took up the sporting director role this summer, identified extending Gvardiol's contract as one of his most urgent priorities. The club have now tabled a concrete response to Real Madrid's approach: a contract extension to at least 2030, with a club option believed to be part of the structure, according to GoonerDaily. The message from the Etihad is unambiguous — Gvardiol is not for sale, and City are prepared to match his market value in terms of wages to keep him.

Gvardiol's current deal runs until summer 2028, giving City a meaningful contractual cushion. There is no fire-sale scenario here, no risk of losing him for free in twelve months. That security strengthens Viana's hand: City can negotiate from a position of stability rather than urgency, and let Gvardiol weigh both options without the pressure of a ticking contract clock.

City's Footballing Argument

In footballing terms, the case for Gvardiol remaining at the Etihad is substantial. Under Enzo Maresca — a coach whose system at Leicester and Chelsea was built on progressive, ball-dominant defensive structures — a left-sided centre-back who can carry the ball and contribute to build-up play is not merely useful but essential. Gvardiol fits that template almost perfectly, and his role under the new regime is likely to be more consistent and central than his rotation-affected usage under Guardiola in certain periods.

The squad around him is also one worth being part of. The combination of Haaland, Foden, Cherki, Doku, Semenyo and the incoming Elliot Anderson represents a side being rebuilt for the long term. For a player at a formative stage of his career, being a cornerstone of that project — rather than an addition to an already-assembled one — carries its own appeal.

The Madrid Proposition

Real Madrid's pitch is different in kind rather than degree. The Santiago Bernabeu, freshly renovated and among the most spectacular stadiums in the world, offers the kind of stage that motivates specific types of players — those for whom the institution itself is the draw, not merely the football project. Real Madrid's status in Champions League football, their commercial weight, and the prestige attached to the shirt are variables that no counter-offer from another club can entirely neutralise.

If the rumoured appointment of a new manager at the Bernabeu materialises over the coming weeks, Gvardiol would also be joining a squad in a period of renewal — giving him the opportunity to be a senior defensive voice in a rebuilt side rather than one figure among many.

The World Cup Factor

The timing of any resolution is complicated by the 2026 World Cup, which began today. Gvardiol is expected to feature prominently for Croatia, a side capable of progressing deep into the tournament. Any public declaration about his club future is unlikely to come until Croatia's campaign concludes. Real Madrid and Manchester City have both navigated summer transfer sagas alongside major international tournaments before; patience from both clubs is expected, and the practical resolution — whichever way Gvardiol decides — is likely to come in late June or July.

What Comes Next

The Premier League transfer window opens on 15 June. Whether either club makes a formal public move at that point will depend on how quickly Gvardiol's own preference crystallises. City's publicly expressed confidence — consistently reported by CityXtra, and reinforced by Viana's stated commitment to making the renewal a priority — suggests the club genuinely believe they can keep him. Real Madrid's decision to table a concrete offer suggests they are not so certain of that outcome.

This is the kind of saga that defines a transfer summer. A resolution, when it comes, will say something significant about the direction of both clubs — and about Gvardiol's own ambitions at a stage of his career when every decision carries long-term weight.
