Romano 'Here We Go' on Trafford as Bouaddi Talks Accelerate and Real Madrid Make First Approach for Rodri

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 30 July 2026

Manchester City's summer transfer window is moving at pace on three fronts simultaneously. Fabrizio Romano has issued his "here we go" on the James Trafford–Leeds United deal, signalling the agreement is complete. Separately, Romano reports that talks over Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille are "accelerating" with City "confident of wrapping up the signing in the next days." And Real Madrid have now made their first formal approach to Manchester City for Rodri, per Managing Madrid.

Trafford to Leeds: Romano Says 'Here We Go'

The long-running Trafford saga appears finally to be at an end. Fabrizio Romano, whose "here we go" phrase has become the industry standard for a confirmed deal, issued the confirmation that Leeds United and Manchester City have reached a club-record agreement for goalkeeper James Trafford. The fee stands at £40 million initial with add-ons and a sell-on clause — making it the largest spend in Leeds United's history and one of the highest fees ever paid for a British goalkeeper.

Trafford, 23, had made clear his desire to move on following an unsuccessful loan spell at Burnley two seasons ago and then a limited role behind Gianluigi Donnarumma during City's FA Cup-winning 2025/26 campaign. He appeared briefly in the FA Cup Final — famously starting at Wembley in Donnarumma's absence — and had been earmarked by City as a future starter, though Donnarumma's commanding performances have made that path difficult. Leeds, who won promotion to the Premier League, view Trafford as the centrepiece of their top-flight rebuild.

Signatures are expected to be completed shortly, with Trafford due to undergo a medical. Manchester City have not yet made an official announcement via mancity.com. Per Fabrizio Romano ("here we go" — approx. 28 July 2026); Yorkshire Evening Post (transfer on verge of completion — 30 July 2026); TeamTalk (on verge of completion — 30 July 2026).

Bouaddi: Negotiations Accelerating, City Confident

The Ayyoub Bouaddi saga is also edging closer to a resolution. Romano updated on 29–30 July that negotiations between Manchester City and Lille are "accelerating" and that City are "confident of wrapping up the signing in the next days." The personal agreement between the 18-year-old Moroccan international and City has been sealed for some time — a five-year contract running to June 2031 — with the outstanding issue being the gap between the clubs' valuations. Lille are demanding approximately €100 million; City have been offering in the region of €90 million plus add-ons.

City have informed Bouaddi directly that they plan to integrate him into Enzo Maresca's first-team squad immediately rather than sending him back to Lille on loan — a significant signal of intent. At 18, Bouaddi is already a full Moroccan international and has been described as one of the most technically refined young midfielders in European football. His style — press-resistant, mobile, creative in tight spaces — would give City a very different profile to the more physical Elliot Anderson already signed from Nottingham Forest.

David Ornstein of The Athletic had described the medical as "imminent" as far back as 25 July, and TEAMtalk reported "minor details remaining" on 27 July. The pace of reporting has not slowed. There is no official mancity.com announcement as of 30 July 2026. Arsenal remain in the picture as competition, though Romano and multiple sources place City firmly ahead. Per Fabrizio Romano (negotiations accelerating — 29–30 July 2026); David Ornstein / The Athletic (medical imminent — 25 July 2026); beIN Sports (City close signing — 29 July 2026).

Real Madrid Make First Formal Approach for Rodri

The most strategically significant development on 30 July comes from Managing Madrid: Real Madrid have made their first formal approach to Manchester City for Rodri. This marks an escalation from the previous days' reports, which had focused on Rodri giving Madrid the green light (Yahoo Sports; OneFootball) and City "opening the door" (beIN Sports, 28 July), but without a concrete approach having been made club-to-club.

The formal approach now puts City in the position of having to respond with a number. Their position has consistently been that they want a fee in the region of €80 million or above — with some reports citing City holding out for as much as €100 million — while Real Madrid are believed to be willing to spend approximately €50–60 million. That gap, currently estimated at €20–30 million, is the central obstacle.

The context matters: Rodri is in the final year of his contract at the Etihad, underwent back surgery on 28 July (recovery estimated at 4–6 weeks), and has privately communicated his desire to return to Spain. Florentino Pérez is said to have given the green light for Madrid to proceed with negotiations. Whether a compromise fee can be found — or whether City choose to hold Rodri to his final year — is the pivotal question of City's summer.

The timing is also connected to the Bouaddi deal. Multiple reports suggest that City completing the Bouaddi signing may, paradoxically, accelerate Rodri's departure — having secured a midfield replacement, City would have less reason to resist Madrid's approach. Per Managing Madrid (Real Madrid first formal approach made — 30 July 2026); beIN Sports (City open the door — 28 July 2026); Yahoo Sports (Rodri gives green light — July 2026); Managing Madrid (City want €80m — July 2026).

City's Window: The Bigger Picture

In summary, Manchester City now appear to be in active negotiations on at least three simultaneous outgoings and incomings of significant scale: Trafford out (done or near-done), Bouaddi in (imminent), Rodri out (first approach received). The Malo Gusto situation — a right-back wanted by Enzo Maresca at a fee Chelsea have so far refused to match — remains a parallel strand.

City fly to Hong Kong tomorrow ahead of Saturday's pre-season friendly against Inter Milan at Kai Tak Stadium — Maresca's first game in charge of the club. The transfer picture may clarify further before the squad travels, though that remains to be seen. For now, City fans follow a window as busy as any in the club's history.
