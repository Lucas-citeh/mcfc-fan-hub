Domestic Double Sealed: Bernardo Lifts the Cup in His Wembley Farewell

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 16 May 2026

Manchester City are the 2025/26 FA Cup winners. Antoine Semenyo's 72nd-minute backheel sealed a 1-0 victory over Chelsea at Wembley, completing a domestic cup double — and handing Bernardo Silva the most fitting of farewell moments as captain.

The win adds the FA Cup to the EFL Cup City claimed earlier in the season, making Pep Guardiola's side only the eighth club in English football history to hold both domestic cups simultaneously. For a squad that has navigated a relentless campaign across multiple fronts, Saturday's result underlines that this group still has the hunger and the quality to win things.

The Captain's Last Dance

Few images from the 2025/26 season will endure longer than Bernardo Silva raising the FA Cup at Wembley in his final cup final as a Manchester City player. The Portuguese midfielder, who confirmed his summer departure earlier this week after nine seasons and 451 appearances, was named captain for the occasion by Guardiola — a gesture that felt as much a tribute as a tactical call.

Bernardo's influence on the game was subtle but important. It was his combination with Haaland inside the Chelsea half that helped unpick the Blues' defence in the build-up to Semenyo's winner. The arc of his City career — from a relative unknown arriving from Monaco in 2017 to a serial trophy winner lifting silverware at the national stadium — was complete by the time the final whistle sounded.

John Stones, also confirmed as leaving at the end of the season, was an unused substitute. He will get his send-off in the coming weeks, but it was Bernardo's night.

Rodri's Return and the Tactical Shifts That Decided It

Guardiola made five changes from the dominant 3-0 win over Crystal Palace three days earlier, rotating heavily ahead of two crucial league fixtures. The most significant selection news was the return of Rodri to the starting XI — the Ballon d'Or winner had missed the previous four matches with a groin complaint — alongside a straight swap at goalkeeper with James Trafford replacing Gianluigi Donnarumma.

The opening 45 minutes were tight. Chelsea, under interim head coach Calum McFarlane, pressed with discipline and the first half ended goalless. Guardiola responded at the break by withdrawing Omar Marmoush and introducing Rayan Cherki — a switch that shifted City's attacking threat and gave them greater fluidity in and around Chelsea's defensive block.

With Rodri — who had played the full first half on his comeback — showing signs of fatigue, Mateo Kovacic replaced him in the 64th minute. Kovacic steadied City's possession game through midfield, and eight minutes later the decisive moment arrived.

Twenty-One and Counting

Erling Haaland combined with Bernardo Silva on the right before threading the ball into Semenyo's feet. With his back to goal, the Ghanaian forward improvised — spinning and rolling a backheel finish past Robert Sanchez and into the far corner. It was Semenyo's 21st goal of the season across all competitions, and his most important by far.

The goal capped a remarkable half-season at City for Semenyo, who joined from Bournemouth in January and has since proved himself one of the shrewdest pieces of business the club has done in years. In a campaign that has seen highs and lows, his arrival provided a spark that the club needed at exactly the right time.

What Comes Next

The domestic double is secured, but the Premier League title remains the final, elusive prize. City travel to Bournemouth on Tuesday 19 May knowing they must win both their remaining fixtures — and that Arsenal must slip up — to leapfrog the Gunners at the top. The gap at kick-off was two points, with goal difference marginally in City's favour.

Guardiola will need to manage his squad carefully. Rodri was withdrawn before the hour at Wembley, and the schedule — Bournemouth on Tuesday, Aston Villa at home on Sunday 24 May — is demanding. But if Saturday proved anything, it is that this squad knows how to deliver when it matters. The title is not in City's hands alone, but they have done everything they can to keep the dream alive.
