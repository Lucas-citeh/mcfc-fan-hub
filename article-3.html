Norway 1-2 England AET: Haaland Eliminated, Anderson Marches On to World Cup Semi-Final

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 12 July 2026

Erling Haaland's 2026 World Cup ended in heartbreak as England beat Norway 2-1 after extra time in the quarter-final at Hard Rock Stadium, Miami on Saturday night. Jude Bellingham scored twice — equalising on the stroke of half-time before winning it three minutes into extra time — to send England into the semi-finals for the fourth time in their history. Haaland, who had seven goals going into the match, was kept goalless as Norway were eliminated.

A Night of Contrasting Emotions for City Fans

For supporters of Manchester City, this match carried a unique duality. Erling Haaland — their talisman and goal machine — was hoping to drive Norway to only their second ever World Cup semi-final. On the other side, Elliot Anderson, City's club-record £116m summer signing, was in England's squad under Thomas Tuchel, looking to help the Three Lions reach the last four.

It was England — and by extension Anderson — who prevailed, leaving Haaland to return to pre-season preparations earlier than expected while Anderson continues his international campaign. City's new head coach Enzo Maresca will now have to wait a little longer before he has his most expensive purchase available for pre-season training, with England's semi-final pencilled in for Wednesday 15 July.

How the Match Unfolded

Norway took the lead through Andreas Schjelderup, whose cross-shot from the left-hand side flew in off the post past Jordan Pickford. Norway looked well-organised and threatening throughout the first half, with Haaland involved in build-up play but unable to find the decisive touch.

England's response came through Bellingham — a composed finish on the stroke of half-time that restored parity and shifted the momentum going into the interval. The second half remained tight, with neither side able to break the deadlock, and the match went to extra time.

In the third minute of the additional period, Luke Rogers attempted a long-range shot that Norway goalkeeper Ørjan Nyland fumbled, and Bellingham pounced on the rebound to prod home from close range. Norway pressed desperately for an equaliser but England held on, with Haaland unable to conjure the moment that would have saved them.

Haaland's Tournament in Numbers

The Norway and Manchester City striker ends the 2026 World Cup with 7 goals from 5 appearances — a stunning tally that kept him in contention for the Golden Boot throughout the knockout rounds. His highlights included a brace that knocked out Brazil 2-1 in the last 16. But the quarter-final against England will be the defining memory: a night when arguably the most clinical finisher in world football couldn't find the net when Norway needed him most.

Haaland's World Cup exit means he will return to Manchester City's pre-season preparation ahead of the Asia tour in August. He will face Inter Milan on 1 August in Hong Kong, the K League All-Stars on 5 August in Seoul, and Atlético Madrid on 9 August — then the Community Shield against Arsenal on 16 August in Cardiff. City fans will be glad to have him back in sky blue sooner than expected.

Anderson Keeps Going

For City's new midfield signing Elliot Anderson, the journey continues. The 23-year-old, who became the most expensive British player ever when City agreed a £116m deal with Nottingham Forest earlier this summer, now faces an England semi-final. Should England reach — and win — the final, Anderson would not return to City's pre-season until after 19 July, cutting his preparation time before the Asia tour short.

Enzo Maresca has publicly acknowledged the situation, with City planning their pre-season schedule around the availability of World Cup players. Anderson's integration into the squad — physically and tactically — will be accelerated once he returns, given how central he is expected to be to Maresca's midfield blueprint for 2026/27.
