Savinho Stays: City Sign Brazilian to New Deal Until 2031 as Tottenham Transfer Falls Through

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 14 June 2026

Manchester City have blocked Tottenham Hotspur's summer pursuit of Savinho and secured the Brazilian winger's future at the Etihad with a new contract running to June 2031. Fabrizio Romano confirmed the deal as official, reporting that the 21-year-old is staying "with a higher salary after City turned down all Tottenham proposals." Manchester City have also confirmed the signing via their official channels.

City Reject Two Spurs Bids

Tottenham Hotspur had made Savinho one of their top summer targets, with multiple credible sources describing the deal as "on the verge of completion" as recently as 9 June. CaughtOffside insider Paul O'Keefe reported a basic fee of approximately £60 million agreed, with Savinho said to have reached an agreement in principle on personal terms — a contract to 2031 worth £120–140k per week. Fabrizio Romano had also signalled Spurs were "set to announce" the move once the transfer window opened on 15 June.

Manchester City, however, were unwilling to sell. According to Romano, the club turned down two separate formal bids from Tottenham. Sporting director Hugo Viana, installed this summer to oversee the Etihad rebuild under Enzo Maresca, identified Savinho as non-negotiable — an integral part of the squad rather than a tradeable asset at the figures Spurs were prepared to offer.

The Official Confirmation

Fabrizio Romano confirmed the contract extension as official: Savinho has signed a new deal at Manchester City valid until June 2031, with an improved salary. Romano's "Here We Go" confirmation — the journalist's shorthand for a fully completed transfer or contractual agreement — was backed by an official announcement from Manchester City.

The deal represents a new six-year commitment from the winger and resolves one of the most extended uncertainty sagas of a complicated summer. With Bernardo Silva and John Stones departing as free agents, the Anderson pursuit still unresolved, and Gvardiol's future the subject of Real Madrid interest, retaining Savinho removes a significant variable from Maresca's equation going into pre-season.

Why City Would Not Sell

At 21, Savinho is precisely the profile Maresca values: direct, technically gifted, capable of creating and scoring from wide positions. His six assists and five goals across all competitions in 2025/26 — spread across 27 appearances — understate a contribution that frequently extended beyond statistics. His ability to run at defenders in tight spaces and create wider openings for Haaland, Marmoush, and Doku made him central to City's attacking transitions in the second half of the season.

Under Maresca's system — which at Leicester and Chelsea demanded wingers who could press high, recover quickly, and carry the ball at pace into attacking zones — Savinho fits the template almost perfectly. Selling him to a Premier League rival, even for the reported £60 million, would have meant replacing a proven, ascending talent at exactly the moment his relationship with the new head coach was beginning. Viana and Maresca concluded, correctly, that no realistic replacement exists for that fee.

There is also the matter of the 2026 World Cup. Savinho is with Brazil's squad for the tournament. A move completed on the back of strong international performances during the World Cup would have come at a premium; City pre-empted that scenario by tying him down before it could arise.

The Blow for Tottenham and De Zerbi

For Roberto De Zerbi, City's decision to block the transfer represents a significant setback in his first summer as Tottenham head coach. De Zerbi had identified Savinho as a priority wide signing — a player whose directness and technical quality suits his possession-heavy, high-press system. With Savinho now committed to the Etihad, Spurs must redirect their attention elsewhere for a first-choice right-wing option.

The outcome is a reminder that Premier League-to-Premier League transfers remain difficult to execute when the selling club do not wish to sell. Tottenham's reported fee of approximately £60 million may have been reasonable in market terms, but Manchester City's refusal to engage at any price means the negotiation never had an opportunity to progress beyond initial bids.

Savinho's Future Under Maresca

The new contract through 2031 gives both club and player the platform to build a long-term working relationship under the new management era. Savinho enters pre-season as a confirmed starter, an FA Cup winner (Semenyo's 72nd-minute goal against Chelsea at Wembley in May sealed the domestic double), and one of the younger anchors of a squad in genuine transition.

Maresca's systems have historically generated more output from wide forwards than Guardiola's later tactical setups — his Leicester and Chelsea wingers combined directness with higher shot volumes and more frequent dribbling sequences than City's 2023–25 units. With a full pre-season and a defined role in the starting eleven, an improvement on Savinho's 2025/26 numbers is a realistic expectation going into 2026/27.

A Statement of Intent

City's decision to block the transfer and offer improved terms sends a clear message about the direction under Viana and Maresca: this squad is being built, not wound down. Despite the departures of Guardiola, Bernardo Silva, and Stones — and the ongoing Gvardiol uncertainty — the Etihad hierarchy are actively retaining talent the new coaching staff regard as essential. Savinho staying is not a passive outcome; it is a deliberate act of construction for the era ahead.
