Norway vs England QF Preview: Haaland's 7-Goal Blitz Sets Up City's Biggest World Cup Showdown

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 8 July 2026

Manchester City fans face an almost impossible situation on Saturday 11 July: their club's two biggest stars — Erling Haaland and Elliot Anderson — face each other in the World Cup quarter-final at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami. Kick-off is 10pm BST (5pm ET). Haaland arrives with seven goals in four games, joint-leading the Golden Boot with Mbappé and Messi. One of them goes home on Saturday night; the other could yet play three more games.

Haaland's Tournament in Numbers

It is already one of the great individual World Cup performances. Seven goals in four games puts Erling Haaland level with the finest strikers on the planet at this tournament — tied with Kylian Mbappé (France) and Lionel Messi (Argentina) at the top of the scoring charts. For context: Norway had scored five World Cup goals in their entire history before this tournament began. Haaland has almost doubled that tally on his own in three weeks.

His route to the quarter-final: two assists in Norway's Group I opener (a win over South Korea), a hat-trick against Ecuador to seal top-group-two qualification as runners-up behind France, a late winner against Côte d'Ivoire in the Round of 32, then his stunning brace against Brazil — headers, clinical finishes, the lot. Every time Norway needed him, he delivered.

Gary Neville on Sky Sports summed up the challenge facing England: "Haaland at this World Cup has been absolutely unplayable. They [England] have to find a way to limit his touches because when he gets the ball in the box, he scores." England are favourites, Neville concedes — but only if they keep Haaland quiet.

Norway's Path Here — A Historic Run

Norway have never previously reached a World Cup quarter-final. Their group stage was uneven but Haaland-powered: Group I runners-up behind France, they arrived in the knockouts without the expectation that dogs the traditional heavyweights. That underdog status evaporated the moment they beat Brazil 2-1 at MetLife Stadium on 5 July.

For all that this is Norway's collective achievement, the honest assessment is that this run is being carried by one man. Haaland's two late goals against Brazil — 79th minute and 90th-plus — were the difference. Without them, Norway go home. The question England's defence must answer is whether they can do what Brazil, Ecuador, South Korea and Côte d'Ivoire could not: keep him off the scoresheet.

England's Route — Battling Through

Thomas Tuchel's England side have been less spectacular but equally effective. Jude Bellingham's form has been outstanding. Their Round of 16 win over Mexico at the Azteca — 3-2 with 10 men for the final 36 minutes after Jarrell Quansah's red card — showed real character, particularly Harry Kane's composed penalty in the 60th minute to restore the two-goal lead when the ground was shaking.

Elliot Anderson has been part of England's World Cup squad throughout the tournament. The City record signing has not been among Tuchel's main starters — Bellingham and Declan Rice have anchored the midfield — but his presence in the group gives England depth and he has contributed from the bench. He is on course to return to Manchester later than initially expected, but at least he arrives with World Cup knockout experience on his CV before a ball is kicked at the Etihad.

What Saturday Means for City's Pre-Season

For Enzo Maresca, the quarter-final has significant practical implications. Both Haaland and Anderson are guaranteed to be unavailable until at least 12-13 July. Standard return-to-club protocols after a World Cup knockout — typically two to three weeks of rest and recovery — mean they will miss at least part of City's Asia tour.

City's pre-season opens on Saturday 1 August against Inter Milan at the Kai Tak Stadium in Hong Kong. If both players are eliminated on 11 July and take the customary two-week break, they could be available from around 26 July — in time to join late in the Asia leg. A deeper run for either pushes that back further: a semi-final falls around 14-15 July, the final on 19 July.

Maresca's coaching staff will be watching Hard Rock Stadium very closely. The longer the tournament goes, the later the integrations begin — and with a new manager establishing his tactical identity from scratch, early building blocks matter.

City Fans' Dilemma

The matchday WhatsApp groups will be fractured on Saturday. Haaland is City's talisman, the player who has broken records and won trophies at the Etihad. Anderson is the £116m statement signing who represents the future under Maresca. One of them ends their World Cup on the night. There is no comfortable outcome for a Manchester City fan.

Whatever happens in Miami, both players will be back in Sky Blue before August is out — and alongside each other, rather than against each other. The quarter-final is the exception. The next four or five years at the Etihad are the rule.
