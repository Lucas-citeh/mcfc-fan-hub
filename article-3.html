Guardiola's Farewell: Ten Years End on Sunday as City Face Villa

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 21 May 2026

On Sunday afternoon, Pep Guardiola will walk out of the Etihad Stadium tunnel for the last time as Manchester City manager. Aston Villa are the visitors for the final Premier League fixture of the 2025/26 season — and the final chapter of one of the most remarkable coaching tenures in football history.

Guardiola's departure, reported by David Ornstein of The Athletic, brings to a close a decade in east Manchester that has produced six Premier League titles, the 2022-23 Champions League, seven League Cups, two FA Cups, and a transformation of the club from perennial near-misses to the most dominant force in English football.

A Season That Delivered the Double

This has been a season of transition and, ultimately, triumph. City claimed the EFL Cup in February and capped the campaign with a 1-0 FA Cup Final victory over Chelsea at Wembley on 16 May, courtesy of Antoine Semenyo's 72nd-minute back-heel finish. The domestic double — City's third in the Guardiola era — arrives against the backdrop of a Premier League title just beyond reach. Arsenal, finishing on 82 points, were confirmed champions after City drew 1-1 at Bournemouth. City finish second on 78 points.

It is a record that in any other era would be celebrated unreservedly. In the context of what Guardiola's side has done over ten years, second place stings a little. But the double is real and won, and Sunday represents a chance for the Etihad faithful to send their manager out with the victory he deserves.

The Man Who Changed Everything

When Guardiola arrived from Bayern Munich in the summer of 2016, Manchester City had won the Premier League twice in four years under Roberto Mancini and Manuel Pellegrini. What followed was something entirely different. The 100-point season of 2017-18. Back-to-back-to-back Premier League titles from 2021 to 2023. The treble. The invincible Champions League group stage record. A level of tactical innovation, squad depth and managerial consistency that will take years to fully comprehend.

He departs having managed 428 matches for City, winning 305 of them. The transformation of the club's infrastructure, global profile, and style of play is his legacy as much as the silverware.

Maresca Waits in the Wings

Enzo Maresca has reached an agreement in principle to succeed Guardiola as City manager, as reported by David Ornstein of The Athletic. The Italian, who served as Guardiola's assistant during the 2022-23 treble-winning campaign, has managed Leicester City and Chelsea since then and brings first-hand knowledge of the Etihad's culture and methods.

He will inherit a squad itself in transition. Bernardo Silva and John Stones both leave as free agents this summer. Phil Foden — who signed a new four-year contract this week, running until 2030 — represents continuity and belief in the future. Erling Haaland, the fastest player in Premier League history to reach 100 goals, remains the focal point of what comes next.

Sunday's Send-Off

City kick off against Aston Villa at 16:00 on Sunday 24 May at the Etihad. Whatever the scoreline, it will be the end of an era. Guardiola has asked for the occasion to remain focused on football rather than farewell ceremonies, but the occasion will inevitably carry the weight of everything he has meant to this club.

For supporters who have watched him transform Manchester City from a good side into a generational phenomenon, Sunday is about saying thank you.
