Window Closed: City's Record Summer Ends Without Gusto — Looking Ahead to Coventry

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 1 September 2026

The Premier League transfer window closed at 23:00 BST on Tuesday 1 September 2026. Manchester City's most expensive summer in history is officially over — and the right-back position remains uncovered.

The Right-Back Question Remains Unanswered

Despite weeks of persistent speculation, Chelsea's Malo Gusto did not complete a move to Manchester City before the window closed. The French international had reportedly agreed personal terms and given Enzo Maresca's side the green light to proceed as far back as early July, but the clubs were unable to agree a fee — Chelsea holding firm at around €87m while City's ceiling was understood to be closer to £75m.

The impasse proved insurmountable before Tuesday's deadline. City will now head into the remainder of the 2026/27 Premier League season without a specialist right-back on their books, with Matheus Nunes, Rico Lewis and Nico O'Reilly likely to continue rotating through the role until at least the January transfer window.

What the Summer Delivered

Despite the Gusto miss, the scope of City's summer reconstruction remains extraordinary. Seven confirmed arrivals — including two Premier League records — reshaped the club from the ground up following Pep Guardiola's departure.

Ins: Enzo Maresca (head coach, Chelsea), Elliot Anderson (£116m, Nottingham Forest — Premier League record), Ayyoub Bouaddi (€100m, Lille — most expensive teenage signing in Premier League history), Jeremy Monga (£10m, Leicester City), Gerónimo Rulli (€3.8m, Marseille), Pierce Charles (£10m, Sheffield Wednesday), Mathys Detourbet (€25m, Troyes — immediately loaned to Monaco).

Outs: Rodri (FC Barcelona, €76.5m), Bernardo Silva (Real Madrid, free), Savinho (Tottenham, £85m), Tijjani Reijnders (Al Qadsiah, €61m), Nico González (Newcastle United, £52m), Omar Marmoush (Tottenham, loan with £60m obligation to buy), John Stones (Inter Milan, free), Nathan Aké (Fenerbahce, £7m), James Trafford (Leeds United, £40m), Manuel Akanji (Inter Milan, €15m mandatory purchase).

The net outlay — once loan fees and add-ons are factored — represents one of the single largest squad turnovers in Premier League history. The backbone of a six-time title-winning side has been systematically replaced with a generation built for the Maresca era.

The Early Signs Are Encouraging

On the pitch, City have started the new season with maximum points. Wins over Bournemouth (2-1) and Crystal Palace (4-1) have left Maresca's side at the top of the Premier League after two matchdays, with Erling Haaland and Rayan Cherki both among the goals. The structural concerns about a rebuilt midfield and the right-back gap have, so far, been managed effectively.

What Comes Next

Matchday 3 is now the immediate focus. Newly promoted Coventry City — back in the top flight for the first time in 25 years — visit the Etihad Stadium on Saturday 5 September. The game represents an opportunity to extend City's winning start and build further momentum before the first Manchester derby of the season at Old Trafford on 12 September.

The summer is done. The question of a right-back is parked until January. And City — improbably, after all the upheaval — sit top of the Premier League table.
