Arsenal Crowned Champions as Haaland Rescue Act Falls Short at Bournemouth

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 19 May 2026

Manchester City's Premier League title dream is over. A 1-1 draw at the Vitality Stadium, sealed by Eli Junior Kroupi's 39th-minute curler and rescued only in the fifth minute of stoppage time by Erling Haaland, was not enough — Arsenal are Premier League champions for the first time in 22 years.

Pep Guardiola's side needed to win to take the title race to the final day, but Bournemouth — buoyed by a noisy home crowd and playing with nothing to fear — produced a disciplined, courageous display that their performance more than deserved. By the time Haaland swept home the equaliser, Arsenal's celebrations were already under way.

Kroupi Stuns City

The decisive moment came just before the interval. Adrien Truffert burst forward down the left and slid a cutback into the path of Eli Junior Kroupi, who took a touch and bent a wonderful finish into the top-right corner past a helpless Gianluigi Donnarumma. The Vitality Stadium erupted. At that instant, Arsenal's title was all but secure.

City had created chances before the goal — Antoine Semenyo had an effort correctly ruled out for offside and Haaland had won an early header — but Bournemouth's backline held firm and their goalkeeper Djordje Petrovic was excellent throughout.

Guardiola Throws On the Cavalry

Pep Guardiola acted decisively at half-time, sending out an unchanged team but changing his approach immediately when it became clear City needed goals. On the 56th minute, he made a triple substitution: Phil Foden replaced Mateo Kovacic, Savinho came on for Semenyo, and Rayan Cherki entered for captain Bernardo Silva in what will prove to be one of Bernardo's final appearances in a City shirt.

The changes added dynamism and width, and within a minute of the restart Haaland had surged into the penalty area to tee up Nico O'Reilly — only for Petrovic to produce a brilliant close-range stop. City pressed relentlessly. Marmoush was introduced for Doku on 76 minutes as Guardiola sought every avenue.

Haaland — Too Late

With five minutes of stoppage time added, the moment City had been chasing arrived. Rodri advanced and unleashed a fierce drive that crashed off the post. Haaland, predatory as ever, reacted quickest to sweep the rebound home at the second attempt. 1-1. The Etihad faithful watching on television dared to dream.

But there was no time for a winner. The whistle blew. Arsenal, who had earlier beaten Burnley to move onto 82 points, were confirmed as champions. City, on 78 points, finish second — a remarkable achievement in a season that has delivered the FA Cup and EFL Cup double, but the league remains the one that got away.

End of an Era

For Pep Guardiola, this draw at Bournemouth marks the penultimate match of his extraordinary decade at the Etihad. He departs as the most decorated manager in the club's history, with 15 major trophies — including six Premier League titles. Finishing second in his final Premier League season feels like an unsatisfying footnote, but it should not diminish what he has built.

Captain Bernardo Silva was substituted off in the 56th minute and may have played his last meaningful City minutes before his confirmed summer departure. John Stones watched from the bench. The generation that delivered so much is stepping aside.

City's final game comes on Sunday 24 May against Aston Villa at the Etihad — a chance to sign off in front of their own fans, and to consolidate second place ahead of next season under Enzo Maresca.
