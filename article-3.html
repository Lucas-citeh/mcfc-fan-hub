City Target Malo Gusto as Maresca Reunion Gathers Pace

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 18 July 2026

Manchester City have emerged as frontrunners to sign Chelsea right-back Malo Gusto, with personal terms reportedly agreed and the French international having given City the green light to conclude a deal. The pursuit represents one of the most intriguing angles of Enzo Maresca's first transfer window at the Etihad — a manager working to sign a player he worked closely with at his previous club.

Why Gusto? The Maresca Connection

Malo Gusto spent the 2025/26 season under Enzo Maresca at Chelsea, becoming a key component of the Italian's preferred 4-2-3-1 structure. Maresca valued Gusto's combination of attacking output — particularly his underlapping runs and quality on the overlap — with the defensive solidity to hold a back four together. According to Read Man City, who reported on 3 July that Gusto had agreed personal terms to join City, the player is keen to reunite with Maresca and has explicitly indicated his desire to move to the Etihad.

The Frenchman's arrival at City would address a genuine positional gap in Maresca's plans. In 2025/26, Matheus Nunes — primarily a central midfielder listed in the defensive ranks — deputised at right-back for long stretches. Rico Lewis, the gifted academy graduate, is also capable there but is considered to have his future primarily in midfield. Maresca, whose system at Chelsea relied on conventional back-four positioning, is understood to want a specialist right-back capable of operating in a structured defensive shape and contributing high up the pitch.

The Fee Obstacle

The sticking point is the transfer fee. Chelsea are holding out for £75m for Gusto, whose contract runs until 2028. Manchester City's position has been reported at around £50m — a significant gap, though one that sources suggest both parties believe is bridgeable given the urgency of timeline before the Community Shield on 16 August.

According to Fabrizio Romano, whose reporting was cited in Football365, City received internal clearance to make a fresh approach after the World Cup and Maresca has been personally involved in driving the pursuit. With France now eliminated from the 2026 World Cup — beaten 2-0 by Spain in Atlanta on 14 July — Gusto is free to return to London and turn his full attention to his club future. His return from international duty removes the last logistical barrier to accelerating talks.

City's Right-Back Problem

This need predates Maresca's arrival. In the closing stages of Pep Guardiola's tenure, the right-back role became one of City's most evident structural weaknesses after Kyle Walker's departure in January 2024 was never permanently addressed. Nunes' willingness to operate there was admirable but always viewed as a temporary measure. Gusto would give Maresca a dedicated right-back of Champions League calibre around whom to construct his defensive structure for the long term.

Gusto, 23, has made over 70 appearances for Chelsea since joining from Lyon in summer 2023. Known for his energy, directness going forward and improving positional discipline, he fits the profile of a player City can build around for the next half-decade — still young enough to develop but experienced enough to contribute immediately under a manager who already knows exactly how to use him.

Broader Context: A Summer of Rebuilding

Gusto would join a squad that has already undergone considerable summer change. City have brought in Elliot Anderson (£116m, Nottingham Forest) and Jeremy Monga (£12.5m, Leicester City) while losing Bernardo Silva (Real Madrid, free), John Stones (free agent), Nathan Aké (Fenerbahce, £7m) and Manuel Akanji (Inter Milan, €15m mandatory purchase). A deal for Savinho's departure to Tottenham at around £65m is also expected to be formalised imminently.

Hugo Viana's recruitment team is working across several fronts simultaneously. A specialist right-back, a replacement winger for Savinho, and a possible renewal for Rodri — who is set to undergo minor back surgery after Spain's World Cup final on Sunday but is expected to be fit for the start of the season — are all expected to reach resolution before the competitive season gets under way.
