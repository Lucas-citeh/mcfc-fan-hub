Rodri Contract Alarm: Romano Reveals 'Significant Gap' in Talks as Real Madrid Circle Ballon d'Or Winner

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 5 June 2026

Manchester City face a significant challenge in keeping their most important player. Transfer insider Fabrizio Romano has reported a financial obstacle in the club's contract talks with Rodri — a "significant gap" between what the Ballon d'Or winner is demanding and what City have so far been prepared to offer. Real Madrid are monitoring the situation closely ahead of the 2026 World Cup, adding urgency to a negotiation City cannot afford to lose.

Rodri is entering the final year of his current Etihad Stadium contract, which expires at the end of the 2026/27 season. Manchester City presented a new renewal proposal in April, but Romano's reporting makes clear that the proposal has not come close to bridging the gap between the two parties. The Spain international has indicated that he will not make any decision on his future until after the World Cup — which begins on 11 June — leaving the situation unresolved for weeks at minimum.

Romano: 'Significant Gap' Remains

The phrase "significant gap" from Romano is not a minor caveat. In contract reporting, that language signals a meaningful distance between the player's demands and the club's position — not a matter of final clauses, but of the headline figure itself. City made the offer in April and it has not generated a counter-agreement; the silence from Rodri's camp before the World Cup is telling.

Romano also cited the financial context as the core obstacle. Manchester City are managing a squad restructure across multiple positions — right-back, wide forward, central midfield — in the same window, while operating within the constraints of spending rules that the club has navigated carefully since their 115-charge case concluded. Offering Rodri a contract that matches his status as arguably the best midfielder in world football requires a financial commitment that sits above what City have so far tabled.

Real Madrid Circle Ahead of World Cup

The spectre of Real Madrid adds acute pressure to the situation. Presidential candidates in this year's Real Madrid elections have publicly named Rodri as a target, and the appointment of José Mourinho as the club's incoming manager only amplifies the background noise. Mourinho's preference for dominant, combative midfielders — and his history of recruiting players whose contracts are running down — makes Rodri a natural fit for the project he is building at the Bernabéu.

Real Madrid's ability to offer wages at the top of the market, combined with the appeal of playing alongside Kylian Mbappé and Vinicius Jr., gives them a structural advantage that City cannot fully compete with on pure financial terms alone. What City can offer is history, a proven project, and the continuity of playing with teammates who already understand exactly where Rodri's passes are going. Whether that is enough remains to be seen.

The World Cup Pause

Rodri has been clear that he will not address his future — renewal, exit, or otherwise — until after the 2026 World Cup. Spain are among the leading contenders for the tournament, which spans the United States, Canada and Mexico from 11 June through to mid-July. With Rodri almost certain to play a central role for La Roja, Manchester City must sit with the uncertainty for the duration of the competition.

This creates a scenario in which City are negotiating other priorities — the Elliot Anderson midfield bid, the Savinho exit, right-back recruitment — with a cloud hanging over the position those players are meant to complement. If Rodri decides to run his contract down or join Real Madrid on a free transfer in summer 2027, the midfield rebuild underway this summer becomes a far more extensive project.

What City Stands to Lose

It is difficult to overstate Rodri's importance to Manchester City's recent success. His absence through injury in the first half of 2024/25 was a primary factor in the club's form collapsing; his return in 2025/26 — and the structural authority he provided to Enzo Maresca's system from the outset of the new manager's tenure — was integral to the domestic double. He is the metronome who makes everyone else better: Reijnders runs further, Cherki creates more freely, and the defensive line presses with more confidence when he is shielding it.

Losing Rodri would represent something approaching an irreplaceable blow — not just a talented player departing, but a system-defining one. The summer's transfer activity has been framed as a transition into the Maresca era; a Rodri exit would force a far more fundamental rebuild than anyone at the Etihad is currently planning for.

What Happens Next

The World Cup runs until mid-July. Rodri's decision timeline means City are unlikely to have clarity before late July at the earliest. In the meantime, Romano's update will sharpen attention on the 2026/27 season as a pivotal one: if City cannot close the gap and renew, the window to either keep him or receive a transfer fee from Real Madrid opens in summer 2027, with Madrid fully capable of waiting.

City's task before the window closes in late August is to make a compelling enough case — both in terms of financial package and sporting project — that the Ballon d'Or winner chooses to stay. The gap, Romano says, is significant. But it is not yet declared insurmountable.
