Foden Returns With a Goal — What His Comeback Means for City's November

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 18 October 2026

Phil Foden scored on his first start in over a month as Manchester City beat Ipswich Town 3-2 at the Etihad on Saturday 17 October. It was the perfect tonic for both player and club — City extended their perfect Premier League record to seven wins from seven, and Foden served notice that his best form is still to come.

The Long Wait

Foden's absence stretched back to the 199th Manchester derby on 13 September, when he was sent off by referee Michael Oliver in the 23rd minute for violent conduct — a studs-up kick to United captain Bruno Fernandes. The straight red card, confirmed by VAR, brought a three-match domestic suspension that kept him out of the Carabao Cup tie against Norwich City, the home victory over Sunderland, and the 3-0 demolition of Liverpool at Anfield.

Then, during the international break, City confirmed that Foden had undergone surgery for acute appendicitis — an unwelcome complication that pushed his return date back further. He missed the Champions League home defeat to PSG on 14 October while continuing his recovery.

Back With a Bang

Maresca handed Foden a starting berth against Ipswich Town on Saturday, and the Stockport-born midfielder repaid that faith within 19 minutes, opening the scoring with a confident finish. The goal — his first of the 2026/27 season — was exactly the kind of incisive contribution City had been missing in his absence.

Ipswich fought back to level through Maeda (21') and then pulled it to 3-2 from the penalty spot in the 90th minute, but City had already done the damage — Haaland added his brace (34', 69') to seal the points, and Maresca became the first manager in Premier League history to win his first seven league matches in charge of a club.

Why Foden Matters for November

City's November schedule is the most demanding of the season so far: a Champions League away trip to RB Leipzig (4 Nov), a Premier League visit to Nottingham Forest (7 Nov) — a return for £116m signing Elliot Anderson to his old club — and a trip to the Emirates to face Arsenal (28 Nov), sandwiched around a home UCL date with Napoli (24 Nov) and Fulham at the Etihad (21 Nov).

Having Foden available and match-sharp across that run is significant. He brings the ability to drift between the lines, pick short combinations and carry the ball past opponents in tight areas — qualities that become essential in games at Leipzig and Arsenal, where City will not always have the ball.

There is a caveat: after a lengthy lay-off including surgery, minutes management will be important. Maresca has shown a willingness to rotate freely, and Foden is unlikely to play every match at full tilt immediately. But Saturday's showing was exactly the kind of confidence-builder a recovering player needs, and the hope from a City perspective is that the Ipswich goal signals a full return to form.

The Wider Picture

Saturday's win left City seven points clear at the top of the Premier League — a position that looked almost impossible to imagine in the difficult middle months of 2025/26 when Guardiola's side stumbled to a second-place finish. Under Maresca, City have been relentless: 21 points from seven games, 19 goals scored, just nine conceded.

With Haaland on 12 goals in all competitions, Cherki with five, and now Foden back in the goals column, Maresca has a genuine attacking triumvirate to call on as the season approaches its most challenging phase.
