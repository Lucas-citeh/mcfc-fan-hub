City's £500m Summer In Review — and One Right-Back Still Needed

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 31 August 2026

The Premier League transfer window closes at 23:00 BST on 1 September. With one day to go, Manchester City's summer of radical reconstruction is almost complete — bar one outstanding question mark at right-back.

The Scale of It

No club in Premier League history has remodelled so drastically while starting the season with a perfect record. In the space of three months, Manchester City have said goodbye to the spine of a squad that won six league titles and a Champions League — and replaced it, at enormous cost, with a new generation built for the Enzo Maresca era.

The departures alone represent one of the most significant squad overhauls in English football history. Rodri, the Ballon d'Or winner and 2026 World Cup Golden Ball recipient, departed to FC Barcelona for €76.5m on 18 August. Bernardo Silva joined Real Madrid on a free transfer in June. Tijjani Reijnders was sold to Al Qadsiah for €61m after a single season. Savinho moved to Tottenham for £85m just 27 days after signing a new six-year contract. Nico González joined Newcastle for £52m. Manuel Akanji left for Inter Milan via a mandatory purchase clause. John Stones joined Inter as a free agent. Nathan Aké joined Fenerbahce for £7m. James Trafford was sold to Leeds United for £40m, a British goalkeeping record.

And then there is Omar Marmoush — the Egyptian forward loaned to Tottenham on a deal with a £60m obligation to buy — completing a departure list of extraordinary breadth.

The Arrivals

The reinvestment has been equally bold. Elliot Anderson arrived from Nottingham Forest for £116m, the largest fee in Premier League history. Ayyoub Bouaddi was signed from Lille for €100m, making the 18-year-old Moroccan the most expensive teenage signing in Premier League history. Gianluigi Donnarumma arrived from PSG in the previous summer; Gerónimo Rulli joined from Marseille for €3.8m as his deputy. Pierce Charles (£10m, Sheffield Wednesday) and Mathys Detourbet (€25m, Troyes) add long-term depth, with both spending 2026/27 on loan.

Jeremy Monga, 17, was signed from Leicester City for £10m, while Enzo Maresca himself was appointed on a three-year deal from Chelsea in May — the man tasked with turning this assembled group into a coherent, title-winning team.

So far, on the evidence of the first two Premier League games — wins over Bournemouth (2-1) and Crystal Palace (4-1) — it is working.

The One Outstanding Question: Malo Gusto

The position that remains uncovered is right-back. City have operated with Rico Lewis, Nico O'Reilly and Khusanov in that role at various points, with Marc Guehi occasionally pushed out. The solution they have been pursuing all summer is Chelsea's Malo Gusto — a player who worked directly under Maresca at Stamford Bridge and has reportedly given the green light to a move since early July.

The problem has been the fee. Chelsea are holding firm at €87m; Manchester City are understood to have issued a £75m ultimatum — meet that, or City move on. As of 31 August, no deal has been officially announced by either club or confirmed by Fabrizio Romano. The window closes in approximately 24 hours.

If Gusto does not arrive, City will begin the season without a natural right-back in the traditional sense — a structural gap Maresca will need to address creatively until January at the earliest. If he does arrive, the summer will look, to the eye, complete.

What's Next on the Pitch

Regardless of what happens in the window, City face newly promoted Coventry City at the Etihad on Saturday 5 September — Matchday 3. The Sky Blues, back in the top flight for the first time in 25 years, arrive as significant underdogs. It is the ideal fixture for Maresca to keep momentum, blood new faces and assess the squad ahead of the first Manchester derby at Old Trafford on 12 September.

The summer is nearly done. The season has already started. And for now — improbably, after all the turmoil — Manchester City sit top of the Premier League table.
