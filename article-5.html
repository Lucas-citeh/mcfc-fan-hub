Nine Points, One Destination: City Head to Old Trafford Unbeaten

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 6 September 2026

Manchester City travel to Old Trafford on Saturday 12 September for the first Manchester derby of the 2026/27 season — Matchday 4 of the Premier League — with maximum points and a growing sense that Enzo Maresca's side are hitting their stride faster than anyone expected.

A Perfect Foundation

Nine points from nine. Seven goals scored, two conceded. Top of the Premier League. Twenty-four hours after a controlled 1-0 victory over newly promoted Coventry City — Erling Haaland's header from Antoine Semenyo's right-wing cross enough to seal a third successive win — City head into the international break knowing they have set the kind of early-season standard that title-winning campaigns are built upon.

Each of the three wins has told a different story. The Bournemouth comeback — trailing 1-0 at half-time, rescued by Rayan Cherki's creativity and Josko Gvardiol's cool 90th-minute poke — showed character. The 4-1 demolition of Crystal Palace at Selhurst Park, in which Cherki scored a brace and Haaland struck twice, showed quality. The Coventry win showed discipline. Together they point to a side that is hard to beat in multiple ways.

The Derby: City's Opportunity

The Manchester derby at Old Trafford on Matchday 4 is the first significant measuring stick of Maresca's tenure. A win would put City 12 points from 12 and send an unmistakable statement to every other title contender. A draw or defeat would provide United — who, regardless of their early-season position, retain the capacity to raise their game for this fixture — with a morale boost that money cannot buy.

City's home record at Old Trafford under Guardiola was formidable; Maresca will be keen to establish the same authority from the outset of his era. The Blue half of Manchester arrives with the momentum, the depth, and the confidence that comes from winning every competitive match since the Community Shield in August.

Can Doku Return?

The key injury question surrounds Jeremy Doku. The Belgian winger sustained a calf injury during the Community Shield defeat to Arsenal on 16 August and has missed all three Premier League matches since. Maresca had described the concern as a "two to three week" issue, and with three weeks now having passed since his last appearance, City's medical staff will assess whether he is ready to play a part at Old Trafford.

Doku's directness and one-versus-one quality would add a different dimension to City's attack on a stage that suits his fearless style. Even a place on the bench would be welcome. The decision will rest on how he responds during the international break period, with Maresca expected to provide an update closer to match day.

Maresca's System: Growing Cohesion

Three matches in, the tactical shape is crystallising. Elliot Anderson has established himself as the midfield anchor in a 4-1-4-1, with Enzo Fernández, Iliman Ndiaye, and Rayan Cherki rotating behind Haaland across the front four. Antoine Semenyo operates on the right as a combative, high-tempo presence who presses as much as he creates.

The summer's arrivals are integrating with unusual speed. Fernández and Ndiaye made their competitive debuts in the Coventry win and immediately looked at home — Fernández's sharp one-two play directly involved in the goal, Ndiaye's physicality and directness giving City a new dimension on his flank. Ayyoub Bouaddi continues to build his minutes off the bench, growing in confidence with each cameo.

The Bigger Picture

City go into this derby having spent over £600m in the summer, appointed a new manager for the first time in a decade, and entirely rebuilt their midfield. That all three of those things could simultaneously produce a seamless early-season run is not something even the most optimistic supporter would have guaranteed back in June.

Maresca has quickly established a clear identity — intense, possession-based, with pressing triggers built into the system — and the players are buying in. Old Trafford, in front of a hostile crowd, under the scrutiny of the biggest fixture in English club football, is the true test of whether this is a team that can sustain a title challenge through adversity as well as comfort.

City arrive in the best possible shape to find out.

Derby details: Manchester United vs Manchester City · Old Trafford · Saturday 12 September 2026 · 12:30 BST.
