Gvardiol's Stoppage-Time Winner Gives Maresca a Perfect Premier League Debut

By Blue Moonies Fan Hub 23 August 2026

Manchester City 2-1 AFC Bournemouth. Premier League Matchday 1. Etihad Stadium, 23 August 2026. Enzo Maresca's era as Manchester City head coach could scarcely have started with a more dramatic script. Six minutes from time the Premier League's new arrivals were staring down defeat in their opening fixture of the campaign. What followed — two goals in seven minutes, the second arriving after a VAR reversal that sent the Etihad into delirium — was as good an introduction to the Etihad's new era as supporters could have hoped for.

A Difficult First Hour

Maresca's opening Premier League team selection showed intent: Marc Guehi and Rico Lewis were both handed starts in a back four alongside skipper Ruben Dias and Josko Gvardiol, with Elliot Anderson joining Nico O'Reilly and Lewis in a three-man midfield. Antoine Semenyo, Phil Foden and Erling Haaland led the attack, with Jeremy Doku absent through injury.

For much of the first half it was Bournemouth, under new head coach Marco Rose, who looked sharper and more cohesive. Marcus Tavernier broke the deadlock in the 26th minute, punishing a moment of City uncertainty with a finish that left Gianluigi Donnarumma stranded. City were trailing at half-time and had created little of note in response.

The second half began no more promisingly. Haaland found himself starved of supply, and Bournemouth's back line — organised and compact — continued to frustrate City's attempts to build through midfield. At the hour mark Maresca made a double change: Mateo Kovacic for Anderson and Rayan Cherki for O'Reilly, the Frenchman coming on at the 63rd minute and immediately altering the shape of City's attack.

Cherki Changes Everything

Substitute Rayan Cherki delivered both moments that won the match. With six minutes remaining he swung in a corner that Marc Guehi rose to head powerfully home — the centre-back's first Premier League goal in City colours, and the Etihad's signal that a comeback was on.

The winner arrived in the second minute of added time. Cherki threaded a pass into the Bournemouth penalty area, and Josko Gvardiol bundled it home at close range. The linesman's flag immediately went up for offside. The Etihad fell briefly silent — and then the VAR review began. After a check that felt considerably longer than it was, the referee pointed to the centre circle. Gvardiol had been kept onside by a Bournemouth defender, and City had their winner. The bench erupted, Maresca included.

Jack Grealish — introduced for Foden in the 82nd minute — played a minor role in the closing stages, but the night belonged to Cherki, whose two assists after coming off the bench underlined his potential as a match-changing presence in Maresca's system.

Three Points, More Questions Than Answers

The result flatters City somewhat. For long periods Bournemouth were the better side, and it took a sub's moment of quality and a VAR intervention to rescue a win. Haaland drew a blank on his Premier League season debut. The midfield, without the departed Rodri and Reijnders, still looks unsettled — though Kovacic provided composure in his brief cameo.

But debut wins in difficult circumstances are not nothing. Three points, the right side of a VAR decision, and a first glimpse of Maresca's Manchester City in competitive Premier League action. The Etihad will take it. Crystal Palace away — Matchday 2 — awaits on Saturday 29 August.
